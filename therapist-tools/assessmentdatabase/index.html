How to use this tool▾
  1. Use the filters on the left (category, age group, who completes it, and symptoms) or search by name, code, or keyword. Counts show how many assessments match.
  2. Check the box on any card to select it, then use Print Assessment(s) for a scoring and citation sheet, or copy the APA citations.
  3. Add your own assessments with the form, and save your library as a .json file.
  4. Use Start over to clear your filters and selection.

Why this matters

Standardized measures give you a baseline, show change over time, and support measurement-based care, which improves outcomes. A screening score is not a diagnosis: treat a cutoff as a prompt for a fuller clinical interview, and choose tools validated for your client’s age and population.

Assessment Database

Evidence-based screening tools and outcome measures, sorted by diagnosis, symptoms, and age group. Open shareable forms or go straight to each form’s official source, check scoring cutoffs, and copy APA citations for your notes.

About copyright and these forms▾

Most assessments are copyrighted, even when they’re free to use. Copyright protects the exact wording and layout of a form, so “free for clinicians” usually means you may copy it for your own clients. It doesn’t mean another website may post it for everyone. Some authors say so outright: the RCADS authors, for example, don’t allow it on public websites.

Because of that, this site only hosts a form when its owner clearly allows sharing (public domain, or free to copy with conditions like keeping the copyright line). Everything else has a Get it from the source button that goes to the owner’s official page, where you’ll also get the current version and any scoring or training materials.

Each card lists who owns the form, what the terms allow, and the date the terms were checked. Terms can change, so check the source before using a form in your practice. Scoring notes and citations here are summaries for reference, not copies of the forms. This isn’t legal advice.

Please note: Screening measures don’t make a diagnosis on their own. Interpret scores alongside a full clinical interview.
