    📖 How to Use This Tool▾

    What it does

    It gives you quick check-ins on your energy, schedule, caseload, and burnout risk, because working solo means no one else is watching your battery.

    Step by step

    1. Pick a check-in under Check-ins in the tool menu: right now, personal wellness, daily pacing, caseload, checklists, or burnout.
    2. Answer honestly. The summary cards at the top update as you go.
    3. Hide any check-in or tool you don’t use with its Hide button. Show hidden tools brings them back.
    4. Under Caseload & boundaries, use Import from Caseload at a Glance (.json) to fill in your caseload. If you’ve uploaded practice data, your license, malpractice, and CAQH dates show up under Checklists.
    5. Use Print snapshot if you’d like to bring it to consultation or your own therapy.

    Fills every check-in with a made-up week so you can see how it works.

    Works well with

    • Caseload at a Glance: save your caseload there, then import it here.
    • Practice Info Sheet: your license, malpractice, and CAQH dates appear in the checklists.

    Tips

    • The Right now check-in takes about 30 seconds between sessions.
    • Save a .json each week if you want to compare how you’re doing over time.

    Saving your work

    Nothing leaves your browser. The page clears when you refresh unless you Save .json or turn on Save in this browser in the tool menu. Leave browser saving off on shared computers.

    Why this matters

    Burnout and secondary traumatic stress are common in mental health work, and they affect the quality of care. Regular check-ins, like the burnout reflection here, and small consistent supports protect you more than occasional big resets. Caring for yourself is part of ethical practice under the ACA and APA codes.

    Weekly Schedule and Client-Order Check

    Burnout often comes less from how many clients you see than from what order you see them in, like three high-acuity sessions back-to-back at 4:00 PM. Map your workdays below to check your pacing.

    Day Work status Total clients Heavy sessions Most back-to-back (no break) Your peak energy window Where are your heavy sessions? Pacing Remove
    Scheduling rules of thumb▾
    • Match Acuity to Your Chronotype: Place heavy sessions, like high-acuity, complex trauma, or new 90791 intakes, in your personal peak energy window—never in your final slot of the day if you experience afternoon energy dips.
    • The "Rule of 3" Buffer: Avoid scheduling more than 3 clinical hours in a row without at least a 30-minute movement/meal/admin buffer.
    • Soft-Landing Final Hour: Whenever possible, make your last session of the day a lower-acuity, resource-building, or familiar ongoing client so your nervous system isn't spiked right at 5:00 PM.
