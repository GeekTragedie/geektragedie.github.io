The order to file things in, so you don’t get stuck on government forms▾

Step 1: DOH Clinical License (LMHC / LMHCA) → Step 2: Registered Agent + Secretary of State PLLC Formation ($180–$200) → Step 3: IRS EIN Tax ID (free; check IRS.gov for the online tool’s hours, and do this before your State DOR Business License) → Step 4: NPI Number (Free at NPPES) → Step 5: WA DOR Business License + Trade Name ($5) + City Endorsement → Step 6: Business Bank Account (bring EIN + PLLC Certificate + Operating Agreement) & Malpractice Insurance.

3. Picking Your EHR & Billing Platform (No-Nonsense Comparison for New Clinicians)

Platform Type Examples Monthly Cost Best For... Watch Out For...
All-in-One Standalone EHR SimplePractice, TherapyNotes, Sessions Health, Jane ~$39 to $99+/mo Full control over your own client portal, custom intake forms, telehealth, and direct insurance/superbill billing. (Sessions Health is a favorite budget-friendly option). Subscription tiers can climb quickly if you add integrated telehealth, Wiley treatment planners, or e-prescribing.
Insurance Aggregator + Free EHR Headway, Alma, SonderMind Free (Headway) or ~$125/mo (Alma) Fast credentialing (30–45 days) under their group tax ID, guaranteed biweekly payouts, built-in client portal, scheduling, and free EHR charting. You are credentialed under *their* group contract (not your own independent contract); always export backups of your clinical notes and keep your own Google Workspace / local records!
DIY Minimalist / Local-First Google Workspace (with signed BAA) + IvyPay/Stripe + GeekTragedie Toolkit ~$7 to $14/mo Cash-pay / sliding-scale / associate practices wanting near-zero overhead and 100% local ownership of their `.json` notes, treatment plans, and superbills. Requires discipline to organize your encrypted local/Drive folders and manually track scheduling and reminders.
