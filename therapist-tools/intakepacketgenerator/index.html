Intake Packet Generator

Build your client intake packet once (first-time therapy guide, disclosure statement, informed consent, practice policies, HIPAA notice, and intake questions), then save it as a .json file to reload and update later. It also writes 90791 intake notes.

Please note: These templates are a starting point, not legal advice. Check that your disclosure statement and informed consent meet your licensing board’s current rules (in Washington, WAC 246-809-710) and your malpractice carrier’s requirements.

Select Workspace Mode:

1. Practice & Clinician Details (Upload Once, Reuse Anytime)

Fill these in once (or import your Practice Info Sheet .json) and click Apply Details to Boilerplate Below to auto-personalize your packet.

2. Choose Which Sections to Include in Your Printed PDF Packet

Uncheck any section you don't need right now. Checked sections will appear in your live preview and PDF printout.

Part 1: Welcome & "What to Expect in Your First Therapy Session" (For First-Time Therapy Clients)▾

Demystifies the intake process, explains how the first session differs from ongoing therapy, and reassures clients that they are in control of their pace.

Part 2: Professional Disclosure Statement & Client Rights (WAC 246-809-710)▾

Includes clinician qualifications, therapeutic orientation, client right to refuse treatment or choose a practitioner, and mandatory state DOH complaint contact info.

Part 3: Informed Consent for Psychotherapy & Limits of Confidentiality▾

Covers the risks and benefits of therapy, mandatory reporting exceptions (imminent harm, child/vulnerable adult abuse, court orders), and minor/family policies.

Part 4: Practice Policies, Fees, Cancellations, Communication & AI Note-Taker Consent▾

Outlines session lengths, fees, insurance/superbills, cancellation policy, 24-hour response window, emergency 988/911 boundaries, and optional AI ambient note-taking consent.

Part 5: Telehealth Informed Consent & Safety Protocol▾

Covers physical location verification (must be physically located in clinician's licensed state at session time), privacy, tech failure backup plan, and local emergency contacts.

Part 6: HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices Summary & Good Faith Estimate (No Surprises Act)▾

Summarizes client rights over Protected Health Information (PHI) under HIPAA and their right to a Good Faith Estimate of medical/mental health charges.

Part 7: Client Intake & Background Questions (Open-Ended Prompts — No Diagnostic Scales)▾

Customize the open-ended reflection questions that print with write-in lines for the client (demographics, goals, strengths, sensory/communication needs, and history).
