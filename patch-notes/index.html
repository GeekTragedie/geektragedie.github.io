For Everyone

November 14, 2026

  • Added The ND Self-Retreat Builder in Adulting. It’s a low-demand, zero-decision planner for a neurodivergent self-retreat, built around three daily anchors. It includes prep-week checklists, a daily body-weather tracker, wake-up, activity, craft, and game menus, a sensory lab, and journal prompts.
  • Added The builder has two modes. Creator’s Example is a real, filled-in AuDHD + CPTSD retreat you can learn from or use as-is. Build My Own starts you off with simple placeholders. Each mode keeps its own entries.
  • Added Autism, ADHD, and CPTSD toggles (diagnosed or self-identified) change the suggestions throughout the binder, and small tags show why each one is there. Retreats can run 2, 3, 5, or 7 days. You choose your own morning cue, sanctuary space, contact plan, nature spot, movement, food rescue plan, and main sensory focus.
  • Added Build printable binder lets you pick the pages you want and Download PDF in one click, either blank to fill in by hand or filled in with your entries. If you’d rather use your browser’s print window, there’s also an Open print view button.
  • Changed The Adulting menu at the top of every page now lists the ND Self-Retreat Builder, and the Adulting page has a card for it.
  • Added A Where’d my button go?! tab on this page. If a button moved, was renamed, or was removed on any page, it’s listed there with where it used to be and where it is now.
  • Changed On every therapist tool, How to use this tool is now a dropdown at the top of the tool menu, with a short Why this matters note on the clinical reasons behind the tool. A new Your data dropdown explains where your entries live, and a References button jumps straight to the sources.
  • Changed The tool menu no longer scrolls inside itself. It stays put while it fits on your screen and scrolls with the page when it’s taller.
  • Changed The Letter Generator now writes the letter in a live preview as you type and highlights anything still missing, so there’s no Generate button to hunt for. It has two new letter types (gender-affirming care and a release of information form) and a draw-your-own signature pad.
  • Added Release of Information (ROI) is now featured on the Therapist Tools page and in the Therapist Tools menu on every page.
  • Changed The Note Generator is now the Session Note Generator, in the Therapist Tools menu on every page and on the Therapist Tools page. Like the Letter Generator, it writes your note in a live preview as you type.
  • Changed The Crisis Plan Generator is now the Safety / Crisis Plan Builder, in the Therapist Tools menu on every page and on the Therapist Tools page.
  • Added A Future Feature Attraction page. When something on the site isn’t ready yet, like the assessment PDFs, its link takes you there instead of to a dead page.
  • Fixed Multi-select boxes in the therapist tools (like insurances or specialties) no longer cut off their placeholder text when nothing is picked yet.

For Therapists

November 14, 2026

  • Added ND Self-Retreat Builder (in Adulting, next door to the therapist tools). A low-demand, zero-decision self-retreat planner for neurodivergent clients or clinicians, built on three daily anchors, with prep-week checklists, a daily body-weather tracker, a sensory lab, and journal prompts. Autism, ADHD, and CPTSD toggles shift the suggestions, and small tags show why each one is there. It can be a helpful handout for clients planning a rest week.
  • Added Download PDF builds the whole binder (blank or filled in) as a ready-to-print file, with no print dialog.
  • Added A new Where’d my button go?! tab on this page lists every button that has moved, been renamed, or been removed, page by page.
  • Added Upload practice info in every tool menu. Upload your Practice Info Sheet .json once and each tool fills in your name, license number, NPI, and practice contact details for you. It stays on your device until you click Forget practice data, and it never overwrites something you already typed. On the Practice Info Sheet itself, Use on my other tools does the same in one click.
  • Changed One shared diagnosis list now feeds the Note Generator, Letter Generator, Treatment Plan Builder, and Caseload at a Glance. It uses current billable ICD-10-CM codes (FY2026), and the ICD/DSM switch now shows the official ICD-10-CM title or the DSM-5-TR name for the same code, since DSM-5-TR uses ICD-10-CM codes. The old DSM-IV numeric codes (like 300.02) are gone.
  • Fixed Diagnosis list errors: suicidal ideation (R45.851) and homicidal ideation (R45.850) were swapped, Z63.5 and Z65.4 had the wrong names, a DSM code was listed twice, and several non-billable category codes (like F31 or F32 on their own) were mixed in with billable ones. Letter accommodation suggestions now follow the new codes.
  • Added Load sample in the How to use dropdown of the Practice Info Sheet, CEU Tracker, Supervision Log, Business Dashboard, and Practice Starting Aid. It fills the tool with a made-up example (Jordan Rivera at Cedar Path Counseling) so you can see how it works. The Practice Info sample also works as test practice data on the other tools. Samples for the other tools come with their updates.
  • Changed Every Sources list is left-aligned with an APA hanging indent, including the Assessment Database’s printed scoring sheet.
  • Changed Text in every field and dropdown is left-aligned and vertically centered, and the × that clears a multi-select (like Diagnosis or Theory) is now a clearer red button.
  • Fixed Generated notes now list three or more items with a serial (Oxford) comma, as in “CBT, DBT, and ACT”. The rest of the site’s text was checked and already uses it.
  • Changed Letter Generator, all letters: a live preview that highlights missing details; the client’s first and last name in the subject line; a “Respectfully,” sign-off; a license line with type, state, number, and expiration; and a signature block with your NPI, practice address, phone, and email that skips anything left blank. Every letter except the ROI ends with a verification clause: “Should you need to verify the authenticity of this letter, please contact my office at [contact info]. Please note that under HIPAA and [state] confidentiality laws, I can only confirm that this letter is valid; I cannot share any further information without a signed Release of Information (ROI) from my client.” The draft wording was lightly edited so it no longer reads as “can only verify … unless”, which contradicted itself. Your license state also adds that state’s law to the letter and to its Sources.
  • Added Signatures: upload an image as before, or draw your signature with a mouse, finger, or stylus.
  • Changed ESA letters: one or more animals (the wording switches between singular and plural), example description chips, your NPI, address, and phone, and a line that the animal is an emotional support animal, not a service animal under the ADA. Checkboxes record which major life activities are substantially limited (sleep, concentration, regulating physiological arousal, daily routines and ADLs, social functioning, or other). The disability-law list is now the FHA, Section 504, and your state’s law (for Washington, RCW 49.60); the ADA is no longer cited for housing. The “I am familiar…” line was rewritten around your treating relationship and direct assessment, and the “presence of this animal” paragraph was removed. A license expiration date in the past is flagged before you send the letter.
  • Changed Accommodation letters: open with “To Whom This May Concern”, include the client’s date of birth and length of treatment (calculated from “client since”), and allow several diagnoses at once. Suggested accommodations come from all of them. A toggle includes or leaves out the diagnosis and each accommodation’s rationale: recommended for a Disability Services Office, unchecked for a professor. The letter cites Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 and the ADA as amended by the ADAAA, adds a functional-impact paragraph, and follows AHEAD’s documentation guidance.
  • Changed Referral letters: a diagnosis picker (DSM-5-TR or ICD-10-CM names); a co-treatment, transfer of care, or assessment toggle; optional referral goals once a diagnosis is picked (medication/psychiatry, assessment, higher or lower level of care, PCP medical rule-out); an optional “what has worked well” note; and a safety line that says “no current safety concerns” or lists the concerns you check.
  • Added Gender-affirming care letter (draft), based on WPATH Standards of Care, Version 8. You pick the care requested, record your assessment basis, include a diagnosis if the recipient requires one, and check only the SOC-8 criteria your assessment supports. The letter uses the client’s name and pronouns, with their legal name only if you add it.
  • Added Release of Information (ROI) authorization (draft), with every element 45 C.F.R. § 164.508 requires: who releases, to whom (or release to, obtain from, or exchange), what information, the purpose, an expiration date or event, the right to revoke, no conditioning of treatment, the redisclosure notice, and signature lines, including an optional parent, guardian, or representative. It excludes psychotherapy notes, and can add the 42 C.F.R. Part 2 notice for substance use records. Both draft templates are marked “Draft: review before use”.
  • Fixed Letter Generator bugs: the referral letter’s “client since” date overwrote the ESA letter’s; the ESA template said “John’s” instead of the client’s name; a missing space after the client’s name; and diagnoses only came from one coding list. Diagnosis names now default to DSM-5-TR wording, which reads more naturally in a letter.
  • Changed Session Note Generator (renamed from Counseling Session Notes Generator): a live preview instead of a Generate button. Diagnoses read as “Generalized Anxiety Disorder (F41.1)”, without the old “ICD diagnosis of” wording. DSM-5-TR names are the default, and both views use ICD-10-CM codes.
  • Added Session details: session type (individual, couple, or family), who was present (with quick-add chips), an optional CPT code with add-ons (90785, 90840) and a hint that suggests the right code from the session length, and start times in 5-minute steps beginning at :00.
  • Changed Location is now one choice: in person, telehealth, or Other with a write-in. Telehealth asks where the client is (required), whether it was video or audio only, and for confirmation that the client was in a private location. It also reminds you about modifiers 95 and 93 and place of service 10 or 02.
  • Added Skills from the approaches you use: pick CBT, DBT, ACT, and so on, then choose the specific skills you worked on, like thought records or TIPP. All 47 approaches have a skill list, and you can type your own. The note says “The clinician used CBT to help the client practice thought records” instead of the generic “to support the client in building their skills”.
  • Added Risk and safety assessment on every note: suicidal ideation, homicidal ideation, self-harm, access to lethal means, overall risk, and safety plan status, with a link to the Safety / Crisis Plan Builder. Every answer starts unselected, so the note only states what you actually assessed.
  • Added An optional Mental Status Exam on every note format, with 12 domains, quick-pick chips, and a “within normal limits” button that fills typical findings for you to edit. The MSE note format now uses the same fields; older MSE notes import into them.
  • Added Intake (90791) note format, moved here from the Intake Packet Generator, with the orientation checklist, history sections, and diagnostic impression. It shares this tool’s risk, MSE, telehealth, and CPT fields.
  • Added Create superbill (.json) saves the session’s date, CPT code, place of service, telehealth modifier, and diagnoses for the Superbill Generator. Its per-session import arrives with the Superbill update.
  • Changed Diagnosis and Approach now sit side by side and line up at the top, and their clear-all × is easier to see. Notes saved in the old format still import, including the old times, lengths, and locations.
  • Changed Intake Packet Generator: every section now builds itself from your settings and updates the live preview as you type, with no “Apply Details” step. You can still edit any section’s wording. Edited sections are marked Edited and stop updating until you reset them. Packets saved in the old format keep your edited wording.
  • Changed Not just Washington anymore: pick your license state, and the disclosure statement, confidentiality laws, abuse reporting, record retention, minor consent, telehealth location, and complaint information follow it. Washington fills in its own laws and the Department of Health contact. Other states get neutral wording and a highlighted “[check your state’s rules]” where you need to add details.
  • Added License and education details: license type, state, and number as separate fields, plus degree type and field. Pick an associate license, and it asks for your supervisor’s name, license, and contact, then adds a supervision statement.
  • Added Fees and billing: separate intake, assessment, and session fees (90837 and 90834); a fixed fee or sliding scale; out-of-network, in-network (with your plans), or private pay; superbills; billing platform and payment methods; cancellation notice in hours plus a late fee. When you accept Medicaid, a note explains that missed-appointment fees can’t be charged; for Washington it names Apple Health.
  • Added Policies: an AI use statement (none, administrative only, or a note-assist tool with separate consent, plus how it handles data and a consent checkbox on the signature page), record retention, minor consent with a parent or guardian signature, and “no forensic or custody evaluations”. The questionnaire now asks how the client wants to be contacted (voicemail, texts, email).
  • Added A full Good Faith Estimate worksheet (No Surprises Act) filled in with your fees, a checklist of assessments to include from the Assessment Database, and your own section: upload a .txt or .md file, or paste text.
  • Changed One consolidated signature page lists every section the client is acknowledging, replacing the separate signature block in the disclosure statement. The “Part 1–8” labels and numbered headings were removed. The clinician’s 90791 note moved to the Session Note Generator; this page now links there.
  • Fixed The Washington consent text said child abuse is reported to DSHS. Since 2018 it goes to the Department of Children, Youth, and Families (DCYF), and vulnerable-adult abuse goes to DSHS Adult Protective Services. Also fixed: Forget practice data showed in every tool menu even when no practice data was saved.
  • Changed Treatment Plan Builder: plan styles are now add-ons. Check as many as fit (medical necessity, person-centered, neuro-affirming, trauma-phased, SFBT, youth and family), and each adds its focus to a new “Plan Approach” section. The plan preview updates as you work, so the Generate button is gone.
  • Changed Frequency and modality are now two separate dropdowns, each with an “Other” option. Strengths, protective factors, and resources are three separate boxes. Next review date has quick 30-, 90-, 180-, and 365-day buttons, counted from the plan date.
  • Added Interventions that follow your approach and diagnosis: each goal lists interventions built from the approaches you picked (using their skill lists) first, then interventions for the selected diagnoses, then the general library. Each one is tagged so you can tell where it came from.
  • Changed Discharge criteria and client participation are now two full-width paragraphs that start empty, with clickable examples to insert and edit, instead of prefilled text side by side. Section numbering was removed.
  • Added Import a session note: import a Session Note Generator .json to fill in the diagnoses, approaches, and plan date. Plans saved in the old format still open; the old combined frequency box moves into “Other”.
  • Changed Safety / Crisis Plan Builder (renamed from Crisis Plan Generator): plan types now combine into one plan. Check the core Stanley-Brown safety plan (marked Recommended) and add only what helps: wellness and recovery (WRAP), youth and caregiver, intimate partner safety, neurodivergent overload, or relapse prevention. A tip appears when you pick several, because a long plan is harder to use in a crisis. You can also write in your own plan title. The preview updates as you type.
  • Added Crisis lines follow the plan: pick a state and plan types, and the matching resources are checked for you. Domestic violence adds the National DV Hotline; youth adds the Trevor Project, Teen Line, and Childhelp; relapse prevention adds SAMHSA and Never Use Alone. Washington adds the WA Recovery Help Line, WA 211, the WA DV Hotline, and Teen Link. Any line you edit stays the way you set it.
  • Changed Warning signs and triggers are now separate lists, each with their own presets. “Reasons for living” and “What matters most” are separate boxes. The lethal means wording is now “Making my environment safer: limiting access to means”. “(Caregiver Responsibility)” was replaced with a selectable caregiver role (parent, guardian, foster parent, grandparent, or other), which carries into the plan. Step numbers and section numbering were removed.
  • Added Remove section on each part of the support network (people, professionals, and crisis lines), with an Add back link. The Remove button on each line now drops neatly below the fields on narrow screens instead of squeezing beside them.
  • Changed Assessment Database: the six combined entries are now 12 individual assessments, each with its own respondent, length, scoring, and APA citation: AQ, AQ-10, OCI-R, Y-BOCS (now correctly listed as clinician-rated), AUDIT, DAST-10, EDE-Q, NIAS, RCADS-25, Vanderbilt, WHODAS 2.0, and the DSM-5 Level 1 Cross-Cutting measure. That’s 23 assessments in all. The new citations are flagged for the upcoming references review.
  • Changed Filters now sit in a side panel like the Resources page: category, age group, who completes it, and symptoms measured. Each has check boxes with live counts, plus removable filter chips and a search box. Labels are shortened to fit on one line instead of being cut off.
  • Changed “Print scoring & citation sheet” is now Print Assessment(s), and a new Start over button clears your filters, selection, and the add/edit form.
