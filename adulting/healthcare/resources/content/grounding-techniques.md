Grounding brings your attention back to the present moment when you feel anxious, overwhelmed, or far away from yourself. Try one technique for a minute or two. If focusing on your body feels bad, switch to one that focuses on what’s around you instead.

## 5-4-3-2-1 Senses

Name, out loud or in your head:

- **5** things you can see
- **4** things you can feel or touch
- **3** things you can hear
- **2** things you can smell
- **1** thing you can taste

## Progressive Muscle Relaxation

Tense one muscle group for about 5 seconds, then let go and notice the difference for about 10 seconds. Work your way up the body:

| Area | How to tense it |
|---|---|
| Feet | Curl your toes |
| Calves and thighs | Point your toes up, then press your knees together |
| Stomach | Pull your belly in |
| Hands and arms | Make fists and bend your arms |
| Shoulders | Lift your shoulders toward your ears |
| Face | Scrunch your face, then relax your jaw |
| Whole body | Tense everything at once, then let it all go |

Skip any area that hurts or is injured.

## Body Awareness

1. Sit or lie down somewhere comfortable.
2. Notice where your body touches the chair, bed, or floor.
3. Press your feet into the ground and feel the support under you.
4. Wiggle your fingers and toes.
5. Notice the temperature of the air on your skin.
6. Take a slow breath and notice where you feel it.
7. Name one thing your body feels right now, without judging it.

## Other Quick Options

- Hold something cold, or splash cool water on your face.
- Describe an object near you in detail: its color, texture, and weight.
- Count backward from 100 by 7s, or name an animal for every letter of the alphabet.
