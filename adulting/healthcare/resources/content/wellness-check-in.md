Wellness has many parts, and they affect each other. Use the wheel to check in with yourself: give each of the eight areas below a score from 1 (near the center: running low) to 10 (at the edge: well cared for). The low spots show you where a little attention might help most.

![A wellness wheel with eight colored slices, each numbered from 1 at the center to 10 at the edge](images/wellness-check-in.png)

## Emotional Wellness

**What it is:** having a strong sense of self that helps you notice, share, and be kind to yourself through a wide range of emotions.

**Ideas:** practice self-compassion, say “I love you,” journal, practice gratitude, practice forgiveness, make space to feel, check in on your emotions, learn more about how your emotions arise and why, and reflect on life events.

## Social Wellness

**What it is:** building relationships, handling conflict well, and staying connected to a positive social network.

**Ideas:** check in with friends and family, spend time with coworkers, schedule time with loved ones, think about how you communicate, notice emotions that involve other people, reply to messages, go to a social event, or look up festivals in your area.

## Environmental Wellness

**What it is:** being aware of how you, your community, and your environment affect each other, and caring for all three responsibly.

**Ideas:** organize your space, make household cleaning easier, spend time outdoors and in nature, stay informed about the news and about local, state, and federal politics, reflect on your values and the world around you, or volunteer at a local shelter.

## Physical Wellness

**What it is:** taking care of your body so it can do what you want it to do.

**Ideas:** schedule routine checkups, take recovery time, have safe housing, think about what you eat, move your body, take uninterrupted time (including from phone notifications), be sexual, stretch, get enough sleep, and drink water.

## Intellectual Wellness

**What it is:** staying open to new ideas, being creative, thinking critically, and seeking out new challenges.

**Ideas:** have an interesting conversation, read a book, play a board game, take time to plan, write a poem, cook, make a vision board, take a class, watch educational videos, or learn something new.

## Spiritual Wellness

**What it is:** finding meaning in life events, having a sense of purpose, and living by your values and beliefs.

**Ideas:** go into nature, practice self-compassion, sing, dance, meditate, do yoga, pray, practice self-forgiveness, connect with the earth, find a spiritual community, think about your drive and purpose, find inspiration, volunteer for a cause, or list your values.

## Professional Wellness

**What it is:** having work that is interesting, enjoyable, and meaningful, and that contributes to the wider world.

**Ideas:** take a real lunch break, set boundaries, leave work at work, think about how your job fits your values, take a class, get supervision, talk with colleagues, plan your next career goal, use your vacation and sick time, and practice saying no.

## Financial Wellness

**What it is:** living within your means and managing money for the short and long term.

**Ideas:** set aside time to budget, talk openly about money with the people it affects, keep track of your budget and spending, save for emergencies, ask for help when you need it, and stay open-minded.
