Different decisions call for different tools. Pick the model that fits the decision in front of you.

## A Seven-Step Decision Model

1. Identify the decision you need to make.
2. Gather information about it.
3. Identify the alternatives.
4. Weigh the evidence for each alternative.
5. Choose among them.
6. Take action.
   - Would writing the decision down help you feel settled?
   - Who would benefit from knowing what you decided?
7. Review the decision later.

## Ethical Decision-Making Models

These come from ethics, but they work for everyday decisions too.

- **Consequentialism:** look at the likely consequences of each option.
- **Deontology:** decide based on duties, rules, and laws about right and wrong.
- **Virtue:** listen to your gut and your values. What would the person you want to be do?
- **Feminist ethics:** pay attention to relationships, care, and who holds power in the situation.
- **Discourse ethics:** reach the decision through open, fair conversation with the people it affects.

## RAID: Sorting Decisions and Their Ripple Effects

- **Risk:** did something come up that puts someone or something at risk?
- **Assumption:** was an assumption made that could affect things later?
- **Issue:** is there a problem that hasn’t been handled yet but is lower priority and can wait?
- **Dependency:** does something or someone depend on something else working?

## Architecture Decision Record

A decision model from the tech world that works well for workplace decisions.

First, ask yourself:

- Does your workplace have a preferred way of making decisions?
- Does your workplace want decisions documented?
- Would *you* like your decisions documented?

Then:

1. Name the problem.
2. Name the options.
3. Decide together with others, considering each alternative.
4. Write down the conclusion and why you chose it.
