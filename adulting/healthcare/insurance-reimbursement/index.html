Insurance Reimbursement Helper

If you see a therapist who isn’t in your insurance network, you usually pay up front, and they give you a superbill: an itemized receipt with the codes insurance needs. You send it to your insurance company, and if your plan has out-of-network benefits, they pay part of it back. This page walks you through it.

How to use this tool▾
  1. Check that your superbill has everything on the list. If your therapist gave you a .json file from the Superbill Generator, use Check a superbill in the tool menu.
  2. Call the number on your insurance card and fill in the answers as you go.
  3. Use the answers to estimate what you’ll get back.
  4. Send the claim, then log it so you can follow up.

Your privacy: nothing you type is sent anywhere. It stays in this browser tab unless you turn on Save in this browser or save a .json file.

Good to know: every plan is different. The estimate is a rough guide, not a promise of payment, and this page isn’t legal or financial advice.

Ask your plan

Call member services (the number on the back of your card) or check your plan’s website. Write the answers here.

Call log. Writing down who you talked to and the call’s reference number helps if there’s a problem later.

Estimate what you’ll get back

Uses the deductible, coinsurance, and allowed amount from the last step. The allowed amount is the most your plan counts for a session; if it’s lower than your therapist’s fee, you pay the difference.

Send it in

If a claim is denied, ask for the reason in writing. Fixing a missing code or date and resending is often enough. If not, most health plans have to let you appeal, and many must offer an outside review if the appeal fails. HealthCare.gov explains how appeals work.

Paying without insurance? Under the No Surprises Act, providers generally have to give you a Good Faith Estimate of costs ahead of time. CMS has the details.
