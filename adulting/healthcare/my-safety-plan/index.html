My Safety Plan

A safety plan is a short list you write ahead of time, for the moments when things get really hard. Fill in what you can. It works best when you make it with your therapist or someone you trust, and keep it somewhere easy to find.

If you might act on thoughts of hurting yourself right now, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), text HOME to 741741, or call 911. You don’t have to finish this page first.

How to use this tool▾
  1. Go through the steps in order. Each one is something to try if the step before it isn’t enough.
  2. Tap a suggestion to add it, or write your own words. Your own words work best.
  3. Pick your state to see crisis lines near you.
  4. Print your plan or save a copy on your phone. Share it with someone you trust if that feels okay.

Your privacy: nothing you type is sent anywhere. It stays in this browser tab unless you turn on Save in this browser or save a .json file.

Where this comes from: safety planning is one of the best-studied ways to get through a suicidal crisis (Stanley & Brown, 2012). This page is written in our own words. It isn’t the Stanley-Brown Safety Plan Form and isn’t affiliated with or endorsed by its authors; you can find the official form at suicidesafetyplan.com.

Making my space safer

Putting distance between yourself and things you could use to hurt yourself is one of the most protective steps there is. Think about medications, firearms, sharp objects, or anything else. Someone you trust can hold onto them, lock them up, or take them out of the house for a while.
