How I got from the stage to drama therapy.

My Path to Drama Therapy

Theatre has been part of my life since middle school, when I started doing oral interpretation. The idea of using it to help people came a little later. In high school I went on a trip abroad to teach English at a summer camp, and on the flight home I realized that helping others was what I wanted to do with my life. For my senior project I started a drama program for my district's sixth graders.

In my first college theatre course I watched a news story about The Actors' Gang running theatre programs in California prisons. That was when theatre and helping people came together for me, and I decided to become a drama therapist. At the University of South Dakota I earned a BFA in Theatre, a BS in Psychology, and an Arts in Healthcare certificate. Along the way I designed an independent study on drama therapy, co-founded an improv club, and presented my research at undergraduate conferences. With a research grant, I also built an eight-week summer theatre program for youth in a juvenile detention center. By the end of the summer, participants' social anxiety had dropped.

I went on to earn an MA in Counseling with a Drama Therapy specialization at Antioch University Seattle. I'm especially interested in working with young people, in role theory, and in geek therapy, which uses the games and stories people already love to help them understand themselves.

The essays I wrote when applying to graduate programs tell this story in more detail. You can read them under Academic Writing → Graduate School Applications.
