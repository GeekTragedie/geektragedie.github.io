## History of Mindfulness-Based Therapy

- Evolved out of <em>dharma</em> mindfulness teachings
    - Focuses on mindful attention without judgement
- Emphasizes “first-person experiences of suffering and happiness in human beings” (Kabat-Zinn, 2003, p. 147)
    - Becoming one with suffering (Chodron, 1997; Rosenbaum, 2017)

<p class="slide-notes"><strong>Speaker notes:</strong> Evolved out of <em>dharma</em> teachings which Buddhist teachings also stem from, but is different than Buddhism<br>Emphasizes “first-person experiences of suffering and happiness in human beings”<br>Instead of trying to distract the self from feelings of suffering, mindfulness invites clients to feel it and understand it. Through bringing awareness to the moments of suffering, a client can find points of origin and understanding to help increase self-compassion.<br>With this connection to suffering, MBSR started it’s growth</p>

</section>

<section class="slide">

## History of Research on MBSR

- Kabat-Zinn (founder) developed MBSR for medical patients dealing with a lot of physical suffering
    - University of Massachusetts Medical Center
    - Developed “as a complement” to medical treatment (Kabat-Zinn, 2003, p. 149)
- Aimed to be a model for other hospitals
- Medical Research
    - Skin clearing in psoriasis
    - Brain and immune system changes (EEG imaging)
    - Prostate cancer (Kabat-Zinn, 2003)
- Kabat-Zinn et al. (1992)’s study on Anxiety Disorders

<p class="slide-notes"><strong>Speaker notes:</strong> Knowing the pain and suffering medical patients were dealing with, MBSR aimed to relieve the suffering for these patients<br>Developed for medical patients dealing with a lot of physical suffering<br>Kabat-Zinn’s study on Anxiety Disorders<br>Brought MBSR into the realm of Psychology</p>

</section>

## Economic Benefit: Overarching

- Promotes Self-Care and Self-Management
- (Knight et al., 2015)

<p class="slide-notes"><strong>Speaker notes:</strong> Clients learn to continue their own care through mindfulness practices which decreases the cost of possibly needing to go back to therapy<br>Longitudinal study showed decreased return to therapy after both one and two year post-tests – if they did falter, the participants had stopped their mindfulness practices</p>

</section>

<section class="slide">

## Economic Benefits: Governmental Level

- Self-Management
    - Lower recidivism rates
    - Decrease substance use
- Healthcare
    - Group therapy setting
    - Decreases psychological distress
- Would save Canadian government $375,000 (Knight et al., 2015)

<p class="slide-notes"><strong>Speaker notes:</strong> Governmental Costs:<br>Government spends less on individual therapy because it is healing more people in less time<br>Psychological distress is the leading cause, in Canada, for people to use mental health insurance<br>MBSR participants decreased their healthcare cost by $250, with a total savings of $375,000 when applied to the general population</p>

</section>
