## Resistance: Adolescents

- “Build walls of indifference and defiance”
- Adults
  - Can’t be trusted
  - “Just don’t get it”
- Highly view peers
- Want sense of belonging
- Cognitive processes
  - Concrete terms
  - Restrain creativity
  - Control desires
  - Keep emotions contained
- (Gallo-Lopez, 2005)

<p class="slide-notes">Gallo-Lopez wrote an article that talks about the resistance drama therapists may face when trying to do drama with adolescents. She says they “build a wall of indifference and defiance.” They believe that adults can’t be trusted and “just don’t get it.” They view the opinion of their peers very highly. They also want a sense of belonging among their peers and in their environment. Adolescents have a certain cognitive processes that someone working with them should keep in mind. They think in concrete terms, the attempt to restrain from creativity because they were taught to do so in school. The have been told to control their desires and keep their emotions contained. All of these aspects are enhanced by being in the juvenile facility. Someone doing theatre with this group would have to ensure that the adolescents know that creativity, desires, and emotions are okay in those cirumstances.</p>

</section>

<section class="slide">

## Juvenile Adolescent Resistance

- Gallo-Lopez (2005):
  - Peer belonging “becomes problematic when adolescents are drawn into dangerous or self-destructive behavior as a result of their desire to belong, or when adolescents are isolated, excluded, and even ridiculed as a result of their inability to fit in” (p. 83).
- Juveniles are:
  - Isolated
  - Don’t fit in
  - “Locked away from a society that has never been kind to them” (Taylor, 2011, p. 205)
  - “looked down upon by others” (Hensley, 2016, p. 416)

<p class="slide-notes">Gallo-Lopez states that peer belonging “becomes problematic when adolescents are drawn into dangerous or self-destructive behavior as a result of their desire to belong, or when adolescents are isolated, excluded, and even ridiculed as a result of their inability to fit in”<br>Juveniles are isolated and excluded. They don’t feel as thought they fit in. Taylor states that juveniles are “Locked away from a society that has never been kind to them” and Hensley says that juveniles are “looked down upon by others.” This is hard on juveniles because of what they view as important in their life isn’t given to them because they are put out of society.</p>

</section>

<section class="slide">

## Resistance Break: General

- Humor
- Trust
  - Consistency, Respect, Acceptance
- Confidentiality
- Identify Significant Issues
- (Gallo-Lopez, 2005)

<p class="slide-notes">There are ways to break through the resistance! In general, you can use humor to break resistance. As an AIH-P, It is important for adolescents to trust you and for you to be there consistently. You must respect them and their trust. You must also accept them for who they are to keep their trust. It is important to keep confidentiality with them. It is also important to identify significant issues if they discuss them and think of how to understand their situation.</p>

</section>

## Arts in Health Breaks Barriers

- “Drama is a natural means of helping children ‘find and give expression to lost and hidden parts of themselves, and to build strength and self-hood” (Gallo-Lopez, 2005, 82).
- Humor
- Trust
- Cooperative activities
- Story-starters
- Video techniques
- Props
- (Gallo-Lopez, 2005)

<p class="slide-notes">There are ways that AIH can break these barriers as well. Gallo-Lopez says that “Drama is a natural means of helping children ‘find and give expression to lost and hidden parts of themselves, and to build strength and self-hood”.  Theatre can also be funny. Doing theatre, you must trust your partner to work with them. Theatre incorporates cooperative activities, so peers are working together. This breaks the barrier of feeling belonging.  Gallo-Lopez also says that doing story-starters, video techniques, and props also help engage the resistant teen. However, video techniques wouldn’t work with adolescents in a juvenile facility because technology is not allowed for visitors in the facility. Props is also difficult to use because only certain items are allowed into the facility. So, when working with juveniles, these wouldn’t work, but when working with adolescents outside of the facility, like in with an at-risk program for adolescents, this could work.</p>

</section>

## Theatre Teaches Adolescents

- Vocational abilities
- Social situations
- Inter-personal relations (Borden, 1940)
- Anger management (Karatas & Gokcakan, 2009, p. 1443)
- Self-Expression (Karatas & Gokcakan, 2009, p. 1443)
- Educate (Lauby et al., 2010)
- Emotions (Smeijsters, Kil, Kurstjens, Welten, & Willemars, 2011)
  - Displayed
  - Interpreted

<p class="slide-notes">Borden, while working in a juvenile girl’s facility, found that theatre helped increase the adolescents’ vocational abilities and with different social situations. Their inter-personal relations were also improved through the theatre. Karatas and Gokcakan found that theatre helped adolescents with anger management and self-expression. Lauby and collogues found that theatre can also help educate students both in and out of the program. This group of research on the bottom found that theatre really helps adolescents with their knowledge of emotions, both displaying them and interpreting them.</p>

## Ethical Consideration

- Not therapy
- Responsibilities and Respect
- Confidentiality
- Informed Consent
- Professional Relationships
- Principles of Teaching and Training
- Principles of Physical Contact, and Role Play
- Research Participants
- (NADTA, 2013)

<p class="slide-notes">This isn’t therapy. <br>Responsibilities and Respect – you must make sure that you adhere to the ethical codes and are to make sure the students keep their rights and dignity. <br>Confidentiality – keep all information confidential, unless there is informed consent <br>Informed Consent – because this is working with adolescents, consent must be given from a guardian<br>Professional Relationships – this includes being sure to not discriminate on anyone because of “age, gender, identity, race, ethnicity, culture, national origin, religion, sexual orientation, disability, SES, or any basis proscribed by the law” This also includes not engaging in any sexual activity. Given the previous slide discussing sexual assault, that is very important with this population. <br>Principles of Teaching and Training – being comfortable with the projects you are doing. Do not go outside of the area that you have been trained in. establish and keep boundaries. <br>Principles of Physical Contact, and Role Play – all contact is kept safe and is in a respectable manner with the best interests of the adolescents. Keep boundaries. Inform the adolescents that they have the right to refuse and refrain from any physical contact. <br>Research Participants – keep all ethical codes when working with human participants. Participants have the right to refuse to be in the research portion of the research. Must gain informed consent.</p>

</section>
