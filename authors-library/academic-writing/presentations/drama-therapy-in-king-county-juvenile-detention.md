## Definitions (because you aren’t my target audience)

- Resocialization
  - “the process of learning new attitudes and norms required for a new social role” (“Resocialization,” 2020)
- Recidivism
  - “the chronic tendency toward repetition of criminal or antisocial behavior patterns” (“Recidivism,” 2020)
- Recidivism rates
  - the percentage of people who are repeatedly arrested for the same behavior

<p class="slide-notes">Resocialization <br>Changing social roles and needing to learn new social norms <br>Example: Youth are going from the rules of the juvenile facility back to their community <br>Recidivism<br> repeating the same crime or illegal behavior<br>Recidivism rates<br>the percentage of people who are repeatedly arrested for the same behavior</p>

## Issues

- Difficult for youth to get access to mental health care (Desai et al., 2006)
- Higher suicide rates in juvenile facilities
  - 21.9 per 100,000 (JDC) vs 7 per 100,000 (general) (Teplin et al., 2015)
- Disproportionate amount of youth are people of color (US Department of Justice, 2015)
- Recidivism rates are crucial
  - Rearrest rates for youth within 1 year of release averaged 55%
  - Reincarceration rates within 1 year of release averaged 24% (Snyder & Sickmund, 2006)

## Solutions

- More mental health support should be provided
- Racial stories need to be held
- Intervention is recommended while incarcerated (Office of Juvenile Justice, 2017)
- Recidivism needs to be addressed
- Program is therapy
- Program is trauma-informed
- Interventions are tailored to the youth
- Resocialization programs and programs similar to this have proven to decrease recidivism rates
