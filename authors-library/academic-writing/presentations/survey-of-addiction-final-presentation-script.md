<section class="slide">

## Nature of the Problem

- Viewing substance use as illegal behaviors = the criminal justice system is taking precedence over the healthcare system
  - It is a legal issue instead of a health issue
  - Connects with the idea of substance use being a choice instead of a disease
- These people are criminals, so why should we put money into rehabilitating them?

</section>

<section class="slide">

## Barriers

- Policies surrounding treatment availability
- Lack of mental healthcare
- Lack of record sharing & communication
- Less focus on resocialization & education programs

</section>

<section class="slide">

## Policy Barriers

- Laws
  - Laws decide who gets treatment and who goes to jail
- Facility Policies
  - Policies do not include adequate mental health assessments
  - “Some patients… will not have a diagnosis, though it could be argued that most prisoners could be seen to fit one of the definitions in the DSM-IV” (p. 204).
  - Inmates with serious mental illness, such as schizophrenia, are more likely to receive mental healthcare than those suffering from other mental illnesses, such as major depression disorder or social anxiety disorder (Reingle Gonzalez & Connell, 2014).

</section>

<section class="slide">

## Mental Healthcare Barrier

- Higher risk of mental illness
  - “A dysfunctional family background, lack of education, limited employment opportunities, poor housing, and inner city deprivation all contribute to the likelihood of someone committing a crime” (p. 201).
  - “A substantial number of patients will have been victims of physical, emotional or sexual abuse, and have been brought up in families where domestic violence is common” (p. 205).
- Percentage imbalance
  - While 51.1% of the United States’ incarcerated population deals with mental health issues (p. 13), only 25.8% of the population receives counseling (Bronzon & Berzofsky, 2017, p. 8).
- Access to mental health treatment
  - Again, those being treated are only those who have been assessed as having mental health issues
  - Lack of access if someone decides later they want treatment
  - What if something were to happen to them while they are incarcerated?

</section>

<section class="slide">

## Lack of communication with Caregivers

- Reingle Gonzalez and Connell (2014) report only 52% of inmates who were incarcerated while taking medication for mental illness were given access to continue their medication while incarcerated.
- Has been proved to be beneficial, but it is not practiced

> “to assume or expect that a period of two years, plus or minus, is going to dramatically change [an inmate’s] entire personality is very immature thinking”
> 
> — Mintz, as cited in Ryan, 1976, p. 34

</section>

<section class="slide">

## Resocialization programing & education barriers

- Resocialization programs statistically have been proven to lower recidivism rates, but are not being funded
- Education programs are also proved to be beneficial, but are only offered to those with preexisting conditions, which people with mental health issues are not always flagged in the first place
- The education programs offered to all inmates are not addressing recidivism, overdose education, substance use treatment, mental health treatment, or healthcare education
- Inmates leave without a plan of obtaining adequate care, particularly insurance

</section>
