The morning of Friday, May 3, 2019, I interviewed [a clinician] who is a counselor at the reSTART Life treatment center in Bellevue, Washington. [Details of the clinician’s education, licensure, and career are left out.] By interviewing [the clinician], I learned more about people struggling with internet, video game, and virtual reality addiction, the demographics these process addictions influence, the treatment philosophy of reSTART, and their treatment process. After discussing my interview with [the clinician], I offer some critique of the program based on my interview.

## Organization Services

The reSTART program treats people from ages 13 to 30 years old struggling with internet, video game, or virtual reality addiction (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019). While this is the population the services are offered to, [the clinician] stated the clients are primarily males in early adulthood. When I asked about the ethnic background of clients, [the clinician] said “diversity is great,” but clients tend to be from wealthier families; and my question regarding ethnic background went unanswered (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019).

While reSTART focuses on these process addictions, [the clinician] noted other mental health issues their clients have been diagnosed with prior to admittance: anxiety, depression, and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019). Sometimes clients’ gaming addiction results from using gaming as a coping strategy for the clients’ other mental health issues, so clinicians at the treatment center also help clients with their other diagnosis. Clients are brought to reSTART mainly to create a social network offline, get them away from technology, and continue their academics (many adolescents are brought into the program for refusing to go to school) (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019).

## Treatment Philosophy

The goal of the reSTART facility is to have clients stop using video games through relationship-based therapy (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019). The clinicians working at this center do not have an overarching specific therapeutic approach, but instead focus on connecting clients to one another in person. The licensed clinicians are free to use their own personal approaches as long as they are working to treat the addiction; clinicians that have worked at the center have specialized in cognitive behavioral therapy, group therapy, dance therapy, outdoor therapy, mindfulness, and wilderness therapy. reSTART takes an abstinence-based approach regarding gaming and transitions clients into using technology by teaching clients how to healthily use technology, which was said to be the most challenging part of the work due to possible relapse (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019). An example of healthy use would be for a client to use a cell phone to only make a phone call and resist the urge of playing an application for longer than expected after making the phone call.

## Treatment Process

reSTART is an in-patient treatment center that has two types of treatment programs: one for clients ages 13 to 17 and another for clients ages 18 to 30 (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019). The different programs are primarily to separate the different age groups. They both complete intake assessments, have time in nature, go through a therapy-centered stage, and transition out; however, the older group has the option to complete a special transition program (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019).

### Getting Started

Before starting at the treatment center, the clients complete an intake interview and assessment. The assessment and intake process are used for the clinicians to better understand the severity level of the addiction as well as any other diagnosis the client may have. The client is then introduced to the program’s steps and what to expect during treatment (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019).

### Nature Stage

Following the intake, the first stage is sending the clients into nature in Carnation, WA where they detox from internet and video games. Teens go to “Serenity Mountain” where they can continue to complete their studies while learning to “cope with life’s stresses” in “a screen free healing environment to connect with what matters most – life” (reSTART, n.d., para. 1). Adult clients’ go to “Rise-Up Ranch” where they can connect with animals, create friendships, and create a “foundation for lasting change” (reSTART, n.d., para. 2). There is some therapy offered at this stage, but the focus is to make interpersonal connections with others face-to-face and create self-identity separate from the video game realm (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019).

### Heavensfield

After connecting to nature and finding the self, clients are brought to Heavensfield, where more therapy-centered work is done (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019). The time at this stage of treatment varies depending on the client and their age group. The goal at this stage is to have clinicians and staff aid the clients in “build[ing] a plan for a sustainable future” (reSTART, n.d., para. 3). The therapy done at this stage can be individual, but is primarily group therapy, as the overarching philosophy of reSTART is relationship-based.

### Transition Out

The transition part of the program is only for clients older than 18 because they can live on their own. This program is six months long and helps young adults how to be independent and live on their own without their addiction. The clients are housed together with other clients and are assisted in career development, living alone, and how to manage their time (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019).

### Relapse

Relapse certainly happens to clients struggling with these addictions. There are few clients that experience relapse after completing this program, but those who tend to relapse are clients who opted out of the six-month transition program. If a client is brought back to reSTART after a relapse, they must start the program over again to experience the detox stage and get acclimated with new clients in person. There is no program designed specifically for clients who experienced relapse (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019).

## Critique

The work done at reSTART is beneficial to many people, which should not be overlooked; however, I have critiques about the way the program thrives and believe future research should be considered for this population. My main critique regards the demographics of the clients at this treatment center as it appears the clients are mainly rich, white males. The fact that [the clinician] did not directly answer my question regarding diversity makes me feel as though the clients served are primarily white, which makes me question the marketing techniques and treatment modalities used in the center if they are not gaining clients with a wide racial background. I am curious as to the population that suffers from this particular process addiction, but there seems to be a lack of evidence showing cultural backgrounds of this population.

Another critique about this program is that reSTART Life does not take insurance, nor does it work on a sliding scale (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019). The reSTART program appears to be too expensive for clients of low and middle socioeconomic status. [The clinician] stated there are scholarships available, though the event of scholarships being offered is very rare. [The clinician] also stated that their clients primarily come from wealthy families (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019), which is not surprising given the payment options available to clients.

A final critique of reSTART is that it does not work with the family system. The facility takes in the clients and builds bonds through the group but does not appear to offer family therapy. [The clinician] says a focus of the treatment program is to help clients become independent and support themselves. Clinicians do not want to transition clients back into their own home for fear of possible relapse (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019). This technique does not help the family system reorganize to accompany the treated addiction. The technique also does not take into account cultures that are community based and do not strive for individual strength outside of the community.

## Conclusion

[The clinician] is a passionate clinician at reSTART Life that deeply cares about his clients, evidenced by the way he spoke about his clients and feelings regarding their treatment process. There is a certain demographic that tends to attend this treatment center, but the program is open to those who can afford it between the ages of 13 and 30. The treatment rates of this relationship-based program are promising. While there are critiques to the program, it is important to keep in mind the rewarding aspects of the clinicians’ work: “providing relief to the families… and creating the interpersonal relationships and [lifelong] friendships” (clinician, personal communication, May 3, 2019).
