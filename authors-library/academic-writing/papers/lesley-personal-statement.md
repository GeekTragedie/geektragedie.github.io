I went on a mission trip to Ukraine my junior year of high school where I organized camp activities and taught Ukrainian students English. I enjoyed working with the students to break down our language barrier as we took on a variety of tasks, such as Camp Olympics and fixing up the school yard. On my flight home, I reflected on the trip and realized how much I enjoyed helping other people, and from that moment, helping others became my main goal in life. When I view myself, I do not see individual characteristics, I see the stories, like my Ukraine experience, that have shaped who I am and who I want to become.

I had a passion for theatre in high school, which I followed into college, becoming a theatre major. In my first theatre course, I watched a news broadcast highlighting the work of Tim Robbins and The Actors’ Gang in the California prison system. The program led by Robbins taught prisoners to express emotions they have been taught to suppress. The work done by The Actors’ Gang intrigued me because they brought theatre to other populations who do not have easy access to drama programs. This news program showed me that theatre can be used to improve the lives of others, which motivated me to combine my aspiration of helping others with my love for theatre by becoming a drama therapist. With my interest in theatre with the prison population, Dr. Jason Butler’s work with the homeless in New York reminds me of what brought me to drama therapy given the marginalized nature of the population. While the two populations are different, the reason I connect the two is due to the fact they both are people outcast and separated from society. The knowledge that Lesley graduates have worked in correctional facilities also shows Lesley can provide me a bright future.

My sophomore year of college, I was one of the first students to participate in the Arts in Health certificate program with professors Alison Boughn and Ariadne Albright at USD. This program gave me the ability to learn about how the arts work with different populations and experience the work firsthand with college students, dementia patients, adults with developmental disabilities, and elders. My experience working with diverse populations reinforced my beliefs in the benefits of the arts. Due to the places where Lesley’s graduate students are located, I believe I can continue to work with diverse populations in the program.

In my sophomore year of college, I co-founded the USD Improv Club. With my creative abilities, I was able to help others advance their own improvisational skills. I also fundraised and organized the contract to bring Upright Citizens Brigade (UCB) from New York to our campus to offer workshops and perform and our club learned a lot from UCB’s attendance. The club has grown to forty-three members, and performances became a place where other college students could come and relax. I am proud to have used my leadership abilities to create this club for people who have utilized its activities, meetings, and performances. I would like to continue the use of improvisation in my work as a drama therapist, which I believe Lesley University can help me achieve.

Later in my collegiate career, my leadership skills were shaped through my experiences as a director. I have learned that a successful director needs to have a strong sense of what they want their production goals to be. They then need to lead the artistic team, designers, and performers, down this path. When directing my production of *… and others* at USD, I acknowledged the fact that I knew little regarding lighting and sound, so I fully trusted my designers, and I was thoroughly impressed with their abilities which taught me that full collaboration and trust when working in a team makes for the best outcome. I was blessed with the ability to direct again for the department when a professor approached me and asked me to direct another one act play, *‘dentity Crisis.* This play helped me explore my directing abilities in a comedy show and I honed my skills through this production, learning how to make actors a larger part of the collaborative team. With my experience in directing, I am drawn to Lesley University’s use of performance to understand inner experience as part of the drama therapy process.

Using self-motivation, I got into a competitive research grant program the summer after my senior year. After being awarded this grant, I created an eight-week, summer theatre program for a local juvenile detention center (JDC) which consisted of improvisation, mask creation, ambiguous scene work, playwriting, and performance. I designed a questionnaire that measured self-esteem, self-consciousness, social anxiety, and self-concept. At the conclusion of the grant program, I had gained statistically significant information showing that participants’ social anxiety decreased by the end of the summer program. While I was pleased to have gained quantitative information, I feel as though I have learned more about the JDC population.  I was moved by one of the adolescents sharing that, with theatre, she learned she could have fun without using drugs, and another voiced she learned theatre was a positive outlet for emotions. I am overjoyed I started this project because I was able to inspire the youth in this facility to enjoy theatre and gave them a creative outlet to express themselves. I believe Lesley can help me learn more about how drama therapy can be used with this population through their course work and internship opportunities. I also believe that Lesley can help me increase my research abilities. As a drama therapist, I would like to continue research, enhancing the field of drama therapy. I believe that Lesley’s theory and research courses can help me continue this pursuit.

To finish my Arts in Health certificate program, I applied for an internship at Sanford Arts with a supervisor who graduated from Lesley University’s Expressive Arts program. I have started to work in their children’s wing, cancer center, and rehabilitation center shadowing expressive art therapists and artists in residence at the facility. I look forward to using my abilities and create diverse arts projects for the different populations. I cannot wait for the ideas I currently have to make a difference in the lives of patients at Sanford. I believe this internship will be a great experience to help prepare me for graduate school where I can learn new drama therapy techniques to help individual populations.

After hearing about my interest in drama therapy, my internship supervisor and previous professor, Alison Boughn, highly recommended Lesley University. With a master’s degree in drama therapy, I would like to help adolescents learn about themselves and give them tools to help them in the future, particularly those in foster care, incarcerated, or at-risk. The combination of improvisation, research, and diverse locations, I believe Lesley University can provide me the skill sets I need to be an influential drama therapist.
