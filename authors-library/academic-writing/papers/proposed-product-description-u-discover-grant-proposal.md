## Significance

Arts in Healthcare (AIH) is a growing field where the fine arts are used in healthcare facilities to help increase the overall wellbeing of those who use their services. The Global Alliance for Arts & Health (2017) defines AIH as “a diverse, multidisciplinary field dedicated to transforming health and healing through the arts” (para. 1). Some modalities that fall under the concept of arts are visual arts, music, movement, and theatre. Theatre offers many positive effects for audiences and participants in many settings. Catterall, Chapleau, and Iwanaga (1999) find that theatre arts promotes “gains in self concept and motivation, and higher levels of empathy and tolerance for others” (p. 2). Emunah and Johnson (1983) have found that theatre performance helped increase the self-images of psychiatric patients. In the AIH field, theatre programs have been implemented in many facilities, such as prisons, retirement homes, hospitals, and juvenile detention centers (JDCs), to improve the wellbeing of residents, patients, and clients of these facilities. One theatre program that has seen significant progress is famous actor Tim Robbin’s program with The Actor’s Gang in California. This program has helped decrease the reoffending rates by 10.6 percent and the “men who took the active class... [showed] an 89% reduction in infractions” (Rapkin, 2016; Bissell, 2016, para. 7). Julia Taylor writes about her experiences working at Vista Maria, a school for juvenile girls. Taylor (2011) discusses how theatre “gives a voice to those who have lost theirs” (p. 198). Taylor (2011) comes to the empowering realization that, when doing theatre, “individuals become free, free from our histories that keep us confined in a one-dimensional version of ourselves” (p. 211). With the AIH field growing, it is important to research the effects to help support this field’s base. The research must be conducted within a variety of facilities and with diverse populations. Research on theatre’s effects on adolescents living in JDCs is slim. While there are case studies that have been conducted, there seems to be a lack of quantitative research done with this population. With theatre being a medium of AIH, conducting research within a JDC will help expand the field and diversify the field’s population. It is crucial to collect statistical data of the effects theatre has on the adolescents in the Minnehaha JDC for the credibility of the AIH field but, more importantly, it is important work with this center to create a program that will engage, inspire, and increase the quality of life for the adolescents.

To connect with the Community-Based Research requirements, Minnehaha JDC and I discussed the benefits of this program for the adolescents. The Minnehaha JDC’s goal is to help and support the adolescents that stay in their care. Knowing the goals of the facility and the researched effects of theatre on various populations, I hope to increase the overall wellbeing of the adolescents, as well as their self-image, self-esteem, and self-concept. Improving these three traits will help the adolescents once they leave the facility through interactions with others and their feelings towards everyday-life experiences. These tools will increase the support the adolescents have, helping meet the goals Minnehaha JDC aims to achieve. To best meet these goals, the facility and I need to ensure that we produce an environment that is transformational and empowering to the adolescents. Creating an environment like this will help the adolescents feel important to society and not an outcast population, as Taylor states many adolescents feel in a JDC (Taylor, 2011).

## Project Description

My project will measure aspects that will help grow the adolescents and increase skills for the future. This project will allow me to conduct research that measures the self-esteem, self-image, and self-concept of adolescents in the Minnehaha JDC. While some people may consider these concepts to be the same, the three words have different meanings. Bruce Blaine (2013) defines self-esteem as “feelings of personal value, worthiness and competence” (p. 213). He defines self-image as “our views of ourselves as good, worthwhile, and competent” (Blaine, 2013, p. 72) and self-concept as “the summary of one’s self-knowledge [of their] traits, roles, and abilities” (Blaine, 2013, p. 211). The process for conducting this research can be split into four steps: program creation, survey creation, program implementation, and research analysis. The first step of this project is creating the program. I am enrolled in an AIH class that focuses on diverse populations, ethics, and multiculturalism. In this class, we have the opportunity create a program to fit a specific population. The population I chose for this class is adolescents in juvenile facilities. I will create an 8-week program for this class and will then get the program approved by my AIH professor before implementing it at the Minnehaha JDC. The program will include different aspects of theatre including: improvisation, play and monologue writing, monologue performance, 10-minute scene performance, and mask creating. Depending on adolescents’ interests and what is and is not allowed in the JDC, one area may take more precedence. The program design will be completed before the end of the 2017 Spring semester.

The second step in this process will be to create the survey that will be given to the adolescents. I will research how other scholars have measured self-image, self-esteem, and self-concept to see the safest and most ethical way to write and conduct my survey. The survey will be relatively short to ensure the attention span of the adolescents. The hypothesis I would like to test will be open-ended to test for the overall effect: Theatre has an effect of adolescents living in juvenile detention centers. This way I can see if theatre has a positive or a negative effect on these adolescents, even though research does not support a negative effect. Since this research will be conducted with human participants, the survey and program will then have to be submitted to the institutional review board (IRB) for approval.

The third step to this project will be to conduct the program itself. To perform some of the workshops, materials must be bought throughout the program process. Due to the contents of the workshops, the materials that will need to be purchased for this project include 10-minute scene books, monologue books, writing materials (paper and pencil), and mask creation materials (markers, string, three-hole punch, scissors, and paper plates). Due to the regulations at Minnehaha JDC, the males and females need to be split into two different groups. With this to take into consideration, there will be two workshops each week. The boy’s workshop will take place for an hour on Tuesdays for 8 weeks and the girl’s workshop will take place for an hour on Thursdays for 8 weeks. The workshops will start Tuesday, June 13 and will end Thursday, August 10. The workshops will have the same games and projects for the boy’s group as the girl’s group. The last days of meeting with the adolescents, I will distribute the same surveys as the first day, with an additional comment section, to analyze the change in self-concept, self-image, and self-esteem.

The fourth step to this project will be to analyze the data found. I will create a normal bell curve based off z-scores depending on the possible changes in the adolescent’s attributes. Once the data has been analyzed, I will be able to conclude whether my hypothesis is supported. The conclusion surveys will also have a place for comments if adolescents would like to add anything about the program. That way, there can be some qualitative data with the quantitative. Getting feedback that is not necessarily measurable is also very important when looking at the effects of AIH and theatre in general.

At the end of my research, I will be able to then apply and attend IdeaFest to make my research available to the public. With this research, I hope to help the University of South Dakota make an influence in the AIH field, possibly helping them gain recognition for their AIH program. This opportunity will help advance the research in the AIH and theatre fields. I will also be able to gain the experience of presenting and explaining my own research to other people.
