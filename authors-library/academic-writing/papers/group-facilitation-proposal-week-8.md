In Antioch University Seattle’s Fall 2019 group counseling course, GeekTragedie and a colleague (authors) will co-facilitate a pseudo-group counseling session with their classmates. In preparation for this session, the co-facilitators chose their theoretical lens, ensured to consider the level of group development, and created goals they wish to accomplish while leading. The facilitation outline can be found in Appendix A of this document. The purpose of this essay is to consider and aid the facilitators, future counselors, in thinking about how to prepare for a group counseling session.

## Theoretical Orientation

With the group having officially met seven sessions the co-facilitators believe the group is needing to transition from the working stage to the final stage (Corey et al., 2018). Corey et al. (2018) recommends the use of an integrative approach to group counseling. Based on Corey et al.’s recommendation, the co-facilitators aim to utilize an integrative approach of person-centered therapy, drama therapy, Gestalt therapy, mindfulness-based therapy, and cognitive behavioral therapy. Throughout the group session, facilitators would like to focus on preparing the group for termination by offering a synopsis of where the group has been from its inception to current state. To prepare the group facilitators aim to focus on the following themes: finalization, endings, loss, growth, and self-awareness.

During the middle section of the group facilitators will focus on the group process. Members will begin with an invitation for any reflections or reactions from last week’s group to include specifically reflections around some of the common themes [from the group’s previous sessions]. The facilitators will also try to hold space for anyone who has not shared much, as well as link members who might have shared similar experiences. To focus on an integrative approach during the session, the co-facilitators will be attuned the needs as they arise through listening to group members’ tones, watching members’ body language and interactions, and noting recurring themes within the setting.

## Session Goals

The co-facilitators have three goals for this group session. First, facilitators would like to focus on maintaining the here-and-now. Yalom and Leszcz (2005) highly encourage the use of bringing group members into the here-and-now to develop more group cohesion and develop deeper relationships through experience. To meet the goal of staying in the here-and-now, the facilitators plan to ask members how they feel in the moment while sharing information with the group. The facilitators will invite members to utilize “I” statements while interacting. The initial check-in also asks members to reflect on their feelings in the here-and-now regarding their goals for group sessions.

Secondly, facilitators want to allow the group to maintain the equilibrium by itself with less interaction from the facilitators. With the group being in the working stage and almost the termination stage, facilitators hope the members will be able to hold the session themselves because of the level of group cohesion that has been developed over the course of the sessions. While the check-in provides a starting point for the session to help the group think about their goals, the group should be able to continue their discussion and process themselves afterwards.

Thirdly, the facilitators will prepare members for the finalization of the group counseling cohort. By addressing unfinished business and allowing the group the opportunity to observe its growth from the first session up to the current eighth session, the facilitators’ hopes are that the group will develop its own techniques and methods for the termination. Facilitators also hope to remind members to think about their goals of the group and welcome them to work and reflect on their goals in this final stage. To prepare for this goal, the facilitators have added a starting activity to aid members in thinking about and reflecting on their goals.
