<p class="paper-title">Metatheatre and the Humanist Perspective in Early Modern England</p>

Nikos Kazantzakis, a Greek novelist and playwright, writes, “Since we cannot change reality, let us change the eyes which see reality.” The eyes of reality are changed through theatre by the themes the plays contain and the way the stories are presented to the audience. Audiences during the Early Modern English Period were effected by theatre through the plays instilling the Humanist perspective and the characters thought processes. Humanism is “the belief that man, and not God, \[control\] his own fate” (Genna). Humanism contradicts the previously believed Great Chain of Being but, overall, creates a dissonance with medieval thinking. By theatre portraying humanist ideas, the audience sees reality differently. Audiences are also introduced to the idea of metatheatre during this period, also expanding the audience’s perspective of reality. Through the theatricality of performance in Kyd’s *The Spanish Tragedy* and William Shakespeare’s *Hamlet* during the Renaissance, one can see theatre impacting the change in society to more fully understanding Humanist ideals through the metatheatricality used and themes discussed or shown in the plays.

*The Spanish Tragedy* changes the perspective of audiences’ reality by confusing the difference between life and death and metatheatre. Kyd creates the allusion of confusing life and death to an audience. The first way this is shown is by Andrea watching all the action unfold and commenting on the action every act. Andrea is already dead, but the audience sees him come back to observe the characters and give his opinions. Medieval Christian beliefs did not include this state that Andrea is in, increasing the already assumed tension between the medieval and humanistic ideas. Audiences see Andrea in this state and question whether they could be in the same state. Questioning this state of life falls under the humanistic perspective because it is humans thinking about the actions they would do to fall into this state Andrea is in. These thoughts and actions give agency to humans to create their own path and not to a god that would create humans’ fate. If humans believe it is by their own deeds that can get them to heaven, through these ideas, audiences can believe they have the right to control these actions. Through audiences watching the dead Andrea watch the actions of the play unfold, there is a submersion of life and death that has the audience questioning their own life

Through the audience watching Andrea watching, there is an aspect of metatheatricality happening that influences the audiences’ thought process. Andrea reacts to the events happening in the play and how they make him feel. These reactions are that of a devoted audience member when watching a play, even though Andrea personally knew the performers. Through watching Andrea’s reactions, the audience can see how they may react to the actions happening in the play. The audience is shown how they should feel and then may possibly feel this way as well. By showing the audience how they should feel, the actors manipulate the audience, bringing into question the separation of theatre and reality. If the audience is being told how to react to situations, they are learning how to act situationally through the psychological learning phenomenon called modeling. People learn through following another’s actions. Andrea, an audience member to the play, is modeling for the audience how they should behave in regards to the material in the play. This modeling questions the separation of theatre and reality because of the direct impact and possible manipulation of the audience. Using modeling and metatheatre, *The Spanish Tragedy* effects the way audiences should think and act to the situations the various characters find themselves in.

Another impactful metatheatrical moment in *The Spanish Tragedy* is the play within the play in 4.4. The audience watches the audience characters in the play watching the play. The idea of modeling still comes into effect. However, the biggest impact of this performance in the play is the deaths in the play. The characters in the play within the play die. While this is normal, the actors in the play within the play realistically, in the play, kill themselves. The audience watching *The Spanish Tragedy* view the play within the play as normal, but then have similar reactions as the audience members in the play to the deaths that more realistically happen. While the actors playing the characters of Balthazar, Lorenzo, and Bel-Imperia do not die in real life, they give off the illusion of doing so by the play killing actors during their performance. The actors dying in the play creates questions in the minds of the audiences of real actors dying in plays. The audience wonders what it would be like if their actors were to die on stage. These questions break the barrier between theatre and reality because the audience starts to consider the life of the actors in the real world. These ideas emphasize the humanistic ideas by having audiences question reality and combines theatre with reality through these questions.

*Hamlet* uses spying, characters’ acting and agency, and questioning life to change the perspectives of audiences’ reality. The idea of spying is heavily prevalent throughout the play: Claudius spying on Hamlet using Ophelia, Rosencrantz, Guildenstern, and Polonius, Polonius spying on Laertes using Reynaldo, Hamlet spying on Claudius himself. These ideas of spying creates a constant audience to all the action and behaviors on stage. The characters are always being watched, therefore, the characters always have an audience. If there is a constant audience, then all actions in life are a performance for those around us. The idea of a constant audience impacts audiences of *Hamlet* as they realize their own lives are under constant watch. While the characters in the play are always being watched, they are also in control of their own actions or performances. Having this control falls under the idea of humanism; the ability to control your own life calms audiences who may feel they are constantly watched by society. The humanist idea can be seen through the idea of spying in *Hamlet* with the constant spying and the idea of having control over your own performance of life.

Another performance in *Hamlet* is Hamlet faking that he is mad throughout the play. Hamlet, the character, is acting as though he is mad, but the character Hamlet is not actually mad, meaning Hamlet is constantly performing in the play as though he is mad. The audience to his madness, in the play, is the rest of the characters in *Hamlet* and the audience watching the performance of a mad man. This madness is part of Hamlet’s plan of taking control of his life and creating a fate for Claudius. These actions are Hamlet giving himself agency in his life. While the medieval ideas believed God created humans’ fates, *Hamlet* is giving off other ideas. Hamlet creates his own fate, fitting the Early Modern English idea of humanism.

The idea of questioning medieval ideas is also furthered through Hamlet’s “To be or not to be” in 3.1. This speech is Hamlet debating death and whether or he should kill himself. Hamlet questions what would happen to him after he dies. This idea would not have been considered in the medieval period because they believed that after death humans went to heaven or hell depending on the life they lead. They believed that if someone did good and lived a good life, they would go to heaven without questioning it. Hamlet’s speech states otherwise by questioning what would happen if he were “to die, to sleep -- / to sleep, perchance to dream” (3.1.65-6). Hamlet questioning life after death raises the same questions to the audience watching Hamlet debate this subject. The audiences’ way of thinking changes pertaining to what comes after life on earth. Through Hamlet questioning life after death, he reinforces the humanist ideals and also changes the way audiences view life.

*Hamlet* and *The Spanish Tragedy* question medieval ideals and apply more Humanist ideals in their plays through their metatheatricality and themes shown or discussed in the plays. *The Spanish Tragedy* utilizes Andrea’s reactions and state to question audience’s existence and reactions to situations happening in the play. This play also causes audiences to view deaths of characters/actors differently due to the deaths in the play, bridging theatre and reality. *Hamlet* uses the idea of spying and agency to show audiences that they too have audiences, but have control over what that audience sees. This play also questions life after death, going against the ideals of the medieval period. After analyzing the representation of humanism and medieval ideals, both plays seem to use the humanistic ideals more than that of the medieval ideals. Both plays used metatheatre to connect with their audiences on a familiar level. Metatheatre can effect audiences in different ways and can be a great tool in showing audiences how to think in different ways. Theatre creators need to remember, “Since we cannot change reality, let us change the eyes which see reality” (Kazantzakis).
