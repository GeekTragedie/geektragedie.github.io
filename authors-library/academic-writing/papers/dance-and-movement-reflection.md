## Dance and Movement Reflection

Dance/Movement Therapy (DmT) is a form of Creative Arts Therapy (CAT) that requests participants to control their body and feel their impulses to work through feelings and sensations. As a Drama Therapy student, it is important for me to understand the work of Dance/Movement Therapists to create comradery between Creative Arts Therapists and understand the strengths of each field. Learning the different CATs can help Drama Therapists create relationships for possible consultations and references for clients. This paper explains classroom experientials done with my CAT class and details moments during the experience that continue to impact me upon reflection.

## Experiential Activities

On January 10, 2019, the CAT course had a guest presenter, a dance/movement therapist, lead the class through three DmT exercises. The first exercise was a warm-up called Flocking. The facilitator arranged participants in a diamond formation then asked the participants to lead and follow various movements to music. There were groups of four and, over the course of the exercise, more groups were added to move at the same time. I volunteered to begin this exercise and found joy leading and following movements to the music. I did not enjoy witnessing others move, because I felt left out or wanted to move with them. This sensation may have been formed by volunteering to start the exercise or could simply be the fact I love dancing.

The second DmT exercise was done in pairs where one partner explained a struggle they were experiencing, and the other partner reflected their struggle back in form of movement. I felt some resistance to this exercise, because I did not want the responsibility of holding my partner’s personal story. I worried I would not do an honorable job reflecting the story back in my movement. Through the discussion of my movement upon completion, my partner verbalized connection to my movements, which made me feel more comfortable in my ability to hold another’s story.

The final exercise lead by the facilitator was a DmT form called Authentic Movement. The facilitator explained the different impulses one may feel during this exercise and encouraged the group to react upon these impulses to move for ten minutes. After the movement, the facilitator, the witness, gave each participant the chance to share their movement, and then shared her experience to each participant’s movement. I have previous experience moving to impulses from a movement class I took in my undergraduate degree and was excited for the opportunity to move with my impulses again. Being comfortable with the exercise, I allowed myself to remain open to what I was feeling in the moment, which is a rare occasion for me. I was nervous to hear the facilitator’s reflection of my movements but was amazed as to what she experienced in relation to my movement. There were aspects I had forgotten I had experienced during my movement that she commented on during her reflection. I was able to remember these feelings during her discussion after the movement.

## Personal Experiences

Reflecting upon the experiential, there were various moments during the DmT exercises where I felt physical and emotional responses, high engagement, as well as over and underwhelmed by the activities. There were many movements I performed during this experience in which my body reacted in various ways. After the Flocking exercise, I noticed I became short of breath, thirsty, and tired. While I felt physically tired, I was more mentally focused after the warm up. I was more awake and ready to be present for the experience. During the partner exercise, I was more relaxed and used slower and shorter movements for expression. My movements during the Authentic Movement experience were combined with fast, slow, and no movement; I let myself explore and be more in tuned with what my body was feeling. Overall, I noticed my physical movements wanting to explore the various planes the body can use: forward/backward, up/down, and out/in. Exploring these planes allowed me to feel how my body reacted to each plane and what emotions arose.

A moment I was most engaged and continues to resonate emotionally with me after the session was during the partner movement exercise while I was the mover reflecting my partner’s struggle. Many emotions transpired during the DmT experiential including happiness, frustration, calmness, worry, and anger. My familiarity with sadness and guilt was felt immensely with the movements I performed for my partner. This moment was the only memory that arose during the session as I remembered the feelings and times I spent worrying about my brother before he passed. My movements reminded me of the times I sat feeling as though I should have done more to help him. As I lay with a blank stare on the floor, I remembered the empty sadness I am still very familiar with. I do not recall a time that I have ever resonated so much with a piece of art in my life.

I felt under-challenged during the authentic movement when I found myself in child’s pose. I used this pose at home when I try to calm down and get ready to sleep. Finding myself in this position, I became tired and losing conscious control of my body. After realizing this sensation, I made myself move more to stay engaged with the activity.

During the flocking exercise, I felt overwhelmed by the amount of activity happening at the same time. When there were three groups moving together, it was fun to be in the small group; however, I found myself being taken out of the fun of the movement and started to worry about the safety of the people in the group. With three groups possibly moving backwards and forwards and doing various, possibly fast, movements, I was concerned someone would get punched, kicked, or pushed over and stepped on. When in the leader position, I became concerned that I did not have control over so many other groups but felt responsible for my group if something went wrong. I was overwhelmed by the stimulus of many people trying to move differently in such a tight space.

## Conclusion

The DmT experiential during my CAT course was truly impactful. The exercises granted me the ability to explore different physiological sensations as well as emotional responses. While there were some moments where I felt under engaged and overwhelmed, the overall experience was certainly memorable, and I can see the benefits of DmT. I could see myself consulting a Dance/Movement Therapist in the future when contemplating Drama Therapy techniques.
