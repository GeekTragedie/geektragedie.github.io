<p class="paper-title"><strong>Population Considerations</strong></p>

When working with various populations in a healthcare facility, there are multiple things one should consider. With different populations, there are different safety matter to consider and accessibility strategies to think about. There are also different invitation strategies that may be considered when working with several diverse population. This paper explores these different strategies when specifically looking at the following populations: families, children, adolescents, youth in juvenile detention centers, college students, educators, veterans, caregivers, people in clinic waiting rooms, and people in ICU waiting rooms.

Overall, there are some safety considerations that should be made when working with any population. Whenever doing a project with a population, one should count the materials, especially any sharp or toxic materials. Some sharp materials may include pencils, pens, paint brushes, or scissors. Some toxic materials may include paints, markers, or glue. It is always important to know what your materials are made out of. A facilitator does not want to be responsible for materials being stolen or what the participants might do with the materials after they have taken them.

With any population, a facilitator should be considerate if there are any handicap participants. Any project should be adaptable to any physically handicap participants. It is also important to be able to accommodate to any possible mental health issue or learning or developmental disability. Keeping away from any politically charged projects is also a good idea to prevent any negative feelings or behaviors.

## Families

When working with a family, there are certain considerations that should be made. Every family has a different dynamic and it is important to attempt to understand and accept this dynamic as the project progresses. While the facilitator is in charge of the project, there may be an authority figure in the family that the members may need to follow. Different members of the family may be more comfortable or less comfortable with different members. Some members may feel as though different members of the family will judge them. As the projects continue, these are some items to keep in mind. There are also probably family inside jokes or traditions that may be missed by the facilitator. Each family member also has their own personality type and different members of the family may know how to deal with this person instead of the facilitator. While the facilitator can attempt to engage different members of the family, the family members themselves might be beneficial in helping with this process.

When working with a family, the facilitator will be working with a variety of age groups. It is important for the facilitator to make all safety considerations for all of the ages participating in the activity. Due to a vast age group, it is important to pick a project that might be intriguing to all members of the family at any age. During a project, the facilitator might have to gage whether or every member of the family is fully invested in the project at hand.

## Children

Children can be defined as any child that is between birth and adolescence, normally considered teenage years.

### Invitation

While working with children, there are different invitation, safety, and accommodation, strategies that should be considered. When inviting children to participate, a facilitator should be friendly and not overbearing. They should not come off as a stranger because children are taught to keep away from these people. Keeping a positive attitude and a smile is welcoming and will make the children feel more open to working with someone. It is also important to have the permission of the children’s guardians. Children usually look toward their guardians for approval to participate in an activity, so having this consent.

### Safety and Accommodations

Safety and accommodation that should also be thought through when working with children. When using sharp objects, it is important to either help the children with these objects or keep a very close eye on the children that are using the sharp objects. It is also important to keep an eye on any materials that can be considered toxic if ingested. Children like to put things in their mouths, which can be dangerous when working with art materials, such as glue or paint. It is also important to consider safety when the children are moving around. Children can have very high energy, so having control of the group so that the children do not lose control is important. When a group of children get out of control, they can potentially harm themselves or others.

An accommodation that should certainly be considered is the level of education of the children being worked with. It is more difficult for children in lower levels to read, so when working on a poetry or literature project with this group, it may be difficult for them to understand certain words. It is also important to consider attention span with children. Children can be jittery and not want to sit still. This manner makes it difficult to do longer contemplative or slower movement projects with this population as well.

It is also important to consider the group dynamic when working with children. Some children may bully other children and some of the children might have cliques. These are some important for the facilitator to watch as they are working with this population.

## Adolescents

For a child to move into adolescence, it depends on their own puberty changes. However, many people consider adolescents to be people in their teenage years, so between the ages of thirteen and nineteen.

### Invitations

When approaching adolescents, it is important to understand that there might be some resistance coming from this population to do anything that is creative, unless you are working specifically with a creative population within this population. This population is going through a phase where they believe that the opinion of their peers is more important than anyone else. They may feel as though participating in an arts project is unpopular. They may feel as though they are being judged for their participation and the work they are creating. They may feel embarrassed to participate in a theatre game because they are worried of being made fun of for their abilities, whether they are good or bad. So, when picking a project with this population, it is important to choose a project that can ease the group into doing the project. The project should appear non-threatening.

### Safety and Accommodations

When working with the adolescent population, it is important to be careful with all materials being used. Be sure to count the materials so nothing is stolen. When working with this population it is also important to be mindful of possible bullying. If someone makes fun of a way someone is doing something or what someone is making, it can be very damaging to that person at this stage in their life. It is also important to make sure that all the content the adolescents are using is appropriate. There are certainly times when this population may have distasteful humor or content, so make sure to reinforce being appropriate so that all are comfortable during the project.

Many adolescents like competition, but this competition may have high stakes on an individual’s social standing. While competition is good, it is important to ensure that there are no hurt feelings at all, this applies to the game Captain’s Coming! in the projects I have selected for my final.

## JDC Youth

A juvenile detention center (JDC) is a secure place for youths under the age of eighteen that have been sentenced to be detained on a short-term basis due to illegal activity. This facility is similar to a prison or jail for adults.

### Invitations

More than likely when working with youths in a JDC, they either signed up for the project or they are forced to participate in the project. However, it is shown that these adolescents are more open to trying new things because they are not offered a variety of different opportunities. With this idea in mind, the adolescents are more willing to try creative projects. There are leaders within this population, though, and if the leaders are willing and open to attempting the project, then the rest of the group is more willing to follow.

The arts will more than likely be a new idea to youths in JDCs, so easing them into the art will be most beneficial. Due to the fact that they are adolescents, choosing a project that seems less threatening should also be applied to this population. Most of the considerations made with adolescents should also apply to the JDC population.

### Safety and Accommodations

With the JDC being a lock down facility, there are a lot of safety and accommodation considerations that should be made. It is crucial in this facility to count all of the materials brought in. When preparing materials, it may be beneficial to give a staff member a list of materials you plan to use and ask them permission to use these materials. The facility might also insist on using their own materials for projects, such as pencils and erasers, because they have certain rules and regulations regarding these materials.

When accommodating to this population, a facilitator must accommodate to the facility and follow their rules. It is important to know which adolescents are on separation, meaning they cannot be by each other, and which have blue shirts, meaning they are unable to talk. There is also a rule that adolescents are unable to touch each other or staff members. In the facility, the adolescents are unable to swear or talk about gang affiliations. With all of these rules that need to be followed, it is also important to ensure that the youths voices are not silenced. When adolescents are put into a JDC, they are locked away from the world. Most of the time, they are unable to speak their mind. So, doing arts activities is important for the adolescents because it gives them the ability to express themselves. It is also important that the facilitator ensures that all of the youths are being inclusive. There is also a mixture of racial diversity within this population, so when being inclusive, it is also important to keep this in mind.

## College Students

### Invitations

When working with the college student population, it is important to keep in mind that a lot of students will not approach the project, the project may have to go to the students. Many college students have a specific schedule and a certain place they like to spend their time. Due to this fact, having a project that can be mobile and approach college students is good. It is also important to make the project appear short, because time is very important to college students. They do not want to seem as though they are wasting their time.

### Safety and Accommodations

Time is a crucial accommodation consideration with this population. A facilitator needs to know how long the project will take and try to keep the project short. If the project takes more than thirty minutes, it is important for the student to be informed of this fact. It is also imperative to know what is happening at that school during the time you are doing your project; is it midterms, finals week, before or after a break? These factors will affect whether the students will or will not accept doing the project when approached. Many students can be more or less tired depending what time it is in the semester.

When choosing a project, keeping away from food projects might be a good idea. Many college students are very mindful of money and food. Doing a project with food may seem like a waste to this population. Many college students are able to use most other materials and a facilitator should simply keep the overall safety concerns for this population.

## Educators

Educators can be defined as any person who teaches another group of people. Some people that fall under this category are school teachers, professors, or coaches.

### Invitations

When inviting educators to do arts projects, it may be easiest do approach them during a staff meeting or during a teachers’ in-service day. A facilitator can also talk with a higher supervisor to set up a time that works for all educators. Teachers are very busy with students, homework, emails, and lesson plans. Any project facilitators perform with this population should be explained with its importance or else it may stress out teachers more and they may feel as though they are wasting their time.

### Safety and Accommodations

There are not very many safety considerations with this population on top of the general concerns for all population. Educators are normally in charge of the safety of many others. Some important ideas to keep in mind when working with educators is that teachers can be rather cliquey. There is a certain power dynamic that is formed in schools and all schools are different. Finding this power dynamic early-on in the process may be most beneficial. Due to the fact that many schools like teachers to collaborate, splitting up these groups may help break up some of the cliquey atmosphere. The largest accommodation for this population, however, is time. Educators have a lot to do and very rarely receive a break throughout their day; while this art project is supposed to be a break, not all participants may view it in this light. They may view it as an obstacle keeping them from the stack of papers on their desk or the daunting week ahead that still needs to be planned.

## Veterans

The term “veteran” means a person that has spent a long period of time doing a particular occupation. However, for this paper, the definition of veteran being used is a person who has served in the military for a long period of time.

### Invitations

When approaching to work with veterans, it is important to be professional. This population has spent a larger portion of their life in uniform and in a military setting, so professionalism is important to them. It is also important for this population to understand what you are saying and explaining. The facilitator will want to explain the project so that there is little vague area. They should also be able to answer any questions they are asked directly.

### Safety and Accommodations

A safety consideration that is important in this population is to stay away from sudden or loud noises. Many veterans have experienced war and may have some form of post traumatic stress disorder that can be triggered easily through loud noises. Sudden noises can also trigger these episodes from the participant being startled.

When accommodating to this population, it is good to keep in mind that this population has a high consideration for their country. Honor and organization will be important to them because of their military background. So, when facilitating, be sure to accommodate to these needs. Another accommodation that may need to be met is the fact that a large amount of this population may be physically handicap. All projects with this population will, more than likely, have to include the handicap population.

## Caregivers

Caregivers can be defined as anyone who is providing care to another person. This population can include healthcare staff, family, or friends taking care of those needing medical assistance.

### Invitations

When considering inviting caregivers to partake in an arts activity, it is important to know when the caregivers take breaks. A facilitator does not want to invite a caregiver to do an arts activity while the caregiver is busy doing their job to help those that they care about. With this in mind, it is also important to be careful of how much time a project takes. Due to the fact that the caregiver does need to return to someone to provide care, a shorter project should suffice. When particularly wanting to do projects for healthcare staff, a facilitator can talk to a supervisor about a good time to do an arts activity. Staff meetings could possibly be a good time to perform an arts activity and it could also help create a community within staff at the healthcare facility.

### Safety and Accommodations

When working with caregivers, it is important to take specific health safety concerns. It is important for all participants to wash hands before and after doing projects. All of the materials should be cleaned after one person uses them if materials are shared. These tasks are preventative measures to attempt to not pass germs. It is also important to consider the fumes coming from the materials used because some of the patients that the caregivers may go see might not be able to smell or take in the fumes that are in the caregivers’ clothing.

## Clinic Waiting Rooms

This population title speaks for itself; it is people waiting in a clinic waiting room. These people are usually in the clinic waiting room for two reasons: one, they are waiting to be seen by a medical professional; two, they are waiting while a person they know is being seen by a medical professional.

### Invitations

When doing projects in a clinic waiting room, it is important to be mindful of space. People waiting usually prefer to keep away from other people in a waiting room. Depending on what they are in the waiting room for, they could also feel embarrassed for having to go to the clinic. They also might be feeling anxiety about having to either see a professional or hear how another person is doing. With these ideas in mind, being calm and friendly is also important because a facilitator does not know why the people are waiting. Considering doing a shorter project or one that can easily be taken might be helpful because the person waiting could leave at any moment.

### Safety and Accommodations

In waiting rooms, it is important to consider all the times that germs can be passed from one person to the next, especially if someone is coming in for an illness that can be spread. When doing the project, it is important to sanitize all of the materials and / or tools being used in between uses. It is also important for the facilitator to watch their own health, so they may benefit from wearing gloves or consciously making certain safety precautions.  When being in a waiting room, it is also important to be mindful of the person’s privacy and speak softly so that others around do not hear everything you are saying.

## ICU Waiting Rooms

Those who usually wait in intensive care unit (ICU) waiting rooms are not patients. These people are waiting for someone they care about to, hopefully, recover. These people can also be waiting to hear test results from a healthcare professional to update them on how their loved one is doing.

### Invitations

When approaching people in an ICU waiting room, it is important to be calm and friendly, but not too overly happy. A facilitator does not know why these people are waiting, so being as approachable is important. Normally the people waiting are not there because they want to be, so being excited or full of energy is probably not what they are looking for at that point in time. When choosing a project, it is important for the project to be mobile, so the participants can take it on the go with them if needed. The participants might need to leave at any given time, dependent upon their loved one. The project should also take a little less concentration because the minds of those in an ICU waiting room may not particularly stay focused on the project.

### Safety and Accommodations

When working with people in an ICU waiting room, the same safety precautions should be used as used in clinical waiting rooms. One additional safety concern that should be taken seriously, however, is the use of fumes. When patients are in an ICU, those around them should be very careful of everything that is being taken into the room, including scents.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, while there are overarching concerns that should be taken when considering safety, most populations are treated differently. Before working with populations, a facilitator should consider the different invitational strategies they will use. Safety and accommodations may be different when working with various populations as well. A lot of the times, before working with a population, it is always best to talk to a supervisor and ask if there are any particular things that should be considered before starting a project. One of the most important things that should be remembered is to let go and have fun.
