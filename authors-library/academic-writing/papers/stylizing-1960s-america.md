<p class="paper-title"><strong>Stylizing the 1960s</strong></p>

The purpose of this paper is to research the styles in America during the 1960s and apply them to an ambiguous scene. While there were many diverse groups during the 1960s, this paper specifically researches three specific sections of people during this time: one, upper and middle class; two, the LGBT+ movement; and three, the counterculture class. When defining the upper and middle class, this paper discusses legislation and what many of the social norms are for those in higher power in the government. It looks at what the presidents were working towards and where effort was put. This paper also details many of the experiences of the LGBT+ people of this time. Another look into this paper also discusses the reactions from the wars and the changes coming from the counterculture into making this world a better place.  Each area is looked at from nine different lenses from Robert Barton’s (1993) book, *Style for Actors*: time, space, place, values, structure, beauty, sex, recreation, sight, and sound (p. 15). By looking at the 1960s through Barton’s framework, one can gain information regarding the style of the period and formulate an ambiguous scene using the research.

## Time

Time looks at when the scene takes place and how pace of those in that period. It also conceptualizes how the people at this time perceive time passing, fast or slow. It covers how people record time and how they see rhythm. It also looks at the attention span of the people at this time and whether or not people appreciate or fear aging (Barton, 1993, p. 16). For each section, the point in time discussed in the 1960s.

### Upper and Middle Class

For the upper and middle class, time seemed to be moving at a more fast pace. Legislation was attempting to pass laws to benefit people quickly and find solutions to poverty issues (Marx, 2011). John F. Kennedy was assassinated, which happened at a fast pass and people’s reaction were drastic (Ember, 2012). After his death, “many felt that their hopes died” with JFK (Ember, 2012). Johnson picked up quickly and attempted to do right by the people during his time in office (Marx, 2011). He worked on passing multiple legislation while the country was still at war in Vietnam (Morgan, 2010, p. 75). In attempts to fix social problems, Johnson also declared a “war on poverty” (Patterson, 1981, p. 99) and started Great Society programs (Marx, 2011). The wars also made time go by faster. People were focused on the future, hoping to solve the problems of their day. There were many big ideas during the 1960s and people were fighting to their rights.  Women’s movements rose as women attempted to gain more rights (Morgan, 2010, p. 82) and the Civil Rights Movement also gained ground.

### LGBT+

In a time of drastic social change and revolution for many marginalized groups, time seemed to move quickly for the LGBT+ community. The 1950’s brought about the “Lavender Scare”, a period when individuals thought to be LGBT+ were removed from their places of work (Sherouse 2014), which continued well into the 1960’s. In fact, “many politicians, journalists, and citizens thought that [lesbians and gay men] posed more of a threat to national security than Communists” (Sherouse 2014). In a professional sense, the LGBT+ community constantly had to be on their guard to keep their own best interests in sight, and the long term goals of their livelihoods were the norm in favor of moment to moment goals. The generation gap between the older and younger generations seemed to be more drastic than it had in previous decades, with the new, younger generation viewing generations before them as complacent and out-of-date. This new generation thrived on social and political change, and the LGBT+ community in particular recognized that change would not take place if it was not orchestrated on their terms.

### Counterculture

For the counterculture class, too many things were happening, and they just wanted peace also calling some of these people “hippies.” Like mentioned above, the death of JFK caused a major wave of sadness amongst Americans as well as the war. In “Towards a New Political Economy of Youth,” it is stated, “The roots of the political-economy-of youth perspective can be traced to the neo-Marxist attempts to account for the youth movements and countercultures of the 1960s,” showing how in the 60s younger people did not want to just sit around as their time went by (Cote, 2014). They wanted to take the time they had and make a change with it. Some chose to do so with protests, others went to festivals and spent time with their loved ones, or some chose a different route and chose to use drugs such as marijuana, and psychedelic drugs like LSD (being a very popular choice).

## Space

In a scene, space looks at how public or private people are. It defines how close people like to be to one another and how big they consider their personal space to be. It shows how flexible their schedules are. It shows how someone may react to their privacy being invaded or their area being obstructed. This includes how they move in the space as well, including gestures they may make (Barton, 1993, p. 16).

### Upper and Middle Class

For the upper and middle class, space was greatly defined. People felt ownership over the material items that they purchased. They believed that if they bought it, it belonged to them. They were more materialistic, causing their personal space to be a little further away. Many people during this time wanted to help others, such as the Vietnam war and war against poverty. Their space is approximately an arm’s length: small enough to offer a helping hand, but big enough to not be considered close. As for movement, many motions are relatively mechanical and very planned out. If there is an idea, it is thought through and planned in order for that idea to become a reality. The routines of those in the middle class were simple: the woman mostly stayed at home and did house work while the man went to work and came home (Henderson, n.d.). While the women’s rights movement started working more during the 1960s, this information enhances the mechanical and organized idea that was portrayed by a large population of people.

### LGBT+

There was a very clear separation between public and private space for the LGBT+ community. In this post-Red Scare society, it was in many ways unsafe for a queer individual to be out (particularly in the workplace, as detailed above). However, with this growing visibility came a need for a space of their own. For example, when same sex intercourse was outlawed in New York City in the 1960’s, the Stonewall Inn became a safe space for many LGBT+ individuals who had nowhere else (Stonewall riots, n.d.). When police harassed and arrested a number of patrons on June 28, 1969, the LGBT+ individuals present seemed to have reached their breaking point. The police had invaded a space that was sacred and precious to them, and defiling this space caused them to push back and demand respect.

### Counterculture

The big thing that comes to mind when discussing space with this group of people is that everyone there, whether it is at a festival, concert, protest, park, is there to share something in common with the next person and trying to better their worlds and get along. They are already different from those around them, so having a similarity was great to these groups of people.

## Place

Place in a scene looks at the physical nature of how the setting appears. It depends on where the scene takes place and what kind of weather this place experiences during the time the scene happens. It also looks at how characters interact with their surroundings and how they perceive it (Barton, 1993, p. 16).

### Upper and Middle Class

The upper and middle class’s space was very protected, urban, and invitational. They were relatively protective due to their materialism and wars. The upper class was made up “of about 1%-6% of America’s population” (Henderson, n.d.). The upper class was considered CEOs and executives that need to live in urban areas in order to make a living. The middle class also needed urban areas because they mostly lived in suburbs (Henderson, n.d.). The people looked towards a new place with open skies that looked down upon the dark world that they wanted to fix, such as the poverty. There were specific leaders during this time that lead different groups. Four of the specific leaders during the 1960s were Martin Luther King Jr., Malcolm X, John F. Kennedy, and Lyndon B. Johnson (Morgan, 2010). The other characters during this time period were more generalized: those in poverty or from racial minority groups (Morgan, 2010).

### LGBT+

A reality for many LGBT+ identifying individuals of this time was adaptability. In a country where they seemed to have targets on their backs, safety was a priority. Both metropolitan and rural areas were safe and unsafe for different reasons; a metropolitan area might have more visibility within the community and therefore more opportunities for socialization, but it might also have more opportunities for harassment. In a rural area, one might be able to draw less attention to oneself and therefore remain safely closeted when necessary, but it might also provide less opportunities to be “out” and expressive in one’s sexuality. To survive in a society that sought to displace them, the LGBT+ community learned to adapt and blend in almost anywhere, but not being able to be themselves was actorly and impersonal.

### Counterculture

With place, this group of people chose to most definitely be in places where they would be see and would be heard. This new wave was not something that was kept under wraps and not talked about, if anything the point was to get people to talk and bring awareness to the issues at hand. Issues that needed to be mentioned and talked about. The Counterculture class was very vocal. Now when it came to places such as the festivals, those were all about “open love” as they called it. Affection was public, someone’s feelings for another person could be out in the open and there would be no punishment from it.

## Values

This section of a scene looks at what beliefs are shared by people during this time period. It discusses traditions that many people have and how people create communities and relationships. It discusses those who may be role models during the time and what holds people’s attention. This section also looks at how emotions are expressed during this period and possibly who expresses those feelings most (Barton, 1993, p. 16).

### Upper and Middle Class

The values of the upper and middle class seemed to be to help those in poverty. The government hired researchers to find out what was causing the poverty rate to increase in the United States and find solutions to the poverty issue (Patterson, 1981). This effort enlisted people into the Great Society, starting programs such as “Medicare, Medicaid, legal aid, Head Start, [and] student financial aid” (Marx, 2011). There was also a lot of people were involved in different movements, such as the Civil Rights Movement, or Women’s Rights Movement. Many believed that hope died with JFK’s assassination and many continued to believe this through Johnson’s presidency. However, helping others and sharing the love was greatly accepted. There were many groups that disapproved of the war, however, and that caused tension between groups.

### LGBT+

The LGBT+ community is a community that was, and continues to be, a close-knit group that sought to protect its own. The Stonewall Riots of 1969 were incredibly important, acting as a catalyst for the contemporary gay rights movement we are familiar with today. By banding together to fight against the harassment and injustices they experienced from police, and actually seeing results, the LGBT+ community was inspired to see “value in uniting behind a common cause” (Stonewall riots, n.d.).

### Counterculture

Values were probably the most important part of this group. In “The ‘New Age’ for Libraries,” there is talk about this group during the 60s. When mentioning of the Counterculture group it said the ultimate goal was to “produce a New Age consisting of newly evolved humanity coexisting in ecological harmony with a renewed planet Earth” (Dowd, 1989). The notion of alone calling this a New Age shows awareness of the wrongs from the past and wanting to change where they lived in not something that would only apply to the Counterculture, but to everyone.  In the same article it was stated that a value that was wanted to be achieved was to have “humankind be unified, possessing a common religion and common goals of peace, prosperity, and personal fulfillment” (Dowd, 1989).

## Structure

Structure consists of the organization of power structure in the scene, looking at who leads and who follows them. It looks at the daily lives of those in the 1960s and how they have set up their rules and regulations. It also discusses different groups that have been created and who holds the most power (Barton, 1993, p. 16).

### Upper and Middle Class

The structure of the upper and middle class tended to be that the presidents lead, and those who support him most follow. Many follow, even if they do not support him. There were many that disliked Johnson’s choice to continue the Vietnam war, however (Marx, 2011). The other organizations were structured differently. Each movement had their own leader and followers. Families in the upper and middle class were lead by the males in the household and women started getting jobs while still taking care of household duties (Henderson, n.d., Morgan, 2010). There started to be more value in research and education regarding poverty and it held a large amount of value for political leaders. Many groups were created based on social interest, class, and household income. “‘The Other America” consisted “between 40,000,000 and 50,000,000 relatively invisible poor people, the unskilled workers, the migrant farm workers, minorities, people for whom work was sporadic, demeaning, and demoralizing” (Marx, 2011). This group was a large one that was created based on income and lead to the increase in interest in poverty. Many of the upper class, however, were made of CEOs, businessmen, and politicians.

### LGBT+

As previously mentioned, the most outspoken members of the LGBT+ community seemed to generally be white, gay men. Even today, we see very little representation from members of the LGBT+ community who are not gay, white men. People of color, especially LGBT+ people of color, were pushed down and rarely given any opportunity to speak on their truths and experiences. Authority from heterosexual, cisgender lawmakers and enforcers had been absolute, but LGBT+ had begun to fight back to change the status quo. Riots and protests were the only ways that seem to get any attention, and the Stonewall riots kicked off the LGBT+ movement we know today.

### Counterculture

The interesting part about the structure dealing with the Counterculture group is that this group was created by young people. They were the ones who knew there needed to be a change in society and took matters into their own hands knowing that they were the future. Being big into music, they would turn to their favorite singers and bands and see what they were up to and try to follow some of their footsteps (again going back to drug use). The nice thing about this group is that their structure was based on love whether that meant with each other or within themselves. They would all get together and do these protests as a cohesive group that would stand united until the end.

## Beauty

The beauty section discusses what fashion was happening during the 1960s that was popular. It also discusses who people follow as those ideal looks. It discusses how fitness plays a part in body figure and what body figures are considered attractive (Barton, 1993, p. 16).

### Upper and Middle Class

The look of the upper and middle class were pristine. They liked to keep presentable to others and professional. One of the fashion go-to people were Mrs. Kennedy followed by the model Twiggy. A modern-day comparison to the body figured that was wanted during the 1960s is Joan Holloway (Foley, 2012). Many women were “prized for their ample bosoms and curvaceous frames” (Foley, 2012). Fitness was looked at as empowering to women to be more fit (Foley, 2012).

### LGBT+

The desired look for the LGBT+ community during this time varied. The Mattachine Society, a newly founded LGBT+ organization, the members were generally “white, middle-class, and politically moderate (despite its radical roots), encouraging its members to wear gender-conforming attire and avoid overt displays of sexuality and non-gender-conforming mannerisms, attitudes that put them on the more conservative end of the political spectrum by the mid-1960s” (Sherouse, 2014). On the opposite end of this spectrum were drag performers, individuals who dressed as what was considered “normal” for the sex opposite from their own for entertainment purposes. The 1960’s saw an explosion in popularity in both female and male impersonators as entertainment, drag balls, and a general rejection of gender norms in fashion (Boyer, 2016).

### Counterculture

The one word that would fit with fashion within the Counterculture group: flowy. Hair was natural and loose, clothing articles that included tie-dye, psychedelic patterns, the use of “flower power,” were all very common. Thanks to the Beatles, people also began wearing bell-bottom pants and paisley prints.

## Sex

This section discusses what the sexual stereotypes were of the 1960s. It talks about what dating was like and whether or not others deviated from the social norm (Barton, 1993, p. 16).

### Upper and Middle Class

The upper and middle class’s sex life was still dominated by the men. However, women’s outfits were more closed off and women were more conservative about their bodies. They wanted to look more professional to be considered in the business world (Morgan, 2010). The majority were heterosexual and did not allow for much deviation; it was not until later where sexual minorities were allowed to come out. The Civil Rights Movement, however, helped fuel the gay rights movement later (Morgan, 2010).

### LGBT+

Sexuality is what differentiates this group from the rest of society. The heterosexual “majority” saw this nonconforming sexuality as unnecessary and deviant, and outlawed sodomy, which made it illegal for gay men specifically to have sex (Stonewall riots, n.d.). Seduction and courtship generally, then, happened privately. As same sex couples cannot procreate, sex between them was purely for pleasure. Perhaps for this reason, sexual intercourse was frequent and indiscriminate (Boyer, 2016).

### Counterculture

More or less, sex was a natural thing that would happen between people who loved one another or just wanted to do it for fun and pleasure. Hiding this human instinct was uncommon and could be talked about. Affection was very important and the idea of two bodies coming together as one was a beautiful moment for them (Echols & Farber, 2014).

## Recreation

Recreation during the 1960s is what the people during this period did for fun. It talks about special occasions that happened and who participated in them. It also discusses different hobbies that people had during the time and what people were eating (Barton, 1993, p. 16).

### Upper and Middle Class

Some recreational activities enjoyed by the upper and middle class depended on the age of the person. Children enjoyed playing with Barbies dolls and G.I. Joe figures. Slot cars also became an interest during this time period for children and younger adults (Rielly, 2003). Young adults became more interested in chess as well. Adults found board games for themselves and started to watch Jeopardy on television. Many were interested in football and those in the upper class enjoyed spending their time sailing.

### LGBT+

In places where being “out” was not safe during the daytime, nightlife was some of the most important for the LGBT+ community. Similar to the countercultural movement, recreational drugs such as marijuana and LSD were popular with LGBT+ individuals. The growing popularity of drag shows and gay specific bars gave the queer community opportunities they had never had before to have fun and be themselves, and a strong camaraderie developed.

### Counterculture

Due to this time being open about using drugs was a norm, this was something people did for fun. Using LSD and marijuana were the primary drugs during the 60s. Going to festivals were also a big part of the Counterculture environment. Along with these festivals, this group would listen to different type of music like psychedelic music, American folk music, surf rock, and girl groups. On a completely different note, this group was very into doing protests of the things they wanted to see change. Being activists was most definitely a way of life for the Counterculture.

## Sight

The sight section discusses what type of shapes may have been seen during the 1960s. It discusses colors, lights, and movements. This section also looks at material objects that may have been seen during this time period (Barton, 1993, p. 16).

### Upper and Middle Class

The upper and middle class seemed to keep straight lined shapes. Their habits were very organized, and their fashion seemed to be very straight. Their minds were made up on what they wanted to follow and kept with that idea going straight forward. More of the colors for those of the upper and middle class were duller colors, salmon, lighter blue, and green. There was a brightness to this time period as the colors were duller. The light seemed to brighten things up, especially considering their high expectations for the future.

### LGBT+

This group pushed, and continues to push, against cultural gender constructs by dressing in ways associated with the opposite gender. Transgender individuals had recently been recognized in the 1960’s, and the idea of identifying as a gender rather than the biological sex one was born into challenges the normal cultural ideas of gender and sex up to this point. Values such as monogamy and abstinence are not always necessarily valued in the LGBT+ community. For the LGBT+ community, the main goal was change to the status quo that pushed them down and denied them a voice.

### Counterculture

Starting off with this group also known as “The Hippies” sight was a lot of fun! The colors in this group were super colorful having patterns of things that would look “trippy.” There would be different variations of light colors that would go with each other and bolds with bolds, but depending on the person, there could be a mix of trying to match lights and darks. Depending on what this group was doing, the perfect elements to describe the sight of Counterculture would be Air, Water, and Earth. No fire. That was not needed with them. In terms of shapes, straight lines were a big no-no. It was all about the wave of a thing and the naturality of how it came about. Psychedelic looking things were also a big part of this, going back to the word, “trippy.”

## Rehearsal and Performance Choices

After the discussion of the previous topics, each area has their own uniqueness in how they can be applied to a scene. The following applies the different sections in the 1960s to a scene and how it may affect the way a scene is performed. Please refer to Appendix A for the script discussed during this section of the paper.

### Upper and Middle Class

An echo of change and hope are seen throughout the upper and middle class, especially when it comes to helping those of a lower class. When implementing the upper and middle class into our scene, we used the upper class to create one of the characters to contrast the other character. We also wanted to use the contrast between characters to show the motivation of change that is apparent throughout the 1960s. The group was also used because there was a costume available to us from this group. The Kennedys were also a large influence on Americans during this time period. They gave the idea of hope that people continued to strive for during the decade. Their influence is brought out through the costumes and hope in the performance.

### LGBT+

In my research, what stood out to me more than anything was the idea of change among all social groups. This was a period of time when many Americans were unhappy with the way things were and wanted to make things better for themselves. As someone who falls on the LGBT+ spectrum, I was particularly interested in the experiences of non-heterosexual people of this time period.

## Conclusion

The 1960s have various movements and groups that have different influences. We wanted to enhance the idea of change and we did this through combining two different groups during the time period. We brought in two different groups to show the vast experiences of people during the period and to show the interactions between groups. We brought the use of drugs into the scene and contrast mannerisms and attitudes to enhance the scene. Overall, the keys to this world were the ideas of change and hope along with the Kennedy family. These groups helped influence the scene choices and emphasis.
