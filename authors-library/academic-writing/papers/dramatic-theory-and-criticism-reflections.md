### Reflection 4 (12 September 2017)

**Learned**

- Poet comes from the Greek word *Poiein,* which means *to make* (Sidney 120).
- Poets are better than nature because God made humans in his own image, while nature is only second to God (Sidney 120-121).
- While philosophers teach to the taught, poets teach to the masses (Sidney 122).

**Connection**

- When Sidney says, “little reason hath any man to say that men learn evil by seeing it so set out,” it reminds me of people saying that violent video games are evil and teaching people violence (Sidney 122).
- When Sidney talking about having delight, but not having laughter, I connected it to improvisation when we talk about how scenes can be good and entertaining, but not necessarily funny.

**Questions**

- What are the two lists listing (Sidney 121, 123)?

### Reflection 5 (19 September 2017)

**Learned**

- Schiller’s idea of the stage being a mirror of life give more power to the fact that theatre is reflecting the life that is happening back at society (251).
- Schopenhauer says you can “neglect the unity of time and place” as long as you keep unity of action (295).
- Greek tragedy items can still be used and effective in modern tragedies (Hegel).

**Connection**

- I connected my life to Schiller’s line stating “chance and design have equal sway over life” because I am a planner, but sometimes life throws you curveballs that you are not at all prepared for.
- Schopenhauer talks about circumstances, fate, and events that make characters who do actions in plays (292) and I connected it to the idea of given circumstances.

**Questions**

- Does Schopenhauer have a bright side to tragedies?

### Reflection 6 (26 September 2017)

**Learned**

- The Greek chorus goes against Naturalism, which means that this period really helped do away with the chorus because it was too presentational and Naturalists went for representational.
- Some consider the action in a play to be seen “only as a vision” while the chorus is seen as the reality (Nietzsche 346).
- Hegel believes that tragedies need four elements: one, chorus; two, conflict between protagonists and antagonists; three, guilt or innocence; four, resolution (316-321).

**Connection**

- When Nietzsche talked about the dramatic proto-phenomenon and “seeing yourself transformed before your own eyes… as if you had actually taken possession of another body and another character” (345) I thought about Stanislavsky going against this idea when he discusses acting in Russia.
- I connected Hegel’s idea that characters “are the kind of men they are” (325) to psychology of personality and to Darwin and Freud and the environment that people grow up in that shapes personality.

**Question**

- Nietzsche talks a lot about poets and the chorus and how we should not turn away from the chorus convention, but what if the chorus doesn’t reach the audience anymore, like we talked about in our last class?
- What is considered a modern definition of a satyr and how did we go away from the Greek idea of satyr to the way we use it to describe a comedy today (Nietzsche 343)?

### Reflection 7 (3 October 2017)

**Learned**

- Zola states “that everything is interdependent in the theatre” (365).
- Even though a man may have a lower class standing, he can be considered superior to another women, just because he is a man (Strindberg 376).
- Getting rid of intermissions can possibly help audiences because “our dwindling capacity for accepting illusion is possibly further disturbed by intermissions” (Strindberg 377).

**Connection**

- I connected Zola’s idea of language changing and becoming more simple to our conversation in class regarding whether or not heightened language is still important to our society due to lack of usage in this type of language (Zola 366).
- Strindberg’s discussion of improvisation possibly being “the beginning of a fertile new art form, something worthy of the name creative” it kind of reminds me of how difficult it can be sometimes to get theatre people to do improv, and I don’t quite understand why (378).

**Question**

- What is the use of “open suggestion” in a theatre setting (Strindberg 374)?
- Why is ballet considered a traditional theatre art form?

### Reflection (26 October 2017)

**Learned**

- Language is both important and not important because it is a society structure that can possibly prevent us from the true actions of nature; however, we will never know because we are raised with signs (Belsey).
- Parodies only work if one knows what the parody is based on and it also reinforces the idea of which it is a parody to (Hutcheon 126).
- The performance of a reality check and its effect on a wide audience (Elam 173).

**Connection**

- Belsey discusses how we usually come across a term before an idea is discovered and this made me think of my brother the other day looking in the fridge and asking if there was a term for looking in the fridge and not finding anything, but coming back to it a minute later because there is no food anywhere else (52).
- Idea of revealing and ‘denaturalizing’ what seemed ‘natural’ in society and culture reminded me of Brecht and defamiliarization (Hutcheon 126).

**Question**

- What is postmodernism?

### Reflection (2 November 2017)

**Learned**

- I learned the difference between “*inside* representation” and “*outside* representation” (Donkin 327).
- I learned that most gender theory came out of feminist theory.
- A lot of the reasons behind gender inequality is due to the human’s mind thinking in binary.

**Connection**

- When Ellen Donkin discusses feminism and woman having to do “establish herself as ‘female subject’” and that this idea “was simply a gritty determination to survive” (326) it reminded me of *Good Person of Szechwan*.
- Hall states “that one’s biology, body and bodily functioning are not inherently meaningful; they are interpreted and inscribed upon by society and social value systems” and this idea reminded me of Darwin and Freud’s idea of our surroundings helping shape who we are as we grow up (111).

**Question**

- How do we change a society that thinks in binary to not think in that way?

### Reflection (9 November 2017)

**Learned**

- The feminist groups have shifted “from equality to difference” (Hekman 99).
- There are very many different approaches to feminism and each have their own goal and idea of where oppression and/or subordination has come from.
- Freud’s ideas regarding sexuality isn’t how I had originally taken it, which completely changes the way that the Oedipus complex can be understood (Lapsley 75-6).
- Humans are constantly searching for what is lacking because things never really live up -to our fantasized expectations (Lapsley 81-2).

**Connection**

- When Hekman says “that the purpose of women’s existence is to satisfy the sexual desires of men,” it reminded me of our discussion of how this hasn’t always been the case because it only started happening when men told women to cover up their breasts.
- I connected Beauvoir’s statement in Hekman’s reading, “One is not born, but rather becomes, a women,” to the previous reading on gender when it discussed that gender is simply a construction, not biological.
- When Lapsley is discussing Lacan and talks in “terms of the subject’s self-perception” and talks about the subject’s perception of the world, it reminded me in Modern how we discussed the playwright that had the wife that fully believed he was cheating on him, and that was her reality, even though it was not true.

**Question**

- If there are differences between male and female moral decision making – what are these differences (Hekman 100-1).

- Theatre is made up of looking at the past to help mold the future (Roach 89).
- It is difficult to write my learning section of my 3-2-1 because in the readings, I seem to only comprehend the information that I have been taught in the past, so it makes the learning section more difficult to write because I seem to only catch what I know.

**Connection**

- I completely connected to the section that talked about soldiers learning rules because they want to avoid punishment (Goffman 63) and I also related this to psychology positive punishment.

**Question**

- What is “front” and what are the different parts of it, because the chapter only really talked about setting?
- While I thought I understood what restored behavior was, Carlson confuses me on page 74 when he discusses it; what is restored behavior?
- If all behavior is learned and repeated, then how can anything really be original? If everything on stage is learned behavior, how can there be a simulacra?
