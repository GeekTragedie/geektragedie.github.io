<p class="paper-title"><strong>Group Facilitation Proposal Paper Week 3</strong></p>

In Antioch University Seattle’s group counseling course, GeekTragedie and a colleague (authors) will co-facilitate a pseudo-group counseling session with their classmates. In preparation for this session, the co-facilitators chose their theoretical lens, ensured to consider the level of group development, and created goals they wish to accomplish while leading. The facilitation outline can be found in Appendix A of this document. The purpose of this essay is to consider and aid the facilitators, future counselors, in thinking about how to prepare for a group counseling session.

With the group having officially met only once, the co-facilitators believe the group is towards the end of the initial stage of group development (Corey et al., 2018). The theoretical lens with which the co-facilitators aim to utilize is a mindfulness-based person-centered approach. Throughout our group session, we would like to focus on bringing group members into the present moment, which Corey et al. (2018) would describe as a here-and-now focus in the group instead of a there-and-then focus. The facilitators plan to open and close the group with mindfulness-based interventions while the facilitation of group discussion will be held with a person-centered approach.

During the middle section of the group facilitators will focus on the group process. They will begin with an invitation for any reflections or reactions from last week’s group to include specifically reflections around some of the common themes [from the group’s first session]. The facilitators will also try to hold space for anyone who has not shared much, as well as link members who might have shared similar experiences. To focus on mindfulness during the session, the co-facilitators will try to observe group members’ body language throughout the facilitation process.

The co-facilitators have three goals for this group session. First, based on [GeekTragedie]’s goal for facilitation, she would like to make a drama therapy technique approachable and insightful for the rest of the group. To work towards this goal, she plans to lead a walking meditation that will flow into a partner mirroring exercise, found in Appendix B, to help group members check in with themselves and one other person. Through dyad work during the mirroring exercise, the facilitators hope to also increase group cohesion through this somatic-linking check in.

Second, due to the group still being in the initial stages of development, facilitators would like to continue increasing group cohesion. One of [the colleague]’s individual facilitation goals is to effectively link members and switch from one member to another. With a focus on linking members to one another, in addition to our focus on the here-and-now, the cohesion of the group is likely to grow stronger.

Thirdly, the facilitators aim to continue establishing and upholding group norms of confidentiality, safety, and authenticity. They hope that their opening activity will help encourage authenticity by first inviting group members to be present in their bodies, which can translate into more presence and bravery within the group process. Facilitators will remind group members of agreed upon rules that will be posted on the walls of the group room. Through asking about group members’ reactions or thoughts from last week’s session, facilitators will open the space for members to voice concerns regarding confidentiality and safety. Through concluding the session with a mindful body scan, facilitators hope to help move the group members safely from a vulnerable state to a state in which they are comfortable leaving the session.

## Appendix A

<p style="text-indent:0">Outline</p>

- Goals:
  - Making a drama therapy technique approachable and insightful for the rest of the group
  - Increase group cohesion because the group is still in the initial stages
  - Continuing to establish and uphold group norms
- Beginning
  - Walking meditation & partner mirroring (GeekTragedie)
  - Check in about activity/current feelings (GeekTragedie)
  - Recap of themes
    - Acknowledge former group members as facilitators (not discussing their stuff)
    - Group looks different this week (colleague)
  - Invite reflections from last week and/or thoughts about new group make-up (colleague)
- Middle (Both)
  - “Is anyone feeling anxious in the moment that would be willing to share?”
  - Summarizing
  - Notice body language
  - Linking
  - Can we make a little space for people who haven’t shared anything today?
- Ending
  - What might you be more aware of now as we close this session? (GeekTragedie)
    - Popcorn style - if you resonate with what someone said, you can go next
  - Body Scan & Grounding (colleague)
  - One word/Phrase of how you are feeling now (colleague)

## Appendix B

<p style="text-indent:0">Walking Meditation / Mirroring Exercise</p>

- Walk around the space
  - No particular pattern
  - Check-in with your body, notice how you are feeling
  - Tension? Move/stretch to release
  - Notice how you are breathing
  - How are you feeling in this moment?
- Speed
  - If you were to create a scale of your speed from 1-10, note what would you consider your current speed?
  - Invite to walk at a 3
  - 5
  - 8
  - 3
  - 5
- Open yourself up to the space
  - Take notice of things/people around you
  - Make eye contact with people
- Mirroring
  - As you make eye contact, find a partner
  - Your partner is going to be your mirror, as you move, your partner will move
  - Choose a person to lead first
  - Other person lead
  - Have the first person create a continuous movement that may express how you feel in this moment
  - Repeat a couple of times
  - Second person lead
  - Repeat
- Close
  - Thank everyone
  - Find a chair and create a circle
