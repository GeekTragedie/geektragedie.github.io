<p class="paper-title">Theatre, Form, Society</p>

“Drama tends to minister to the quality of society’s life” (Genna). While drama tends to society, this means that different societies have different forms of drama. Each form of drama then will affect their society differently based on the society itself. This idea is true for Greek and Roman theatre. Not only were the plays different in content and form, but they also had different effects on society. While the two society’s reins seem equally as far in the past, the two’s views of theatre were very different in multiple ways. Greek and Roman comedic theatre differ in form and content, but also were utilized differently within their own society’s culture and histories.

Old Greek comedies had particular forms and content that were followed. One of the prime examples of this type of comedy is Aristophanes’s play *Lysistrata*. Old Greek comedies primarily dealt with men that were of a lower class. While *Lysistrata’s* male characters were of a lower class, the primary low class shown is the women. Women during the Greek period were rarely allowed to participate or do anything. It is not even certain the women were allowed to attend large, religious festivals. With the lead role in this play being Lysistrata, the comedy automatically starts with a member of the lower class. The humor is primarily light, meaning that it can be understood by members of the lower class and does not have sophisticated humor. Humor used during Old Greek comedies also contained ridicule and bodily humor. These elements are certainly found in *Lysistrata* when the women dress the Commissioner like a woman and ridicule him. The bodily humor is also found by the male characters wearing giant phalluses to symbolize a huge erection because their wives were refusing to have sex with their husbands. The type of comedy that was particularly used during for Old Greek comedies were satire (making fun of institutions). The institution made fun of in the play *Lysistrata* is that of war. The play discusses how all of the money used by the city goes to finance war. If women take this money away, men are not able to fight. Women fight against the war, shows women having power, which was not possible during the Greek period. Therefore, making a statement that people fighting against the war should not be taken seriously. A final idea that Greek comedies used is the idea of where laughter came from: derision, physical humor, behaviors; incongruity, two opposites in one situation; and, automatism, rule of three (can only do a joke three times). These ideas were far different than what the Romans considered comedic.

Roman comedies had a different take on form and content. A good example of how Roman comedies were set up can be found in Plautus’s play *Pseudolus.* Like Greek comedies, Roman comedies also took place in Greece, which *Pseudolus* was set in Athens. These comedies always dealt with situational love that seeks help from a slave. While Greek comedies did not deal was not situational. In *Pseudolus,* Calidorus asks Pseudolus, his slave, to help win back his prostitute love from her pimp. Money and love normally drive the plot in Roman comedies, as it does in *Pseudolus*. In order for Pseudolus to get Calidorus’s love to him, Pseudolus needs a lot of money. Roman comedies always want to try and make the audience feel superior to the characters. This superiority is obtained through dramatic irony, where the audience knows more that is happening in the play than the characters do. Greek playwrights did not attempt to achieve this idea for their audiences. A final idea that can be found in Roman comedies is Carnivalesque. This idea is that there is one day where the fool becomes king. *Pseudolus* achieves this by the slave being the smart one and out witting the masters, therefore, being seen as a higher character than the masters. With Greek comedies not dealing with upper class people, this makes the idea of Carnivalesque nearly impossible to present.

While Greek and Roman theatre differ on more than form and content, they are also different on how they affect their audiences. Greek comedies were used as part of a religious festival. The costumes and masks were created to try and emphasize arte, the idea of perfection. Perfect symmetry is what was idea for the Greeks. Old Greek comedy uses the happy idea. This concept uses impracticality as a form of humor. The idea that the play would or could never happen. The happy idea in this play is that women could refuse sex to finish a war. These concepts in Greek comedies differs highly from those of Roman comedies. The play, then, reinforces wars. The plays also utilized the happy idea to emphasize the social structure. Women are supposed to listen to their husbands, but this does not happen in this play. The women are depicted as strong for not having sex and, therefore, gives them the idea of power. By audiences laughing at the idea of a woman being in charge and standing up for herself, it reinforces the social structure.

The Romans viewed their comedies as more than festivals. They had the idea of Carnivalesque, as described above. The main goal of Carnivalesque is to keep social order and construction. There are rules and society needs to decide to follow them. In their play *Pseudolus,* even though the slave uses his wits to fulfill the task given to him by his master, he then still remains a slave. No matter how high you can seem in a play, the social structure is always going to be reinforced at the end of the play. Reinforcement is how Rome was able to continue the way they did and for how long they did.

While Greek and Roman theatre seem as though they are both ancient, they certainly have different outlooks on their theatre form and reflection. Greek theatre follows their specific outlines for their comedies as Rome follows their own ideas. While the two are different, they function on a similar level. Theatre will always affect society, no matter how far back in history one has to go to find the answers.
