<p class="paper-title"><strong>Charles Silverstein’s Impact on the LGBTQ Community</strong></p>

From homosexuality being considered a mental illness, to fighting for same-sex marriage throughout the United States, the LGBTQ community has seen its fair share of struggles becoming accepted in society. Throughout history, there have been, and still are, significant people who have stood up for this community in the United States and have helped it grow. Some of these people are more famous, such as Ellen DeGeneres and Caitlyn Jenner; however, those on the psychology side, such as John Fryer and Evelyn Hooker, have helped the community more academically. Charles Silverstein, Ph.D. is a “clinical psychologist and psychotherapist – certified sex therapist – [and] noted author of gay books” (Silverstein, n.d., Information). Silverstein is a gay psychologist that helps members of the LGBTQ community through his own practice in New York, but, more widely spread, Silverstein has published many works of non-fiction via manuals and scholarly journals, that has helped impact the LGBTQ community both academically and socially.

## Background

Dr. Charles Silverstein has gone through many struggles in his life; one of these struggles was being “born into a generation that viewed homosexuality as a sin, as illegal, and as reflecting shame upon one’s family” (Silverstein, n.d.b, para. 1). Silverstein went through seven years of psychoanalysis before he “decided to have a sexual experience with a man, rather than merely confess [his] sexual fantasies to [his] analyst… [and] never looked back” (Silverstein, n.d.b, para. 2). Silverstein then joined the Gay Activist Alliance of New York and “decided to devote [his] professional career toward changing society’s attitude toward homosexuality” (Silverstein, n.d.b, para. 3). The Gay Activists Alliance is then where met his lover William (Sbordone, 2012, p. 31), who largely impacted Silverstein’s writing later on.

Dr. Charles Silverstein earned his “Ph.D in psychology from Rutgers University in 1974” (Silverstein, n.d., Background). He has completed training programs in both gestalt therapy (Gestalt Institute of New York) and sex therapy (Cornell Medical School). He is licensed in psychology in the states of New York and New Jersey and is certified as a sex therapists by various associations and boards. Silverstein has won many awards for his work over his lifetime on top of being a well renowned psychologist and writer (Silverstein, n.d.a, Background).

## Academic Impact

Along with his educational achievements, Silverstein also thrives academically. Silverstein was “the founding editor for the *Journal of Homosexuality*” (Sbordone, 2012, p. 30). Silverstein has also published many scholarly articles relating to the LGB community and sexual behaviors. Silverstein has studied the relationship between Borderline Personality Disorder and gay people (Silverstein, 1988, Abstract).  He studied how removing homosexuality from the DSM impacted society (Silverstein, 2009, Abstract). He also studied the “relationship between psychiatric diagnosis and morality” pertaining to sexual disorders (Silverstein, 1984, Abstract). Through these articles, it is evident that Silverstein has influenced the academic community through his research. Beyond scholarly articles, Silverstein also published academic, non-fiction books.

One of Silverstein’s main topics of publication is on lesbian or gay individual’s relationship to their therapist. Two major contribution to academia are Silverstein’s books *Gay, Lesbians and their Therapists*, published in 1991, and *The Initial Psychotherapy Interview* published in 2011 (Silverstein, n.d.a, Publications). Hidalgo (1995) writes that *Gay, Lesbians and their Therapists* “focuses on the therapist’s countertransference and self-disclosure issues” (p. 138). This book provides suggestions to the therapist by discussing the best way to work with gay and lesbian patients in terms of transference and countertransference. This book also helps “demonstrate how the feelings and perceptions of therapists, as well as their clients, influence the therapeutic process” (Laird, 2010, p. 327). Laird (2010) states that “One of the underlying but unstated messages of this book is that gays and lesbians are essentially ordinary people with ordinary problems” (p. 328). This helps a reader better understand Silverstein’s view of sexual minority people as regular people. This idea is understandable because Silverstein himself is a sexual minority. It is also important for readers to see Silverstein’s view of sexual minority people so that they also can take the same view or at least be familiar with it. Silverstein’s other book oriented around therapists’ approaches is *The initial psychotherapy interview: A gay man seeks treatment*. In Ratigan’s (2011) review of this book, he states, “Psychoanalysis and homosexuality have had a difficult relationship” (p. 218). This book lays out the frame work of what to do and not to do in a first session with a gay patient (Ratigan, 2011, p. 218-219). This book is important for therapists that take on a gay patient so they understand the best way to approach it. For therapists who may not have LGBTQ members in their lives, it is important for them to know what to do in this particular circumstance. Overall, these two books show Silverstein’s strengths of helping therapists work with sexual minority patients. These ideas have a large impact on the field of psychology because they help therapists understand the LGBTQ community so that they can better work with this specific population.

In Silverstein’s *For the Ferryman: A Personal History*, he discusses his own personal narrative throughout his life and relationship with William Bory. … One aspect in this book discusses his own “horrors of aversion therapy and other psych-away-the-gay methods that fell out of favor” (Eubanks, 2012, para. 3). Silverstein’s personal connection to this type of therapy explains his dedication to proving that people cannot change their sexual orientation. Silverstein’s (2003) article *The religious conversion of homosexuals: Subject selection is the voir dire of psychological research* rebuttals Robert Spitzer’s idea that sexual orientation can be changed through religion. Silverstein (2003) discusses the “ethical issues involved in sexual orientation change” (p. 31) and ultimately claims that a person’s sexual orientation does not change because of religion. Publishing this article helped the LGBTQ community by helping prove Spitzer’s article false. Proving this idea false is important because it was being used as a defense against the LGBTQ community on multiple levels. The academic community largely admires Silverstein’s accomplishments, but he is known for more than his academic expertise. Silverstein has helped the sexual minority population socially as well.

## Social Impact

Silverstein’s direct advocacy for the LGBTQ community can be observed when he “helped sway the American Psychiatric Association to remove homosexuality from its list of mental illnesses in 1973” (Eubanks, 2012, para. 2). He cofounded the Identity House and Institute for Human Identity, both located in New York. The Identity House is “a non-profit, all-volunteer organization that provides Peer Counseling, Group Support, and Therapy Referrals to members of the LGBTQ community” (Identity House, 2017, para. 1). The Institute for Human Identity is a “non-profit psychotherapy and training center dedicated to fostering personal growth free of traditional gender, sexual orientation, and cultural biases” (Institute for Human Identity, n.d., para. 1). These two organization highly benefit the LGBTQ community in New York. On top of being a practicing psychologist, Silverstein largely impacted the LGBTQ community, socially, through writing.

## Publication Impact

Dr. Silverstein is also the author of many books, such as *For the Ferryman: A Personal History, The Joy of Gay Sex,* and *The New Joy of Gay Sex* (Silverstein, n.d., Publications). Dr. Silverstein’s *For the Ferryman: A Personal History* has helped the LGBTQ community since 2011 through Silverstein sharing his personal narrative and relationship with William. Eubanks (2012) summarizes that this book is “of [Silverstein’s] relationship with a beautiful, doomed soul named William Bory… [who] succumbs to drugs and disease” (para. 1). For readers, the story can comfort those who relate to Silverstein’s narrative and educate those who do not. Eubanks (2012) nicely states an opinion that shows this book’s impact by writing, “those of us who need mentors to share memories of the struggle that’s gotten us where we are today should thank Dr. Charles Silverstein for his bravery” (para. 6).

The second book published by Silverstein that has vastly socially impacted the LGBTQ community is *The Joy of Gay Sex.* There are multiple editions of this book. HarperCollins Publishers (2017) describe this book as “a resource on building self-esteem, and a trusted aid for coming out of the closet” (para. 2). Despite the title, readers of this book can learn about many different aspects and stages of being a gay person, on top of how to have sex. Silverstein and Picano (2009) state that the book has been largely censored by lawyers and publishers (p. 12). HarperCollins Publishers (2017) state that the book helps with “safe sex… relationship-oriented issues… and on scores of diverse topics ranging from spirituality to online dating” (para. 2). While it is evident that his book has largely effected sexual minority members, the authors write, “We hope that our third edition will assist another generation to fight what homophobic institutions still exist, as well as the internal problems that a small but significant part of the gay community still suffer” (Silverstein & Picano, 2009, p. 16).

## Discussion

Learning the history of people who have impacted the LGBTQ community, such as Dr. Charles Silverstein, is important for both members of the LGBTQ community and non-members.  The reason for this education is to help understand what this community goes through and to help educate those both inside and outside of the community. It is also important to learn this history because people should know the difficulties of treatment for LGBTQ people and how it is different than cisgender and heterosexual people.

It is important to educate those in the community so that they can more fully understand themselves and support others in their community. Through Dr. Silverstein’s book *The Joy of Gay Sex,* gay members of the community can learn about themselves and their partners. They can see steps in the life of a gay person and stages that other gay people have gone through. This book helps create a sense of community and connection. Members reading *For the Ferryman: A Personal History* can either relate to the narrative or learn about struggles their partner(s) have gone or are going through.

Educating the non-LGBTQ community is important because they should be able to understand the struggles of this minority population. Through Dr. Silverstein’s books, he educated therapists on how to interact with their patients. Heterosexuals can learn about the struggles a gay person goes through by reading *For the Ferryman: A Personal History*. They also can learn about the hazards of conversion therapy. For family members that have a relative in the LGBTQ community, they can learn more of how to relate to their relative. By learning about these different aspects, a person’s prejudice can decrease through social learning theory. If people’s prejudice decreases, then the majority can better help and accept the LGBTQ community.

It is important for all citizens of the United States to understand the history and treatment of the LGBTQ community because the majority majorly impacts the lives of minority members. There are a lot of discriminating laws being passed by people who are not educated on the struggles of minority groups. There is also a lot of prejudice against this community and that can be connected to the uneducated general public. Ultimately, educating the all people can help benefit the LGBTQ community through even more ways than stated above.

## Conclusion

There are many people that have positively impacted the LGBTQ community in the past and still do today. Dr. Charles Silverstein experienced many struggles throughout his life, and through these struggles has positively impacted the lives of sexual minorities in the United States. Through his academic impact, Silverstein has helped therapists that counsel members of the LGBTQ community as well as directly helping specific members of the community. Through his social impact, Silverstein has helped educate gay men through their relationships and stages in life. Others can learn from this writing as well to help better understand gay men. Dr. Silverstein has also helped LGBTQ people through sharing his personal narrative. Majority people can also benefit through this personal narrative by reading the struggles and understanding Dr. Silverstein’s perspective. It is important for citizens of the United States to be educated on the LGBTQ community so that they can see the struggles in everyday life that this community faces. Education is important to understanding minority groups and decreases the prejudices.
