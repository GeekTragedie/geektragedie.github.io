This analysis paper is based on Hodge and McLain’s book “Play Directing: Analysis, Communication, and Style.” It breaks down the final scene in Sophocles’ *Antigone* using dramatic action, character, idea, and tone.

## Dramatic Action

According to Hodge and McLain, dramatic action is “the clash of forces in a play – the continuous conflict between and among characters” (25).  It breaks down a scene into units and then breaks down each unit of the scene, helping discover the action. The final scene in *Antigone* is broken up into five units: one, the beginning (Sophocles 1391) to before the messenger’s entrance (1418); two, the messenger’s entrance (1418) to Eurydike’s corpse being brought in (1441); three, Kreon realizing his wife is dead by her body being brought on stage (1441) to Kreon telling Haimon’s dead body that he killed him (1475); four, Kreon telling his men that he wants to go inside (1476) to Kreon exiting the stage (1511); and five, Kreon’s exit to when the leader exits the stage (1518).

### Unit Breakdown Explanation

There are multiple reasons for why the units are broken up in this manner. Unit one starts upon the entrance of a character and ends with the entrance of a character. Unit two ends with the entrance of another character, though the character is dead. It also “a significant discovery is made” when Kreon discovers the death of his wife (Hodge and McLain 31). The third unit ends by Kreon stating that he is ready to leave the presence of his dead loved ones. This is a change in tactics for Kreon’s mourning process. The fourth unit ends with the departure of Kreon and the messenger. The final unit ends with the exit of the final character on the stage as he feels he has finished his objective of making the audience understand the purpose of the play.

### Titles of Units

Each unit has its own title due to the contents of the action in the unit. Unit one is titled “Haimon’s Murder” due to the discussion between the leader and Kreon about Kreon causing the death of Haimon. The second unit is titled “Kreon’s Denial” because the messenger comes in and tells Kreon about the death of his wife. Kreon, however, does not fully seem to grasp what the messenger has told him. He questions what has been said. The next unit is called “Eurydike’s Murder” because Kreon finally sees that his wife is dead and the messenger describes Eurydike’s suicide to Kreon and Kreon agonizes over her death. Unit four is titled “Desired Death of the Murderer” because Kreon begs to die. He wishes someone would kill him and prays to the gods to cause his death. Kreon asks to be removed from the place where the dead bodies of his loved ones are located. The fifth and final unit is called “Follow the gods” because the leader is telling the audience that all should listen to what the gods want because if they do not, their lives will not turn out well.

### Summary of Action

To summarize unit one, the leader blames Kreon, who then blames himself. The leader accuses Kreon who tries to justify himself. In unit two, the messenger warns Kreon who then wonders at the messenger. The messenger then informs Kreon. Kreon curses the gods and then denies to himself what the messenger told him. In unit three, the leader confirms to Kreon. Kreon questions the gods. The messenger then describes to Kreon and Kreon makes requests to those around him. The messenger then accuses Kreon who in turn demands information. The messenger tells Kreon what he wants to know and Kreon admits. In unit four, the leader confirms to Kreon. Kreon pleads to the gods. The leader tries to redirect Kreon and Kreon begs. The leader advises Kreon who then blames himself. Kreon searches for someone around him. The leader then advises the audience.

## Characters

In this section, each character’s desires, will, moral stance, and decorum is described. Then each character is given a list of adjectives and their initial character-mood-intensity is expressed. The initial mood is described by heartbeat, perspiration, stomach condition, muscle tension, and breathing (Hodge and McLain 55).

### Desires

Kreon’s desires are to be with those he loves as expressed by his calling out to them, “Oh unhappy mother, oh my son” (Sophocles 1445). He wishes to die to be with them: “let my own kindest fate/ make this my final day on earth” (1484-5). The leader desires to have Kreon a strong leader. He tries to advise him to stay in the present and to face what is coming his way. Messenger desires to be worthy, especially in the eyes of the gods, because he has more information than Kreon and wants to make Kreon feel bad for causing the death of his son and wife.

### Will

Kreon’s will to get what he desires is low due to his grief and agony. The leader has more will to get what he desires. He is willing to test his status and possibly risk his job to get what he desires. The messenger is willing to risk his job to get what he desires. While the messenger knows Kreon can fire him, he still toys with the knowledge regarding Eurydike’s death.

### Moral stance

Kreon is searching for his moral stance. At the beginning of the play, Kreon was not a good person and refused to listen to the gods. However, later he decided he was doing the wrong thing and tried to fix his wrong-doings. He “saw the right course of action/ but took it far too late” (1405). Kreon’s moral stance is higher due to his experiences in the play. The leader has a very high moral stance. While he knows Kreon made poor decisions in the play, he still advises Kreon and tries to move him forwards. He knows that judging is only for the gods and the fate of man is in the hands of the gods. The messenger has low moral standards. While he knows to listen to the gods, he does not appreciate how others are feeling. He does what he is told because it is his job, whether it is the right or the wrong thing.

### Decorum

Kreon does not show a very good decorum due to his emotional state and those around him. In the play, he does not follow the correct etiquette to bury the dead. Once he realizes his mistake, it was too late. While Kreon should act mightier in the play, due to the deaths of his loved ones, he does not act a way a king should act. The leader has proper etiquette towards the king and knows the correct ways in which to behave. He understands what needs to happen and the proper way to go about doing that. The messenger has very poor decorum due to his low status and the way he describes Eurydike’s death to the king. The messenger also seems to talk down to Kreon more than is custom.

### Summary list of adjectives

A list of adjectives that would describe Kreon are: sorrowful, crushed, yearning, devastated, drained, and cursed. The leader can be described as helpful, just, and wise. The messenger can be described as hurtful, blameful, rude, and snake-like.

### Initial character-mood intensity at opening

Kreon’s heartbeat is rather slow while his perspiration is heavy, particularly on his back from carrying the weight of his dead son. His stomach is in knots from the sick feeling of mourning. His muscles are weak as he stumbles to even move from devastation and his breath is slow and shallow as he feels he cannot get enough air to support. Each breath is shaking as he inhales. The leader’s heartbeat is rather fast as he is anticipating what Kreon shall do next. There is perspiration on his brow that is light as he is waiting. His stomach is rather hollow as he is wanting to see what is coming next. His muscles are rather tense due to being on edge for Kreon’s reaction. His breath is slow and short. The messenger’s heartrate is fast as he is inching to tell his story of how Kreon’s wife died. There is perspiration in his armpits from moving his fingers with irritability to tell his story. His stomach is stone as he waits Kreon’s responses. His breathing is deep and slow as he wants to ensure there is enough breath to tell his whole story with full detail.

## Idea

The idea section of this paper deals with why the play was written and what is meant to be the outcome. What is the audience supposed to take away from this scene? The main idea of *Antigone* is to listen to the gods, for they are in control of the fate of all human beings.

### Meaning of the title

While the play is titled *Antigone,* the play is really about Kreon’s journey. Its name comes from the person in the play who is correct and does what the gods have said to do. The entirety of the show, Antigone is right while Kreon is in the wrong. Antigone is blessed by having the title of the play, which means the idea comes from those who are against her actions.

### Philosophical statements in dialogue

There are multiple times in this play that the dialogue discusses listening to the gods. In the final scene in particular, there are multiple characters that claim the gods should be listened to and regarded in a positive light: “Oh, Hades, you are hard/ to appease” (1429-30) and “Never fail to respect the gods,/ for the huge claims of proud men/ are always hugely punished” (1514-6). It is also stated that Kreon knew what he was supposed to do but did not do it fast enough. Kreon understands that by not listening to the gods, he caused the death of his wife and son. Therefore, philosophical statements also point to the idea showing audiences to listen to the gods.

### Outcome of the struggle

The biggest struggle in this play was Kreon not letting Antigone bury her brother, but Antigone felt as though it was her womanly duty to do so, as told by the gods. Multiple people in the play tell Kreon that he should let Antigone bury her brother, but Kreon does not listen to them or the gods’ rules. The outcome shows that Antigone was right, and Kreon suffers by having to live his life without those he loves, his wife and son. This outcome benefits Antigone’s side of the struggle, there for supporting that people should follow the will of the gods or they shall be punished.

### Why is it in the play?

This particular scene is in the play to show Kreon’s punishment for not listening to the gods. It secures the theme of the play. Kreon even states his actions were wrong and his punishments were from the gods due to his actions. The theme is more emphasized by the leader’s lines at the very end telling the audience exactly what the theme is.

## Mood

This section connects each unit with specific mood adjectives as well as mood images. There are “adjectives with one for each of the senses.”

### Unit One

The first unit’s mood can be described as sulking, howling, cold, bitter, and rotten. Combining these adjectives, this unit gives the image of a lone coyote howling at the moon in a desert with a cracked rock in the background.

### Unit Two

Unit two’s mood is seen as intense, sharp, prickly, sour, and like teenage body odor. This unit, after being given these adjectives, can evoke the image of a porcupine eating a lemon as he stares into the camera with a skunk spraying in the background that is staring at the porcupine.

### Unit Three

The third unit’s mood can be described as a soldier kicking a dead body. It sounds like children going over bumps on a road in the back seat of a car. It feels hot and tastes dry while also smelling of black tea with no flavoring nor sweetener.

### Unit Four

The fourth unit is stale and bland. It feels like water slipping through your fingers. It sounds like waves on the beach that slowly fade out into silence. It looks like a tense hand grasping at sand that crumbles beneath it.

### Unit Five

The final unit’s mood can be feels like the sun slowly heating up your face while you stare at an open sky without sunscreen. It sounds stern and tastes spicy with a strong scent of fresh air.

## Tempos

The tempos for each unit are as listed: one, slow; two, medium-slow; three, medium; four, medium-slow; five, medium.

## Tone

Pain and agony come to those who do not listen and follow order.
