## Rules for the Theatre Workshops

1. Work together.
   - Theatre is a collaborative process. Nothing is going to work if you do not work together. That means everyone, not just a select few working together.
2. Trust each other.
   - In order to work together, you need to trust each other. That means you need to act in a way to gain others’ trust.
3. Try everything.
   - Some stuff we do in these workshops may not seem like your type of thing. All of you are going through these new things together, so you might as well try it.
4. Do not judge each other.
   - This may be new to everyone. Some people may understand stuff faster than others. Some people may come up with ideas faster than others, but we are all working together as a team, so we cannot judge what other people are doing.
5. Have fun, but not at the expense of others.
   - I want you all to have fun and have a good time, because it’s your time that I’m taking, but I want everyone to have fun. Don’t make fun of someone else to have fun yourself.
6. Keep the rules of the center.
   - We will hopefully have a lot of fun. I want this to be a different environment than the one you have outside of this workshop, but we need to still follow the rules you have at the center.
7. Leave everything outside of this workshop, outside of the workshop.
   - Some stuff may go on outside of this time we have together, but you can leave that at the door. Maybe two of you don’t quite get along, leave that conflict at the door because you need to trust each other and work together in order to make these things work.
8. Try to keep a positive attitude.
   - You’re all in this together. This is your hour to be in here having fun, so try to make the best of it. If you wallow through everything, you aren’t going to enjoy yourself, and you will be preventing other people from trying to enjoy themselves. You are would be wasting your time and the time of others. You have this workshop one hour every week and I want it to be a positive experience.

## Week one: Introduction & Theatre Games

- Introductions
  - Name
  - Hobby
  - Favorite Movie
  - Theatre Experience (done theatre, gone to a show)
- Introduce the Theatre Workshop “Rules”
- Zip, Zap, Zop
  - Stress eye contact
  - Don’t think about who you’re going to send it to, just do it.
- Changing Ball Toss
  - Throw a pretend ball around the circle
  - Stress eye contact when throwing
  - Have fun throwing the different type of ball
  - How many different ways can you throw that ball?
  - Ball changes types (baseball, bowling ball, ping pong ball)
- Pass the Object (Improv Ideas, 18)
  - Have the small throw pillow
  - Pass it from person to person
  - Give the object attributes
    - Hot, Cold, Heavy, Missing for three years, Illegal, Makes you sick, Filthy, Sticky
- Clapping Game
  - Focus
  - Eye contact
  - Keeping in a rhythm helps pass the clap
- Counting Game
  - Cannot go in a circle
  - Listen to an impulse and give and take with the other people in the circle
  - If we fail, all take a deep breath together and start over
- Halftime Show (improv!, 32)
  - Make letters or words on ground
  - No speaking or hand gestures
  - Be aware of your surroundings
  - Work together
  - Adapt to changes in your surroundings
- Group Mime (improv!, 38)
  - Group must do a group task
  - No speaking or hand gestures
  - Watch each other
  - Know what is going on around you
    - Move a gigantic rock from point A to point B
    - Build a log cabin

## Week three: Improv (Scene Building)

- What are you Doing? – 5 min
  - Stand in a circle and one person starts with an action
  - Next person asks what that person is doing
  - The person responds with something that is different than what they are doing
  - More fun if you over-act the action you are performing
- I am a Tree – 5 min
  - One person goes in the center and says they are a tree
  - A second person enters and is something related to the tree
  - A third person enters and is something related to the tree and the previously added object
  - Person one and two exit and the third person restates what they are
- Improv Tips
  - There is only one way to do improvisation: YOURS!
  - Trust your Partner(s)
  - Listen to your partners!
  - You need to take risks!
  - Don’t Say “I can’t”
- Yes,  Because Circle (improvising better, 22) – 5 min
  - Someone says something to the person next to them
    - Comment on imaginary locations (school), characteristics (mood), or situations (long line)
  - The next person specifies
  - Ex. “I hate you, Jimmy!” “Yes, you do hate me, Peter, because I stole all of your lunch money the past two years!” or “The traffic is heavy right now.” “That’s because the zombie apocalypse has started in our town and everyone is trying to flee with their families.”
- Eureka! (Improv Ideas, 122) – 10 min
  - Split into teams
  - Give each team a problem that needs to be solved
  - Have 5 minutes to plan a scene that solves the problem or explains the problem
  - Think outside of the box. I don’t want the real, scientific answer
    - Example: Why do dogs chase cats? Dogs and cats used to play in harmony, until one day the cats played an evil trick on the dog and made them believe that their tails were evil and always trying to attack them. The dogs found out about the trick and they have chased the cats ever since in revenge for making the dogs chase their tails.
    - Why can’t I win the lottery?
    - Why do kids beg for candy on Halloween?
    - Why is the sky blue?
    - Why is it cold in the winter?
- Improv Instruction
  - Always say yes
- Yes… And…
- Genre Scenes
- Emotions
- Rotating Scenes
- Sound Effects
- Pyramid of Scenes

## Mask Workshop

- Persona (Psych Definition) Jung
  - Way you act around different people
- Mask
  - Anything to cover a face or part of a face
- Persona = Latin word for mask
- Different ways you act around different people are the different masks you wear
  - The different roles you play
- Think of the different roles you play and how can they be expressed through a mask?
  - Or! You can create an animal mask, emotion mask, superhero mask
  - Keep it appropriate
  - Be creative
- My mask
  - Role with my friends
  - Strong like a lion and a leader and brave
  - But I also have an emotional side too, like all animals do – unless they don’t have the mental capacity

## Week five: Blank Scenes

- Introduction
  - Blank Scenes: Open scenes, Skeletal scenes, Content-less scenes, Ambiguous scenes
  - It’s a scene that has dialogue, but the meaning for the scene is created by the actors
    - Intonation, hand gestures, movement
- Sample:
  - A: Hello.
  - B: Hi.
  - A: I didn’t expect to see you here.
  - B: I could say the same for you.
  - A: What’s new?
  - B: Not much. You?
  - A. Same. It’s supposed to rain today.
  - B: Oh, really?
  - A: Buckets.
  - B: Well, see you later.
  - A: Yeah, I’ll see you around.
- Now as if: One man shows up to ruin a wedding because he is in love with the bride and thinks the groom is not worthy.
  - *B shows up with something behind his back, behind A who is adjusting his tie in the mirror.*
  - A: Hello.
  - B: Hi.
  - A: I didn’t expect to see you here. *A goes to the side, back away from B, B scoffs,*
  - B: I could say the same for you. *B makes his fingers look as though they are running away*
  - A: What’s new? *A goes to B and fixes his tie, maybe a little too tight. B drops the bucket behind his back. B tries to hide it.*
  - B: Not much. You? *A takes the bucket.*
  - A. Same. It’s supposed to rain today. *A motions to the bucket*
  - B: Oh, really? *B backs away from A*
  - A: Buckets. *A pours the water bucket on B*
  - B: Well, see you later. *B wipes his eyes and spits out water. B turns away from A to exit.*
  - A: Yeah, I’ll see you around.

<p class="piece-note">The rest of this plan (teaching notes and an eight-step rehearsal process for open scenes) came from a published open-scene lesson, so it is summarized here: pairs read the scene with no expression, then experiment with expression, volume, pitch, and speed; they decide who the characters are, where they are, and what is happening; they memorize the lines exactly and present a first draft; then the group talks about what read clearly and the pairs rehearse a second draft.</p>

## Week six: Play Writing

- First step:
  - Think of a story you want to tell
- Structure
  - Beginning, Middle, End
    - Beginning: Opening Scene, Rising Action
    - Middle: Climax
    - End: Falling Action
  - Opening Scene
    - Start as far into the story as possible
      - Ex. Married – don’t start at the beginning of their relationship
        - Give the relationship information through dialogue later
    - Introduces
      - Characters
        - Relationships
        - Morals
        - Background
      - Problem
      - Setting
        - Time – Day, Season, Year
        - Location
    - Sleeping Beauty
      - Sleeping beauty’s party when we are introduced to Aurora, godmothers, king, queen and the evil witch
      - Figure out the problem = spell
      - Setting = castle and village = medieval times
  - Rising Action
    - Events that lead up to climax
    - Developing the conflict
    - Sleeping Beauty
      - Living with godmothers, but runs away
      - Falls in love with Prince
      - Pricks her finger & falls asleep
  - Climax
    - Turning point
    - Biggest point in the show
    - Sleeping Beauty
      - Aurora gets kissed by the Prince
  - Falling Action
    - Resolution of climax
    - Aftermath
    - Sleeping Beauty
      - Witch’s plan is foiled
      - Aurora gets her prince
      - They all live Happily Ever After
- Addition to Script (Different than writing a story)
  - Stage Direction
    - Emotions
      - (angrily, sadly)
    - Actions (A stands up, Debbie combs her hair)
    - Sound Effects (Boom, Crash, Airplane Noises)
    - Sounds from Characters (gasp, whine)
    - Entrances (Flin enters SR)
  - Scene description
    - At the beginning, describe what the scene is to look like a little
    - Windy day at Central Park in New York. Ryan enters SL carrying a donut and Kendra enters SR struggling with her umbrella. Kendra accidentally knocks Ryan’s donut out of his hand and the donut hits the floor.
  - Consider how the line would be said aloud
  - Character summaries
    - Felix is a 20 year old male that loves his family and would do anything for them. His younger brother, Alex, is his best friend and they do everything together.
    - Jen is a shaky nurse that is doubtful that she went into the correct career field. She is caring and everyone loves her when they meet her.
  - Props List
