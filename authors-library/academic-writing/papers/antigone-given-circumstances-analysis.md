This paper covers the given circumstances for the final scene in Sophocles’ *Antigone.* The given circumstances are broken down by environmental facts, previous action, and polar attitudes. Environmental facts are broken down into geographical location, date, economic environment, political environment, social environment, and religious environment. Some environments have more effect on the scene than others may have; however, all are important to keep in mind when analyzing a scene. Each given circumstance is also related back to the scene and shown how it may affect certain situations and characters.

## Environmental Facts

### Geographical location

The final scene in *Antigone* takes place in Thebes, Greece, as stated by the beginning stage directions.  Due to the war that occurred prior to the play, Thebes is more than likely in shambles. This information effects the scene through the ground plan. There is not going to be an elaborate entrance due to the scene being in wreckage. This information also effects the weather in the scene. The climate in Greece is hot temperatures in the summer, average of 77 degrees, and cold temperatures in the winter, average of 34 degrees. This information effects the characters in the play dependent on the season the play takes place.

### Date

The play more than likely takes place during the summer. The season can be assumed due to Antigone burying her brother with dirt and the guards talking about the hot sun. With a climate like Thebes’, according to Climate Data, it is probably very hot and dry during the course of the play, which correlates with the high amount of tension in the play and the hot tempers of Kreon and Antigone (“Climate: Thebes,” n.d.). This particular scene takes place in the later afternoon due to all the events that take place between Kreon deciding to bury Antigone’s brother and Haimon killing himself. These events would have taken a lot of time during the day.

The scene also takes place during the time of the Ancient Greeks, 800BC to 500BC. With the scene taking place during this time period, the movements are going to echo those that would happen during the time period as well as the costumes. Kreon will be in color to show his royalty while Leader and Messenger will not to show the contrast between the characters. During the period, the actors would also have on masks. The director already feels as though the style is very different from that she has used before and feels uncomfortable adding masks to the scene due to lack of mask knowledge.

### Economic environment

There are various class levels in the scene. Kreon is of the highest level because he is the king and has the more money than that of the messenger and the leader. While he has the most money in the scene, he does not have the most power. The messenger is the next level in the scene followed by the leader. While the messenger and the leader have less money than Kreon, they have more power with their words due to Kreon’s recent misfortune. They do keep their distance, however, because Kreon does have more money than these two, so they do not want to push their boundaries more than with just their words. Due to the previous actions before the scene, I do not believe the economic environment has as much effect as other environments.

### Political environment

Greece is ruled by kings in the nation and the kings are passed down from generation to generation. The leader and messenger have very little power in this scene. Kreon is the present king who would then pass down the crown to his son. This action takes a lot of effect in this scene because Kreon discusses having no more sons left. With no more sons, Kreon cannot pass on his legacy to another generation. When Kreon finds out that his wife has died, he realizes that he can no longer create another son to then pass down the crown to. The king realizes how far he his family has fallen in a short period of time. Kreon’s downfall was created by his political power. Kreon wanted to show his people that he was to be taken seriously as a new leader. To do this, he demanded that no one bury Antigone’s brother. While multiple people told him this demand was a mistake, Kreon did not listen because he wanted to show his strength and not go back on his word. Demanding this, however, goes against the words of the gods.

### Religious environment

The religious environment makes a large difference in the Greek society. The Greeks highly believe that the gods hold human’s fates in their hands. The gods are able to tell the humans how to act. Due to Kreon disobeying the gods by taking away Antigone’s right to bury her brother, Kreon is punished by the gods. Kreon believes that all the death of his son and his wife are consequences the gods bestowed on Kreon for him disobeying the god’s wishes. Kreon talks to Hades in the scene about taking so many people from Kreon. The leader at the end of the play states that people must “Never fail to respect the gods” (Sophocles, 442 B.C., p. 1514). All actions of people during the Greek period must be used to appeal to the gods or else it is a waste. If one disobeys the gods, they are punished by “fate’s avalanche” (Sophocles, 442 B.C., p. 1510). Due to Kreon’s downfall with the gods, the leader and messenger feel more power because of the religious environment. Leader and Messenger down to Kreon for his mistakes

### Social environment

Most of the social environment revolve around the gods. Antigone firmly believed it was her social right to bury her brother because she was the woman in the family. Kreon ordering her to disobey her womanly rights was against the gods and against the social norm. Antigone performed an act against Kreon’s wishes and buried her brother anyway. Society, believing in the gods’ will and Antigone’s duties, support Antigone’s actions. Therefore, in the scene, the leader and messenger did not support Kreon’s demands and feel as though he deserved the punishment he was given for going against the social norm behavior.

## Previous Action

Many actions happened before this scene. Before the play Oedipus’ sons, Polyneices and Eteocles, were supposed to share the kingdom, however Eteocles did not give over the kingdom when it was his time to step down. He, instead, banished Polyneices from Thebes. Polyneices returned with an army and attacked Thebes. Both brother ended up dead and Kreon was left to take over the kingdom. The play starts with Thebes in shambles. Kreon believed that Polyneices committed treason and demanded no one bury his body while Eteocles was buried with honors. Antigone believes it is her duty as a sister to bury Polyneices against Kreon’s orders. Antigone does so and Kreon locks her in a tomb with food to survive, but ultimately to die in the end. Haemon, engaged to Antigone, begs his father to release Antigone. Tiresias tells Kreon that the gods would not be happy with Kreon unless he does what they say. Kreon changes his mind and buries Polyneices and goes to take Antigone out of the tomb. Antigone, however, has already killed herself. Haemon curses his father and attempts to kill him but fails. Haemon kills himself and Kreon is left feeling guilty for his previous actions. Kreon’s wife has seen her son dead and has exited into the castle. The scene begins with Kreon mourning over his dead son’s body. The previous action effects the scene because Kreon believes he is the one at fault for the death of Haemon. He knows he should have obeyed the orders of the gods and those around him earlier, burying Polyneices and saving Antigone. With Thebes in shambles, Kreon’s life ends up effecting how the setting also looks, with no support system and starting over from the beginning.

## Polar Attitudes

At the beginning of the play, Kreon is attempting to show his power over the people. He wants people to see his control and follow his orders. Kreon does not want to go back on his word or else he will be viewed as weak. Kreon believes in order to show his power, he must not bury Polyneices’ body and show the public what happens to those who commit treason. Kreon, however, is blinded by his power.

At the end of the play, Kreon no longer believes he needs to prove his power. He regrets his decision to not bury and suffered the consequences of his actions. Kreon sees the error in his ways, but it is too late. He loses those he loves, and the leader points out that everyone must listen to the gods to prevent consequences.
