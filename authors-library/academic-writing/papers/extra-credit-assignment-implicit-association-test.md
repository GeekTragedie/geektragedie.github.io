<p class="paper-title">Implicit Association Test</p>

I took Project Implicit’s Abled – Disabled IAT and received the result of “slightly automatic preference for disabled persons compared to abled persons.” When looking at the information given after finishing my quiz, I was astonished at how many people have a strong automatic preference for abled people compared to disabled people (33%). I, on the other hand, am among the 5% that has a preference for disabled people. I can understand this finding for a few reasons.

While I would love to believe that I have no stigma or bias against persons who are disabled, there may be a recent change in my mindset while taking this quiz. I was offered a Rehearsal Assistant job at [an organization] in Sioux Falls for this summer. Due to the fact that the facility works with people with disabilities, I am trying to learn all I can about working with those who have developmental disabilities. This job may have had an effect on my quiz.

I am a Christian and believe that all people were created by God to be perfect just the way they are. People who are disabled are no different and God made them just as special as everyone else. I believe that my religious background certainly could have had an effect on my results in this quiz. I really like to see people as having all the opportunities that they can have in life, no matter who they are. I thoroughly enjoy talking to anyone and hearing what they feel about numerous subjects.

I also have been really working on my fruit of the spirit of patience for ever since my junior year of high school. I believe that if anyone can conquer their ability to be patient, the world would be less stressful. With my work on patience, I really have been able to understand those who are not fully able to understand things easily. With this in mind, having the patience to work with those that are disabled is certainly a blessing for me, because it gives me the chance to possibly make a difference and also meet new people.

Finally, I believe my past experience working with people and having patience probably helps explain my results. I work in the USD Writing Center and also work with ESL Students. These students do not quite fully understand English and I usually have to slow down or rephrase the way that I am trying to say something. This experience has helped me enhance my patience and has given me the ability to converse with someone people do not always try to talk to. I have also volunteered at multiple nursing homes in my life time and through these experiences, I certainly have come in contact with elderly people who have also helped increase my patience or had trouble fully understanding what I was trying to explain. While these two experiences were not directly related to the disabled population, I believe the experiences have helped me really learn patience and, most of all, kindness towards everyone as individuals.

In class when we watched the video of the people with Down syndrome, or “up syndrome” as one of the women called it, I felt myself rooting for them. I really was so happy that they had jobs and were living their life just as abled people would. It is ridiculous the way that people in society do not think disabled people are capable of living on their own. If more people believed in those who were disabled and showed them more appreciation, I really believe those who are disabled would be able to more fully live their lives because they would not have to worry about others holding them back. Others could just lend a helping hand, as they would to any other person in the world.
