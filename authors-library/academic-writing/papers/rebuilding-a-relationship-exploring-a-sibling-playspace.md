## Rebuilding a Relationship: Exploring a Sibling Playspace

The goal of the playspace presentation was to invite the Winter 2019 Creative Arts Therapy class into the playspace of someone in my life. A playspace, as defined by Landers (2019), is “a mutually-acknowledged understanding between people that allows them to engage together in an imaginative activity that is as interesting and/or challenging for them as they can tolerate, and that does not harm them” (p. 1). The relationship I chose is the playspace between my brother and me. I struggle with this relationship since my brother passed away from suicide. My struggle encouraged me to explore this relationship for this presentation. This paper discusses my process creating my playspace presentation and states what I learned from the presentation of this playspace.

## Creative Process

My creative process for my playspace presentation took a long time and was mentally and emotionally tasking. My process for this project is best described by Wittig’s (2019) description of Authentic Movement: “the mover closes her eyes, and waits for an impulse to move; and then follows the impulse where it leads… wait until something changes. That change is the impulse, the reason to move” (pp. 1-2). I felt my brother was the right choice for this project, so I followed that impulse, which lead me through my creative process. If I felt something about the project was not right, I sat with the discomfort until I realized what the root cause was and change it. I knew my relationship with my brother was difficult to explore, so I began my work at the beginning of the quarter and allotted enough time for relationship reflection, brainstorms, activity choice, and emotional consideration.

### Relationship Reflection

First, I reflected on the relationship I had with my brother, and realized I held a motherly role in his life. I was still a young child myself when my brother was born, and our relationship began. I carried him around the house, not sharing him with anyone. I slept on my brother’s floor and taught him math late at night. When things at home felt scary, I always comforted my brother. On Halloween, I took my brother trick-or-treating and oversaw his safety. I hold on to pictures of these memories at this stage of our relationship. I realized the motherly role I held in our playspace, which helped me understand when and why our relationship shifted over time.

When I became a teenager, my brother and I grew further apart, but I realized the support that can come from another person. We had late-night talks where we shared beliefs about the world, music preferences, and difficulties with our parents. I became vulnerable and cried sometimes; he reassured me that our parents still love us, even though we may not feel the love at times. When I went through rough break ups, my brother created cartoons for me to both cheer me up and show me his support; I still have these notes today. Our playspace became an exploration of world topics that sparked our curiosity but topics that were not necessarily safe to discuss with others. Our playspace was full of unspoken support and understanding.

I went to college, and my brother became a teenager, which caused distance to build between us; late-night talks decreased and there were no more notes. I felt like a distant mother, trying to give him space to become his own person but still wanting to guide him. At times, I tried to help him find colleges and tried to attend athletic events when I could. I was always proud of his accomplishments and asked him about his happiness during our rare late-night talks. While our playspace remained the same, it became rare.

There was a shift in our relationship in his last year of life when he was struggling. Our discussions were done at family events or over coffee, no more late-nights. We talked about social justice issues, why life seemed unfair, and white-male-middleclass privilege. Our talks were more intellectual than personal. We went on nature hikes and did not talk; instead, he showed me how to do photography. With my interest in the arts, I was proud of his photography ability and always wanted to show his work to others. I felt like a proud mother and wanted everyone to know how special my brother was. With this motherly feeling came a lot of worry for the mental health of my brother, especially given my educational background in psychology.

Finally came the moment in our relationship I will never forget, our last days together. My mom called and told me my brother had attempted suicide and “things” were “not looking good.” I held my brother’s hand. I talked about our memories together, cried, and prayed. When we learned he was not going to make it, I said my good bye. I sang the song You are My Sunshine, thanked him, and said I loved him.

Towards the end of my brother’s life, I became confused by our playspace. Johnson (2019) states, “The playspace consists of discrepant communications… The playspace is an intersubjective experience mutually understood by all participants” (pp. 2-3). I questioned my role, the rules, and where imagination stopped and reality began. The playspace between my brother and I stopped when he passed away. Johnson (2019) notes “Play does not continue if a party becomes hurt” (p. 1). It hurt me to sit with him and watch my brother suffer. It hurt me to say my last words. It hurt me to watch my family mourn. Now, I am learning how to play with my brother again; I am slowly building trust in our relationship and in our playspace. Through this project, I learned what my playspace with my brother held, so I can hopefully bring some of these aspects back.

### Brainstorming

With many feelings, memories, and thoughts in my mind, I talked through my ideas with someone else. Part of the writing process is brainstorming; so, I utilized this stage in my project process with a trusted, fellow writing consultant. I explained the assignment, discussed my relationship, and rambled any thoughts I had in the moment. My co-worker helped me organize my thoughts and narrow down the feelings and memories I wanted to use for my presentation. I created an outline with ideas and talked through my frustrations. Eventually, I went to my co-worker with activities I wanted to use to evoke the feelings I held in the playspace with my brother.

### Activity Choice

I thought of many experientials I could facilitate for my playspace project. I wanted practice facilitating multiple modalities to evoke feelings but was unsure how to narrow down my list. Originally, I wanted to lead the class through a photography experiential where they looked at their surroundings through the lens of a camera. I wanted people to see their surroundings differently and spark curiosity. I realized this experience did not evoke the feelings of myself in the playspace and photography was more of an activity my brother and I did together. While I spent a lot time exploring this modality, my impulse did not feel this exercise yielded the results I was looking for.

To evoke feelings, I needed to discover the root of my feelings in the different stages of my playspace with my brother. Young (2017) describes the miracle question, a solutioning technique, to get “the client to identify a course of action” (p. 248) so the client can discuss “changes in behavior and…focuses on a world where the problem no longer exists” (p. 249). I used this technique on myself to see what was lacking now in my relationship with my brother so I could learn what was felt in the playspace, therefore learning what feelings I wanted to provoke in my experiential: responsibility for him, support from him, worry for him, and longing for him.

With an idea of the feelings I wanted to evoke, I created an activity that reflected the beginning of our relationship: protective of him. I thought of a drama experiential where one partner led the other around the space; this built trust between the partners as well as a protective nature of one partner over the other. I also thought of an art activity where one partner describes a picture on a page to the other partner without seeing how the picture turned out, building curiosity, trust, responsibility, and pride. After I decided the other activities in my presentation, I chose to facilitate the art activity because I wanted to use visual art. I also felt an impulse, like the one described by Wittig (2019), to use the visual art activity over the drama experiential.

I then wanted to evoke a feeling of support in one partner while creating some distance. I decided on an activity that was like a memory of my brother and me. I led one group through a drama exercise where they felt like emotional teenagers and had them lock themselves in their room. I led the other group members through a literature and art activity to try and cheer up the other partner. I strongly felt this activity evoked the necessary feelings from my playspace.

My final group of activities was meant to evoke worry followed by longing. I chose a movement activity to have the partners get in-tune with each other. I hoped to evoke a feeling of understanding, similar to Lewis’ (1984) idea of somatic countertransference. I wanted the partners to allow the “experience to enter the body” feeling “energy coming into their body… shar[ing] the unspeakable bodily felt experience with the [partner]” (Lewis, 1984, pp. 183-185). After I built this felt relationship, I wanted to make it difficult to understand these feelings and create physical distance between the partners, so each stepped further away from each other. I wanted to create worry by the distance and grow this feeling by one partner turning away. I also hoped to evoke longing by one partner, through not knowing where the other partner is, and from the other partner, through seeing the partner, but not have them there. I wanted to have the group sit in these feelings and incorporate a music technique, so I played the song You are My Sunshine to allow the group to reflect and sit in the moment.

### Process Challenges

I faced challenges throughout my creative process in terms of time, guilt, and lack of emotional understanding. I knew this project took a long time, and while I started my process at the beginning of the quarter, I felt rushed when it came time for me to present. I did not feel as though my presentation was thought out as much as it could have been. My feelings about time may result from my perfectionist personality, but also may come from a current lack of playspace with my brother.

I felt guilty and responsible throughout most of this process. While I feel some responsibility about my brother’s death, I felt guilty when I used my brother to gain personal insight and understanding. I felt guilty when I could not ask for my brother’s permission to use our playspace. I also felt responsible to reflect our playspace correctly but worried I would feel guilty if I did not do justice to our playspace.

I felt unable discuss my project with anyone close, fearful they could not understand my emotions throughout this process. I do not know anyone that has had an experience similar to mine. While my family also lost my brother, their relationship and playspace with him is different than mine. I felt a lack of emotional understanding because I could not describe my feeling to anyone since the emotion defies the boundaries of language. While I brainstormed my project with a co-worker, I felt alone with this experience throughout the quarter. This feeling of loneliness disintegrated after my presentation because my classmates reflected their experience in a way that made me feel heard and understood.

## Presentation Outcome

I learned more about my playspace with my brother from the presentation than I originally thought was possible. I found the best way for me to provide self-care and prepare my mind for an emotionally tasking event. I aesthetically distanced myself throughout the activity, so I could enjoy sharing my experience with others and not cry. I was originally nervous about the short activities evoking the feelings I hoped for but found out how quickly arts activities can create connection and evoke emotion. I learned to trust my classmates and professors to hold space and explore emotion with me. At the end of my process, I found the purpose behind my impulse to explore my playspace with my brother. I felt alone in my feelings and felt as though others did not care about how I feel; people in my life always ask how my family or mom feel. Most importantly, I now know that I can show other people how I feel without words through a creative process.

## Conclusion

My playspace presentation challenged me to explore my relationship with my brother through a new lens. I learned the roots of my relationship with my brother and how to evoke feelings in others to create emotional understanding. The presentation itself gave me peace because I felt heard and understood by others. I now feel encouraged to rebuild this playspace with my brother, which I hope will help me with my healing process.

## Presentation Activities

**Pair Up**

- Split into Person A & B
    - Person A – More of a motherly – leadership role
    - Person B – More of a follower – sibling role

**Motherly Relationship Creation**

- Person A – Verbally describe what you see on your picture to Person B without looking at Person B’s paper
- Person B – Listen to Person A and draw what you hear

**Note Writing**

- Stage 1:
    - Person A – Take a walk around the space. You are now an angsty teen. The person you were in a relationship with just broke up with you and started dating your best friend.
    - Person B – You want to cheer up person A, but you two have drifted apart a little. Create a note with a small cartoon to cheer up your partner.
    - Person A – Sit with this situation.
        - How might you feel?
        - How does it make you feel?
        - Can you relate to this feeling?
        - Is there a certain speed you might be walking?
        - Is there a movement that you can connect to your feeling?
        - Is there a sound that goes with this feeling?
- Stage 2:
    - When Person B is done with the cartoon.
    - Person A stop walking and find a single stance
    - Person B secretly deliver your note to person A and watch
    - Person A open the note
        - How does it make you feel?

**Mirror Exercise**

- Stand face to face with your partner
- Mirroring Exercise
- Person A lead and person B follow
    - Over time, using your own instincts, change the leader between the two partners
- Each partner take a step backwards
    - Continue taking steps backwards
- Partner B turn around, Now

**Song**

- Now this person has been taken away from you.
- I want you to find a comfortable position to close your eyes – standing, sitting, laying down
- Picture this person and imagine them no longer in your life.
- Play song “Jealous of the Angels” (4:03 min)
