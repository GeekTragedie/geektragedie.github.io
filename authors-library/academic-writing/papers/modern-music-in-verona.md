<p class="paper-title">Modern Music in Verona</p>

The University of South Dakota included a production of *Two Gentlemen of Verona* in their 2016-2017 season. This production did not set the play in the times of Early Modern England, but more modernized it. The modernization seemed to match a goal set by the director of making the play more understandable to a modern, college audience. The play’s decision to add music certainly helped with the understanding of characters throughout the play and their view of the situations around them.

It seemed as though the director’s main goal of the USD production was to pique the interest of college students, which is understandable given the venue. One of the ways the production aimed their production was through modernizing the play through music and costumes. The play added music from 2016 to grasp the attention of the audience from the moment they entered the theatre. The music added was song by the characters to provide the audience with more understanding of where the character could be understood, emotionally, at the beginning and middle of the play. Adding these modern musical numbers helped the audience create a parallel between the characters and modern-day emotions and events. The music helped create the world of the play and the characters in this world. The costumes were also not from the Early Modern English period, which helped the audience better identify with the characters. Using modern dress, the audience can see that the character’s issues do not only pertain to the period the play was written, but also can affect the perception of audiences today. This connection to audiences helps increase the directors goal of keeping the play interesting to a college student population.

An aspect of the play that helped with audience understanding was the additional information written for the specific production explaining why the house lights were remaining on and why there were audience seats on the stage. The speech before the play explained how the Shakespearean productions would have been held as well. Another aspect helping with understanding was the constant use of hand gestures with props. These gestures not only were to other actors, they were also to and with the audience seated on stage. An example of this is when Proteus was asking the audience which woman he should love, Silvia or Julia. While the audience may not have fully understood the dilemma he was facing, by directly asking the stage left side audience “Julia?” and the stage right side “Silvia?” it became understood that he was deciding of which one to choose.

One element of the production that makes it different amongst other productions and also helped the audience create textual understanding of a character in *Two Gentlemen of Verona*, was the music added to Thurio’s sonnet. The music was written by actors in the show and the effect on the audience helped them connect more to what Thurio was feeling in the play. The music modernized the monologue, which then made the situation more relatable to the audience. Adding the music to this monologue also made Thurio seem more ridiculous than he was already portrayed as. Throughout the play, no one wants Silvia to settle for Thurio and adding this music number and dance made the audience connect with Silvia’s choice more.

Overall, the production was understandable and entertaining. Audiences could understand the Shakespeare language better using modernization and gestures. The choice of adding music certainly helped the audience connect to the play more and understand the storyline.
