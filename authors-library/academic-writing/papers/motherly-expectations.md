## Motherly Expectations

Relationships in life are constantly changing over time, and each relationship can impact different people in ways they do not realize. My relationship with my mother started close, but as I began late childhood and adolescence, our relationship became more conflicted and disengaged. After working towards resolving our conflicted relationship, it still has some conflict, but I have set more personal boundaries to keep the relationship healthy and close. While completing my education to becoming a therapist, it is important for me to acknowledge and analyze this relationship conflict with my mother to understand myself and how my experience may affect my relationships with my future clients. In this paper, I will analyze my relationship with my mother and how our conflict has influenced other relationships in my life in order to better understand my actions and improve my relationship.

## Relationship Conflict

My mother’s expectations for me has caused conflict in our relationship. My mother’s three main expectations were not getting pregnant out of wedlock, body size, and maintaining high grades. Jackson (as cited in Nichols, 2008) believes family rules are shown through messages and in order to understand these rules one must “observe redundancies in interaction” (p. 158). My mother’s expectations were taught as family rules, and I thought following the rules would help our relationship grow stronger, as I felt an invisible loyalty to my family. Nichols (2008) describes invisible loyalties as “unconscious commitments children take on to help their families to the detriment of their own well-being” (p. 248). However, the pressure to meet her expectations strained our relationship as I struggled to follow the rules. I continued trying to meet her expectations until I no longer lived with her and created my own views on sexual relationships out of wedlock, body size, and academic achievement.

My relationship with my mother can also be described as a pursuer-distancer relationship. Love (2018) states that, in a relationship, pursuers start to “take action by trying to move closer to their partners” when they feel the relationship is being threatened (p. 1). Distancers in a relationship do the opposite when they see the relationship being threatened, they start to pull away from the other person (p. 1). During high school, my mother always pursued a stronger relationship with me, offering me advice or asking questions about my day. I also saw conflict in our relationship, but I decided to pull away.

My mother’s pursuing personality can be described as overfunctioning. According to Williams and Jimenez (2012), “overfunctioners like to give advice to others, often because they feel responsible for them… [they] can convey the message that they know what is best for you through their advice-giving… [they] may have goals for others that they don’t have for themselves” (p. 141). My mother played the role of an overfunctioner in our relationship, which I did not want, so I started to distance myself from her as much as possible. I refused to take her advice and tried to ignore her existence as much as possible.

My reaction to my mother’s pursuing and overfunctioning was to become disengaged.  According to Nichols (2008), disengaged relationships “are independent but isolated;” while it helps individuals find “autonomy… disengagement limits affection and support” (p. 187).  I did not want attention from my mother and wanted to find my own way the world, so I initiated the disengagement and eventually my mother stopped her pursuit.

## History of Relationship Development

In order to best understand the relationship between my mother and I, it is important to look at the history. There is a history of how my mother came to create expectations regarding pregnancy out of wedlock, body image, and academic achievement. The history of our relationship where she communicated the family rules can be traced back to my mother’s history, my childhood, and my adolescence.

### Not Getting Pregnant Out of Wedlock

There is an unspoken expectation set by my mother for me to not become pregnant out of wedlock. These expectations were communicated to me through verbal comments and my mother’s modeled behavior. This expectation came from my mother’s negative experience of becoming pregnant with me out of wedlock. My mother comes from a Scandinavian background and a devoutly Lutheran family. Scandinavians are predominantly Lutheran (Erickson, 2005, p. 646), and, in my family’s values, this denomination believes that sexual intercourse should only occur between two married individuals. Due to the accidental pregnancy, my mother was pressured by members of the family and their Lutheran beliefs into marriage to ensure the baby was not born out of wedlock. The relationship was unhealthy, and she filed for divorce after three years.

My mother’s behavior regarding discussing sexual intercourse follows a family pattern of not discussing secrets. My father and mother wanted to hide my mother’s pregnancy as much as possible by getting married early. McGoldrick (1995) describes families who hide secrets by not discussing the information (p. 59); she says, “at times it is the content of the secret itself that is powerful” giving the example of “a pregnancy that occurs outside of marriage” (p. 78). My mother never discussed sexual relationships or pregnancies outside of marriage, making the topic a family secret. I felt the expectation to not getting pregnant out of wedlock by my mother never talking about sexual intercourse. She let my abstinence-only education from school and church instill fear in me regarding sexual relationships outside of marriage.

My mother saw her pregnancy as a mistake, one that she did not want me to also make. McGoldrick (1995) states, “children may… be constrained by parental fear of repeating a painful past drama” (p. 59). Not wanting me to make the same mistake as she did when she became pregnant out of wedlock, there has always been an unspoken expectation for me to not accidentally get pregnant as well. The easiest way to not get pregnant out of wedlock is to ensure I never had sexual relationships. While my mother neglected discuss sexual intercourse, she did sit down and talk to me about body image.

### Body Size

My mother was not subtle about her expectations related to my body size. Body image has long been something my mother carried herself. When I was getting dressed for school, my mother commented on my outfit choice. I reacted to her statement negatively, to which she defended herself by saying she was only worried about what the others at school may say regarding how my outfit made me look. This comment was the first glimpse as to why my mother was so critical about my size.

My mother’s expectation regarding my body size was created as she tried to over-correct the issue she saw in herself, not wanting the same issue to happen to me. My mother was overfunctioning by helping me try to achieve a goal she set for me. She did not want me to face the same struggles she had faced. Her pursuit for me to reach her goal caused me to eventually disengage from her.

Since a young age, I have known how important my mother views body image. I can remember being in third grade and my mother telling me to suck in my stomach to look better in my Halloween costume. I was always enrolled in athletic activities, even though my passion was not in sports. My mother always stressed that I needed to be in some athletic activity in school to ensure I gained the right amount of exercise necessary. Most of the moments I remember my mother saying she was proud of me regard athletic achievements and nothing else. When I became an adolescent and started making my own decisions regarding my extracurricular activities, quitting volleyball and track, my mother started to forcibly suggest my work outs in other forms. When I would ask permission to spend time with a friend, my mother would respond by asking me if I ran that day or telling me I needed to go for a run before I would have permission to go anywhere. I remember a day when I ran three miles in tears. The more I was forced to run, the deeper my anger for my mother grew. My mother manipulated my exercise, but also ensured to also try to control the food I ate as well.

My mother commented on the amount of food I ate during dinner or made it a point to load my plate with food, giving me a significant amount less than others in the family. When I left for college, my mother made it a point to tell me that I needed to exercise in college because she was worried how other girls might tease me because of my size. This comment shocked me as she was the only person in my life that seemed to bully me due to my body size.

Reviewing the relationship my mother and I have today, her expectation for me to control my body size still exists. My mother still verbally judges my size when she sees me, asks about my exercise schedule, and comments about the amount of food I eat.

### High Grades

My mother’s expectation for academic success has been instilled in her through our heritage, but her continuation of this success created anxiety in me. Scandinavians are known for being hard working (Erickson, 2005, p. 642), and Germans are known to make education a priority (Winawer & Wetzel, 2005, p. 555). My mother’s maternal family are mainly Scandinavian while her paternal family is mainly German. Combining the two characteristics of hard-working and prioritizing education, it is understandable that my mother has a family rule regarding academic success. There is also a multigenerational pattern in our family of college graduates and teaching careers. My great-great grandfather graduated from college and every family member since has also obtained college degrees. There are six members of the current family that are teachers, including my mother.

My mother is an elementary school teacher who believes education is crucial to a successful life. She would work on homework with me at night to ensure I was understanding my material at school. I remember struggling in literature and spending many nights with my mother helping me learn to read faster and appreciate books. The amount of attention she showed towards my education and not in other aspects of my life created an unspoken expectation of obtaining high grades. Having high grades eventually was normalized for me, and I would only gain negative attention regarding school if I received a grade lower than an A. Earning a B in a course gave the appearance that something was wrong in my life, either my friend choices or my time with electronics. This grade would instigate a conversation between my mother and I that usually resulted in an argument. So, I worked hard to obtain straight As in high school to prevent conflict with my mother.

I still feel the need to prove myself academically with my mother, even though she has told me she does not care about grades. I have gained the core belief that education is important and in order to have a better life, I need to be well educated. I also gained a passion for knowledge. My mother’s expectation for me to achieve high grades instilled in me an importance for my own education. I do not know whether the love for knowledge is due to my culture, my mother’s expectations, or my own personal growth.

## Efforts toward Relationship Resolution

My first effort towards resolving my disengaged conflictual relationship with my mother was my effort towards meeting her expectations. I did not become sexually active, maintained a workout schedule, and obtained high grades throughout high school. Fulfilling these expectations did not seem to resolve our relationship problem, but I thought her expectations were important while I was living under her roof. My feelings towards these actions and expectations changed when I moved away for college.

When I started college, I wanted to distance myself as much as possible from her which lead to no success. Bowen (as cited in Hoshino, 2018) describes emotional cutoff in a relationship as “the process of separation, isolation, withdrawal, running away or denying importance of parental family” (p. 2).  Emotional cutoff is not a healthy way to deal with relationship conflict as the emotional frustration does not subside (Bowen as cited in Hoshino, 2018). While I attempted to emotionally cutoff my mother, I was unsuccessful as I needed to continue communication. I needed a place to stay during holiday breaks and still wanted to maintain my close relationships with my younger brothers, which would not have been possible if I emotionally cut off my mother. Once realizing my relationship with my mother was unavoidable, I decided to see a counselor to better understand the issues we were facing on my side of the relationship.

As recommended by my counselor, I talked to other members in my family to see how they view their relationship with my mother as I was unsure how to create a successful relationship. I talked with my maternal grandma and my aunt regarding my mother’s behaviors to see if they had similar problems with their relationships with my mother. One can view this interaction with my aunt and grandmother as emotional triangulation. Bowen (as cited by Kerr, 2008) describes emotional triangulation happening “when two people are in conflict, one person attempts to draw in a third person to stabilize the system” (p. 98). While I created triangles, I gained validation that others also have difficulty maintaining their relationships with my mother. My relatives were able to give me great insight and security that they felt my mother was very distant and did not like to discuss issues she was facing. I also obtained insight from my relatives as to how they maintain their relationships. I was able to apply their tactics to maintaining their relationships to my own relationship with my mother, and my mother and I soon grew closer.

My counselor also used a counseling technique to help me gain insight into my mother’s perspective. My counselor led me through a strategy similar to role reversal. Landy (1994) describes role reversal as taking the point of view of another person to gain insight into their feelings (pp. 141-143). My counselor asked me to consider a time of conflict with my mother. After describing the situation, my counselor told me to view the event through my mother’s point of view and describe any feelings or thoughts that arose. Through discussions with my counselor, I was able to take my mother’s perspective on different situations. For example, I could see why she was worried about my body size as she did not want me to struggle with the same feelings she had carried. Understanding her point of view was helpful for me to grow closer to her and empathize with her. It also allowed me to forgive her actions and comments during my adolescent years.

I grew closer to my mother when I started having more conflict with my father. While meeting with my counselor, I expressed my frustration with my father because he stopped communicating with me completely. He did not return my calls, rarely spoke to me at family holidays, and did not contact me on birthdays. My counselor encouraged me to share my anger with my mother, as she previously had a relationship with my father. When I shared this frustration with my mother, she was very understanding of my situation as she also experienced the same feelings with my father. Being able to confide in my mother regarding my frustration felt good. I felt as though our relationship grew closer when I decided to ask her for help.

From my recollection, after my brother, Thomas, passed away, my mother and I grew the closest. My brother took his own life, which had a major impact on our family as it was a rather sudden death. McGoldrick (1995) shares how families may deal with loss:

> In a sense all families are marked by their shared losses. At times of loss, family members are generally required to deal with one another in an intimate way at a vulnerable time… This has the potential to bring a family together… a death in the family provides an opportunity… to reword old relationships. (p. 127)

I decided to reexamine my relationship with my mother because we had the shared the vulnerable experience of losing Thomas. My mother and I have this experience that we are unable to share with others, drawing us closer together. While our relationship is still underlined by the unspoken expectations, I am more comfortable in sharing my views and she is more accepting. The event of my brother’s death made me realize that, while my mother has high hopes for her children, all she wants is for me to be happy and healthy. I also want my mother to be happy, so I felt more obligated to take care of her emotionally. In turn, my mother started to discuss feelings difficulties more openly. While my mother still holds her expectations, I am aware of them and can start to choose which aspects I want to continue.

## Other Relationships Affected

Though my relationship with my mother is starting to be more positive, there are still aspects from our relationship that affect relationships I have today. Some relationships that have been affected are my relationship with myself, my friendships, and my romantic relationships.

### Relationship with Self

By trying to meet my mother’s body size expectations, I developed low self-esteem regarding my body image. I have never believed I am beautiful and hate myself when I gain weight. Having hated the workouts my mother required of me, I grew to also despise exercise. So, while I have a poor body image, I do not want to work out because I do not like working out based on my past negative experiences with running. I also do not want to exercise as I feel I am giving into my mother’s expectations again. There is a dichotomy inside of me, and my low self-esteem about my body appears to be a never-ending cycle of disliking my size and not wanting to give in to expectations.

While striving to meet my mother’s expectations, I became a perfectionist which has caused anxiety. In my life, I strive to finish everything flawlessly and be a model person for others. In my academics, I try to finish all my assignments and have my work be faultless. I am very organized and ensure to maintain this organization in order to continue being “perfect.” Setting high expectations for myself also creates anxiety as I strive to meet unrealistic goals. Though I have come to realize perfection is not obtainable and need to lower my expectations, I start to become conflicted with myself. One part of me believes I need to not have such high expectations for myself, but the other part of me still believes I should reach the perfection goal. When I fall short of meeting my goals, I feel disappointed in myself and feel as though I am not trying my hardest. My mother’s expectations instilled in me low self-esteem regarding my body image and created my perfectionist personality which has caused my relationship with myself to be conflicting.

### Friendships

By trying to live up to my mother’s expectations, I became a people-pleaser. My need to please others has affected many of my friendships as I did not realize how to create healthy boundaries. Sometimes I will say or do things to try to have other people like me. Once I realize that I am uncomfortable with what I have agreed to, I discontinue the relationship. Many of my relationships in high school followed this pattern. I had a pattern of giving into peer pressure to not cause conflict with others. Over time, my perfectionist personality trait would notice that these friends were not helping me succeed and fix the issue, and I would end the friendships. I remember a group of friends who liked to go to parties, and I agreed to go party with them. After reflecting on multiple parties, I realized I did not want to party anymore and cut off these relationships completely. My relationship with my mother caused me to become a people-pleasing perfectionist which have shown to affect my ability to maintain friendships.

Many times, my people-pleasing personality has caused others to take advantage of me. Once I realize I have made friends with people who take advantage of me, the friendship usually ends with a cutoff of communication. A close friend I had in college took advantage of me emotionally. She would tell me everything she was struggling with in her life but never cared about my feelings or listened to me. When I realized my needs were not being fulfilled in the relationship, I told her, and she ended our friendship. Trying to please my mother started my trend of trying to please other people, which has made making and keeping friends difficult for me.

It took me a long time to find a friendship where I was not taken advantage of and now that I have these relationships, I realize how valuable they are. I have recognized the issue of my people pleasing personality and can see the root of the issue, but it is difficult to change a habit that has been instilled in me for many years, especially when you move to a new environment. Having recently moved to a new city, I have found it difficult to not fall into previous comfortable habits, which learned from my relationship with my mom, while becoming accustomed to my new environment and making new friends.

### Romantic Relationships

My mother’s expectation regarding my body size has also followed me into my marriage. My low self-esteem regarding my size sometimes makes me feel as though I am not beautiful enough, and it is something my husband and I continue to talk through.

## Conclusion

My mother’s family rules communicated to me created conflicts in our relationship; while I made efforts to help resolve our conflict, the effects of my mother’s expectations during my childhood and adolescence influenced various relationships in my life later. Through exploring my conflict with my mother, I hope to be more prepared to be a therapist by understanding possible countertransference. While the initial purpose of this analysis was to become a better therapist, I feel I learned more about my relationship with my mother through this process. I encourage others who may have conflictual relationships to explore the history of the relationship. I learned a lot about my personality traits and roots to their existence, and others may find their own information through their analysis.
