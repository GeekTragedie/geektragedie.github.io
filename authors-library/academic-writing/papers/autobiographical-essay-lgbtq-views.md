Throughout my lifetime, there are numerous aspects that have influenced my view of the LGBTQ community, but there are two aspects that I can pin point to have the most influence: past experiences and spiritual growth. The past experiences in my life that influence my view of the LGBTQ community can be split into two different categories: positive and negative influences. One of the negative influences I have experiences is my small-town South Dakota high school. The sex education at my school is very lacking. They only teach abstinence and do not discuss sex at all. With this education, no one in my school was taught about sexual minorities. The school is very conservative, so with there being no sex education, there certainly was no education regarding gender minorities. I was not introduced to gender minorities until I starting going to school at USD and had a friend explain “bathroom bills” to me.

Another negative influence in my life is my step dad, a conservative catholic. My step dad believes in the gender binary and strongly frowns upon sexual minorities. Due to Target’s bathroom policy, my step dad refuses to shop there. Luckily, I was never close to my step dad until I graduated high school and had moved out of the house. If I had been educated on gender and sexual minorities in high school, I may have had more conversations with family members regarding them. Maybe it was best that I was not introduced to these minority groups until later in life. One final negative influence I had early-on in my life was the boyfriend I had senior year of high school. He was a conservative Christian […] and very set in his beliefs. One of my best friends in high school […] came out after high school as gay. My boyfriend at the time certainly had homophobia and told me he refused to see this friend as a person. My boyfriend believed my friend’s “sinful ways” were against the bible and he was not allowed to acknowledge those who live in sin. I was utterly shocked by this view and rebutted his claims many times. I had been close to this friend [for years], and one boyfriend was not going to change my views on this friend. I am happy that I am no longer with him, but really wish we had broken up sooner. A positive view came out of this experience. I created my own point of view on gay and lesbian couples and argued that they were just like everyone else. While my boyfriend continued to argue for heterosexism, I refused to conform to these beliefs and created my own opinion for one of the first times.

Even though I had this negative experience with my ex, there have certainly been positive past experiences that influence my views of the LGBTQ community. My best friend from high school certainly helped me accept sexual minorities. The experiences that he had to go through with his family and acquaintances, like my ex, made me view sexual minorities as people and not just a concept. My friend had to hide his sexual orientation due to his […] family. He was scared of his families possible enacted stigma, fearing his family would kick him out of the house if he came out. My friend had to deal with people at school making fun of him not acting masculine and people not viewing him as a real person. The fact that he made it through these experience is so empowering. I have not the slightest idea the struggles he went through and deeply respect them. Knowing what my friend been through, I would never wish those struggles upon anyone. I think it is ridiculous that people are not accepted and have to go through those struggles. Coming to college, I have more friends that are sexual minorities. Through these friends, I find it impossible for myself to never accept sexual minorities into the public. As for gender minorities, I never was close to someone that identified as a gender minority until my senior year of college. Once I heard my friend’s struggle stories caused by him being a gender minority, my feelings towards this minority aligned with my feelings with my friend from high school. People from sexual and gender minorities deserve to be treated as those who are part of the majority group.

One aspect that is important in my life is my Christianity and it is important for this part of my life to line up with my values. I have had multiple experiences where Christian people say that sexual minorities are against the bible: one with my ex, one with a good friend of mine, and one at a campus Cru meeting. I had a discussion in high school with a close friend of mine and he told me his views on sexual minorities. He said that people should still be friends and accept those who are living in sin (his view of sexual and gender minority groups), but then try to show them God’s light and change them so they can go to heaven. I had a similar experience with a Cru meeting I went to just this semester. The leader said that Christians have been treating the LGBTQ community terribly and that they should be more accepting. Christians need to show the community God and bring them to God. This idea is how Cru feels towards all people, so I was happy with this message, until the end. The final message of the talk was that we need to lead the LGBTQ community to God and then let God change them. This final word was discouraging to me, but I guess it is what the speaker believes. I believe this view is a step in the right direction for Christians though.

While it appears Christians are against the LGBTQ community, the two certainly do connect and there are sexual and gender minority Christians. Church has always been important to my family, especially since my grandpa is a […] pastor. After my experience with my ex in high school, I know from my personal feelings accepting sexual and gender minorities is the right thing to do. These feelings were further confirmed through my friendships, classes, and God. The more I learn about the LGBTQ community, the stronger I feel love for the community and all people in general. Due to my love growing, it shows God’s love in everything. Many Christians say that sexual minorities are sinful and people are born physically perfect the way God made them, referring to gender minorities. I questioned the interpretation of these people who believe these things. God does create everyone the way they are, including their sexual orientation and gender identity. Humans do not have the right to judge and tell others what is right and what is wrong in the eyes of God. I do not normally feel God in my everyday life, but I have received confirmation about my beliefs towards sexual and gender minorities. It is difficult to express these feelings, but I will attempt to explain them to the best of my ability. During a song at church, I thought about my religious struggle with accepting the LGBTQ community due to what other Christians have said. God calmed my heart and made me feel strong and proud about being an ally to the LGBTQ community. The second confirmation I have received was through a bible verse. At a Cru meeting, we were told to take a bible verse sticky note off the back wall, look it up the verse, and think about how the verse applies to our lives. The verse I was given was one of Jesus’ final commandments saying to love your neighbor as yourself. With this being the final idea Jesus wanted to pass on to earth, it truly must be the most important. Accepting everyone is the one thing that people are not doing on this earth, and Christians are part of the source of the hatred. More Christians need to follow this commandment and stop hating people based on their sexual orientation and gender identity. God is the one that loves and judges; humans cannot understand His feelings.

Overall, these experiences with the LGBTQ community have shaped who I am as a person and how I view other people. Through the lack of knowledge I received in high school, I was able to realize how little diversity small towns in South Dakota have. I do not plan to stay in South Dakota for graduate school, and I knew that I needed to expand my knowledge base before moving away. Realizing I lacked information about minority groups, I have sought out opportunities to increase my knowledge. I am taking Psychology of Sexual Orientation and Gender Diversity, Psychology of Social Prejudice, and Arts in Healthcare: Multiculturalism. While I may never personally experience the struggles racial, sexual, and gender minorities go through, I have sought out the information to try and be knowledgeable about these groups.

Through my experiences with my ex-boyfriend, I have realized how cruel the world can be. Before this encounter, I used to go along with whatever other people believed. I never really had my own stance on what seemed, at the time, to be political issues. Through this experience with my ex, I learned to stand up for what I believe is right, no matter who else disagrees. Keeping my beliefs has helped me create my own identity separate from other people. Becoming an ally to the LGBTQ community was the first step to creating my own set of beliefs and creating my own identity.

The biggest impact I believe I have seen through my experience learning about the LGBTQ community is my ability to be open-minded. I think a lot of people in my life are very close-minded. I have learned to think critically about their views and then create my own beliefs. By learning more about the LGBTQ community, whether it be from class or through my friends in college, I continue to learn about struggling experience that face this community. As new people and experiences enter my life, I will continue to be open-minded and learn from my own and others’ experiences. Learning about the LGBTQ community has certainly opened my eyes and I am grateful.
