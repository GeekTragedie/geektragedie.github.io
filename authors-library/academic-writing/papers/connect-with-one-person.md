I facilitated a group in my Improvisation & Creative Dramatics course on Monday, May 20, 2019. The group comprised of the leader, me; an instructor; and seven students. I was the last of four facilitations that day, and I led three improvisation games, Emotional Fruit Salad (expertvillage, 2008), Getting Connected, and Yes, Because (Carrane & Allen, 2006). Following these games, I used a mindfulness technique as closure to the session; the outline for the facilitation can be found in Appendix A. This paper contains process notes for my facilitation, including what happened and thoughts about what happened, and what I learned through the facilitation process.

## What I Learned

Through the process of creating and facilitating, I learned a lot about therapeutic goals, being flexible with facilitations, and the importance of participant feedback. The most important item I learned from this facilitation is that I am most comfortable facilitating if I have led the game in the past.

### Therapeutic Goals

I have learned that I struggle with therapeutic goals when they are applied in hypothetical situations. There are many therapeutic goals that can be applied to clients, and without a specific person or group deciding on what their goals are, I have a difficult time deciding what to do. I know many improvisation games and multiple goals can be applied to a single game. I was easily overwhelmed. When looking at the incarcerated population, I decided to choose interpersonal communication partially because it was a goal on the institutional level (Frisch & Emery, n.d.), but it was also viewed as a positive outcome in drama therapy research with incarcerated populations (Moller, 2013; Koch et al., 2015).

I felt my therapeutic goals were achieved when I reflected on the engagement in the room and the feedback I received after facilitating. While leading the group and engaging with members, I witnessed people connect with each other and felt connected with others. For example, I felt [two group members] got closer to each other when they enacted a scene together and then the scene was recalled during another game, which makes me feel they made a connection to each other through their scene. While being joyful with [the instructor], I felt as though I connected to him in a different way than I have before and felt closer to him. I cannot speak for the participants, but I felt the exercises used in the facilitation helped achieve the goal of creating interpersonal connection with the group members.

### Flexibility

I learned to incorporate flexibility by listening to the group’s needs while facilitating. I first had to be flexible when I learned four people were absent from the group, making for an odd number of participants for my activities. During my facilitation, I had to change some instructions due to the odd number and the reactions of group members. When I felt [a group member] was uncomfortable during Emotional Fruit Salad, I was flexible in changing my facilitation style to compensate for the negative feelings that arose. Participating during this exercise challenged my ability to focus on other group interactions to reflect on later, but I felt it was a necessary adjustment. I also changed the instruction for Getting Connected by welcoming a group of three in the space, though I did not know how it would turn out.

I was under the assumption that I would run out of time during my facilitation, so when I had five minutes of facilitation time left at the end of Getting Connected, I spontaneously added an exercise to the end. I felt confident in my abilities to be flexible during my facilitation. I have gotten feedback in the past regarding my lack of flexibility, so it is a skill I have paid attention to in myself for a while. I am proud reflecting on my ability to “go with the flow” during this facilitation process.

### Feedback

I gained a new perspective on my rap and the exercises I chose from the feedback I received after my facilitation. I learned that verbiage is very important during the rap because there was a backlash to me using the word “forfeit” regarding the mask. While I was reading the rap, I personally felt the word was too strong as soon as I said it and others agreed with their feedback. I was also told that it was comforting that I asked members to at least connect with one other person in the group.

Multiple people felt two contradictory feelings after my facilitation, which was interesting to me. I hypothesize that these feelings were initiated by the Emotional Fruit Salad exercise when members were quickly changing emotions. I was told that this game can create negative connections between people. Because I started the game with silly (which I did intentionally), the rest of the emotions had a playful connection; however, the emotional connections between people can be negative if they only connect via negative emotions. It is important for me to keep this in mind when using this exercise in the future.

## Conclusion

Paying attention to multiple participants at once takes skill to create clear process notes after sessions. I hope to continue practicing process notes in my future training. There are many learning opportunities when creating a 20-minute facilitation and leading a group through activities. New points of view from group members can help a facilitator learn more about the exercises, and I want to incorporate this feedback into facilitations in the future.

## Appendix A

Rap – Society has a nice systemic way of boxing people up and hiding them away. This can create a lack of trust you have with other people, so sometimes you have to put on a mask to help you fend for yourself, which is a great coping mechanism. Today, I invite you all to try to peek out from behind your mask all together and witness others show what’s behind their masks. Forfeiting the mask may allow you some freedom to explore and connect with another person. As you peek from your mask and see others do the same, note that some frustration may arise and that is alright, embrace it. We have the next 20 minutes together, so let’s try to connect with at least one other person and make the best of it.
