## Art Therapy Experiential Reflection

Art Therapy (AT) is a form of Creative Arts Therapy (CAT) that uses various modalities of art materials to lead participants in creating a visual art form while working towards a specific goal the art therapist may have in mind. Understanding AT is important for me wanting to become a Drama Therapist because visual arts can be used during a therapy session to possible move into a drama or open up a client. As a Drama Therapist, I need to be aware of the different modalities used by other therapists to gain a better understanding of the therapy field. Through this paper, I reflect upon my experience participating in an Art Therapy experiential.

## Experiential Activity

On January 31, 2019, a professor in the Art Therapy program at Antioch University Seattle lead the CAT class through an AT directive experimenting with various art materials. During the experiential activity, participants were asked to use various art materials on a large piece of paper to create one art piece. There were thirteen different media used and participants passed the media around the circle, using each item for five minutes. The facilitator also invited participants to not use a medium if the participant did not feel as though the material would work in their art piece.

## Personal Experience

This AT experiential created in me an overall calmness due to its ritualistic aspect. The facilitator did not change the format of the experience and continued to use the same statements in the same increments of time, which helped me create a routine in my art creation. I felt encouraged to explore in a safe space with a safe format and never felt worried.

Calm was one of many emotions elicited during this experiential including, but not limited to: anger, curiosity, joy, and frustration. I found many of my emotions started from the colors I was deciding to use, which seemed to also result from the medium I used. I felt calmer at the beginning and ending of the experiential because I was working with purple and blue tones and found a rhythm that felt like water in my body. I felt tense and angry when I used red and orange tones which I may have led me to working with black. I was so tense using the black oil pastel that I put a lot of muscle and force into one long line on the page. Reflecting on this moment brings back the anger and tension in my body, making me feel uncomfortable. Color tends to have a large impact on a person’s mood and state of mind.

My instance of joy caused me to be the most engaged during the art activity. I was using watercolors and experimenting with how the color showed up while varying the saturation of paint on the brush. I then realized if the brush contained more water, it dripped. I started to play with flicking the paint onto my brush and became engrossed in the joy of the experiment and pigment. I became unaware of the brush, however. The head of my brush flung off and landed on another participant’s artwork. I suddenly felt embarrassed and realized how engrossed I was in the activity. I quickly retrieved my brush head and felt challenged to continue my joyful experience while being more aware of my materials. In my life, I can become engrossed in my own project that I need to remind myself to be aware of my actions and thoughts. I am sure there are times when my actions, like my paint brush, may impact others in a way I do not intend.

I felt uneasy and rather overwhelmed at the beginning of the experiential when I was given a large piece of paper. I tend to draw doodles and small items that do not take up space. I became overwhelmed with the large amount of space I was expected to fill up. The feeling of being overwhelmed came back towards the end of the experiential when the facilitator stated we had only five more rotations of materials left. I asked myself where the feeling of being overwhelmed was coming from and was then passed crayons. The crayons themselves and their smell reminded me of when I was younger and used crayons in coloring books. I was reminded when my mom would tell me to not leave “white monsters” on my pages and to make sure to color the whole page. I then used the crayons on my art piece to color in any white space left on the paper, which then decreased my feeling of being overwhelmed. I can connect this feeling of being overwhelmed and “white monsters” to other aspects in my life, such as starting a new project that may have many aspects to consider and making sure to fulfill the project to the best of my abilities, leaving no “white monsters” for others to notice.

## Conclusion

Taking part in the AT experiential certainly helped me learn about myself as well as how art can be used for therapeutic goals. It was particularly beneficial having the facilitator inviting participants to consider applying various goals to the project and how the goal may change the experiential. Using the various media helped teach me more about materials as well as how I react to different media. As a future Drama Therapist, I need to remember how different media and colors can impact participants’ feelings.
