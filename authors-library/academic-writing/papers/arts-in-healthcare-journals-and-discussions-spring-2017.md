I need to think about these things in my own life when I am deciding what to do in any given situation.

As for ethics for me as an Arts in Health Professional, it is important for me to realize the written codes of ethics for wherever I work. While there are overarching ethics codes created by society, it is important to review the specific codes held at for the facility I may be working in. Understanding the terminology of these codes is also important. I believe learning ethics takes a lot of time and practice. I need to learn as much as I can, book wise, about ethics so that I understand things as I go into the world. Learning things from the codes and books is one thing, but experience is another. Doing a scenario in a book does not have to deal with real consequences and real feelings, this because a different argument when one enters the world and has real peoples’ lives in their hands. The idea of possibly doing something ethically wrongs is scary to imagine and I really do not want to find myself getting the wrong consequences from an ethical perspective.

We certainly need all the different ethical codes. Practices are very diverse in their modalities, with different practices, different issues are going to arise. Every association should write their own code of ethics because they are the specialists in their field. They know what issues may arise and they know what could ethically go wrong. The NAPT is going to have different ethic codes regarding physical contact with clients that the NADTA because a drama therapist may use different movement and have different interactions than a poetry therapist.

Ethical codes should, in no way, be thrown out. There needs to be an ethical standard for people to be held to because of the diversity of ethical feelings people may have. A person that grows up in a family where being racist is seen as a norm needs to have a code of ethics that makes them accept those who are a different race or else there will be discrimination issues. Discrimination is against the codes of ethics. It is important for no discrimination to be allowed in any practice and these codes of ethics do not allow for it. This example is just one reason why we cannot rid of ethics codes all together.

## Journal 2

*6 February 2017*

**How can our difference in political views help and/or harm us?**

Different political views can help people through working together. People with different views have different personalities and values. It is important to acknowledge that people have different points of view and when working with someone that has a different view, they help you expand your knowledge of their thinking. If two people see the other’s views and work together, they can use their different personalities and values to enhance their ability to help others. Like the video we watched in class, conservatives want things to stay more the same and liberals are more open minded and wanting things to change. Conservatives need to level out the liberals. If things continually change, there is not enough room for organization. People need organization for things to happen. Conservatives add organization to liberal’s need for change and this is important.

Different political views harms people because it creates two in-groups. In-groups create a norm and treat people that are not in their group differently. People having different political views can affect how they treat others. Many people take their political views to heart and feel as though if someone else disagrees with their political views, they don’t like that person in general. These feelings can harm us and how we feel we are perceived by the world.

**How would you address political views in an AIH session?**

If I noticed someone may have different political views as mine, I would probably ask people about their views and where their views came from so I could better understand why that person has these beliefs. I think it important to understand where people are coming from. It is important, however, to not let that person’s beliefs have an effect on others’ freedoms. Once this line is crossed, it is important to keep both parties in mind and time to create a strategic solution.

**How does our lecture/discussion today, impact your views personally and as an AIH practitioner?**

These discussions helped me see others’ views as simply different because of peoples’ backgrounds. Just people someone’s views are different does not mean they are wrong. There are certainly aspects to people’s beliefs that I will fully disagree with, but it is important to keep their own constitutional rights in mind as well. This lecture also helped me remember to keep a possible aggressor’s ideas in mind when doing AIH. This view was seen when talking about a person possibly wanting to draw a swastika on a community art piece. It is important for everyone to do art and the experience should not be taken away from anyone, even if their views do not fully line up with mine.

## Journal 5

*27 February 2017*

It is important to understand different generation’s experiences because it can improve the work of an Arts in Healthcare Practitioner. The best way to understand this is to look at certain categories and see each generation’s perspective of that category. In the table “Generational Differences,” there are four generations: Builders, Boomers, Busters, and Bridgers. One category that is viewed over the generations that is important to remember how generations view their families. Builders have a high view of family and believe in marriage honor; Boomers view family as divorce increases; busters’ view of family is non-existent, nuisance, and dysfunctional, and bridgers have a new respect for family. When working with these generations, it is important to see how they see their family. Not every sees their family the same and when doing theatre with different people, they will not view family lives the same. This being said, different generations of populations will play out families differently and view a performance of a family differently.

It is also very important to see what morals the different generations have. Builders believe in puritan ethics, boomers have sensual morals; busters have cautious morals and bridgers are tolerant. When working with people, it is important to have their morals in mind. Builders will have more traditional beliefs and will want those to be honor while bridgers will be more tolerant of how things are done. When working with these populations, you want to monitor the content of the performance or work to include these morals. Also knowing these morals when seeing work done by these generations.

One final idea to keep in mind is characteristics that are important to the different generations. When working with builders, it is important to be a hard worker and be dependable because that is what they see as important. Boomers are focused on education, fitness, and being independent. Letting them do their own work would be important and not belittling them and making sure they believe they are right. Busters are highly spiritual, loyal to relationships, and can be stressed out. When working with this population, it is important to honor their relationships and respect their spirituality. It is also important to be organized to make sure that people aren’t stressed during the art.

## Journal 7

*20 March 2017*

It is imperative to keep the history of Native American populations in mind when working with people in this population. Knowing what white people have done to this population is very important, because it can explain a lot of behaviors and interactions they have with you. Native Americans carry with them historical trauma. This is passed on from elders through word of mouth, but also effects the population biologically. Elders in a Native American family may warn their children to beware of other races, specifically white people, because of our past relationships with them. They may warn their children to not trust white people because of our suppressing nature and behavior.  It is important when working with this population to understand that you need to earn their trust. Do not force yourself into their lives, you need to get them to trust you. It is also important to note that you cannot simply change this population’s mind when it comes to white people. It is in their biology to not trust white people. Their cortisol levels are not the same as white people and they cannot simply change the way their body works. This will change possible modalities that I will use because I may need to ease this population into trusting me as a AIH-P. It may take more time to do introductory work with this population than it would others. This means, when creating a program, it is important to keep this in mind.

<p class="piece-note">Looking back: I’d put this differently now. Distrust like this isn’t biological; it’s a reasonable response to historical and intergenerational trauma, which research on epigenetics suggests can echo across generations.</p>

When working with adolescents in juvenile detention centers (JDCs), two positive supports that are important are leaving your problems at the door and listening to what they are saying. By leaving problems you may be having in your life outside of the AIH program, you can interact with the adolescents genuinely and not be distracted with what is happening outside. By leaving your problems at the door, you are less likely to use the wrong emotions and get angry easily. With adolescents, they are not the easiest to work with. Getting angry and frustrated about something easily, because of something that may be happening outside of the JDC, can break trust that has been created or cause adolescents to not participate. These things can hinder the progress of the AIH programing with this population. It is also vital to listen to what the adolescents are saying. This population feels as though they have been outcast from society and not cared about. Julia Taylor says that theatre helps give this population a voice and helps them feel free. If someone isn’t listening to them and supporting their voice, the person is going to be viewed like the rest of society. This will hinder trust and break the relationship that is growing. The youth may not want to participate and may not put effort towards the program because they feel they are not being heard. When listening, it is also important to comprehend and then respond, finish that communication aspect.

## Journal

*29 March 2017*

I can use the brain as in AIH-P because it helps me understand the capabilities of the populations I would be working with that might have had negative experiences with the brain. Knowing that someone has had a brain injury will help me look at how to best assist them with activities and adapt activities to best fit their needs. If I know someone has had injury in their temporal lobe and I am doing a musical activity, maybe I add visuals to see how the music is moving or add paint on the top of drums to watch the paint splatter (which would make a huge mess, but sounds fun!).  If I know someone has issues with their occipital lobe and I am doing a visual art project, maybe doing something that is less particular with things being placed in a specific way or doing something that is connected directly to touch, like finger painting (just being sure to steer away from coloring in the lines or having to view something a specific way). I just need to be sure to keep in mind what aspects are connected to different parts of the brain.

Some projects that I can use that will engage the entire brain are the painting drums I listed above. You can put paint on the drums, then hit the drums with your hands and put your hands on a mural of some sort. The music has a specific rhythm that needs to be calculated (using the left hemisphere) while the drums have the paint that can then be used to create the mural (using the right hemisphere).

Another project that can engage the entire brain would be to bring in a Shakespeare company to perform. The audience would take in the creativity of the movement that the actors are portraying and also have to interpret what the actors are saying, needing critical thinking. If the actors are doing a good job, however, audiences should be able to understand the story without understanding the words. It just takes a critical mind to then interpret what the actions mean.
