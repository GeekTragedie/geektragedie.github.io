# Writing Process Reflection: A Letter to Dr. Davis

**Degree / Program:** MA in Mental Health Counseling  
**Course / Context:** Writing in Psychology (Rebecca Davis, Ph.D.), Antioch University Seattle  
**Date:** December 4, 2018  

---

<p class="piece-note">The end-of-course process reflection, written as a letter to the professor. The mailing address has been removed.</p>

December 4, 2018

Rebecca Davis  
Writing in Psychology Professor  
Antioch University Seattle

Dear Dr. Davis:

Thank you very much for taking your time to teach Writing in Psychology at Antioch University Seattle. I became inspired in your course to improve my writing abilities and am blessed to have had the opportunity to learn from you. I started the course feeling discouraged from my placement in the class; however, I learned a lot about myself as a writer and increased my writing confidence. Many lessons I learned in this course resulted from the journaling activities, the critical conversations assignment, the discourse community discussion, and revising my research essay.

Through the process of journaling, I increased my writing confidence and learned to write freely. The journal activities towards the beginning of the quarter made me realize I was too hard on myself as a writer. The free writing expressed my internal voice and then allowed my critical mind to realize I needed to change my writing process. My previous writing style was closeminded and not willing to explore new ideas in theses. By freely writing, my internal critic was suppressed, allowing my personal, creative voice to speak.

Before this class, I did not consider stream of consciousness to be beneficial to my writing process, feeling it was a waste of time. Through journaling in this class, however, I have been able to explore more ideas and write freely, which helped me develop the thesis of my research paper. The reflection done outside of class regarding our process as a writer helped me step away from my work and think about what excited me in my writing. Realizing my excitement gave me insight to what I was passionate about in my writing and fueled my motivation to continue. I plan to continue journaling in my academic career to allow more of my stream of consciousness to be voiced.

The critical conversations exercise taught me an important writing skill regarding using sources in papers and developing critical thinking skills as a reader. In my undergraduate courses, I avoided paraphrasing and summarizing information for fear of plagiarism or misinterpreting the authors’ meaning. The critical conversations exercise allowed me to embrace paraphrasing and summarizing while instilling my own voice into the discourse, and I now feel more comfortable integrating outside sources into my writing without direct quotes.

The critical conversations assignment also helped me develop critical thinking skills as a reader by considering my thoughts on a topic. I was encouraged to reflect on what I read and wrestle with the information. I asked myself questions regarding the subject matter and increased my self-awareness on the subjects I reflected upon. This assignment well prepared me to contribute to my future discourse communities by finding their voice and opinions.

My awareness of academic audiences increased after the class discussion and article regarding discourse communities. I realized writing in APA style helps me communicate to my discourse community in a way that will increase my credibility as a writer. I also am more aware the discourse community I would like to be a part of and can learn their language to incorporate into my writing to better communicate my point of view. For my writing to be taken seriously, I must take the time and effort to understand my community’s values. The exercise in class helped me recognize which discourse communities I want to participate in, so I can do future research on the member’s values, language, and ethical standards. This research will help me in my academic and professional development.

Revising my research paper taught me to incorporate passion and take criticism, which are important to my future academic development. When deciding on a topic for my research, I was encouraged to write about a subject that interests me. During the research process, I found myself enjoying the articles, and the research did not feel dull. I became motivated to write the best I could and deliver my ideas effectively. For the first time, making major revisions to my paper did not feel tedious, and I wanted to make time for large changes. I plan to continue writing about topics I am passionate about to increase my motivation and passion for my work.

The revision stage was challenging for me because I have been known to be defensive when talking about my writing. I have tried to take criticism well, but writing is very personal to me. Reflecting on the feedback I received throughout the semester, I enjoy developing my paper topics to clarify understanding; however, I need more practice phrasing my questions regarding my feedback to not sound defensive. I am happy to have realized what piece of me can work on taking constructive criticism, and I look forward to applying this awareness in future situations.

I learned more in this course than I initially imagined, from increasing my knowledge of writing and developing my self-awareness. Dr. Davis, you offer support to your students and care about each student individually, which is valuable and encouraging. Please do not stop teaching and inspiring students to reach their full potential because the world needs more professors like you.

Sincerely,

GeekTragedie  
AUS Student, CMHC/DT
