<p class="paper-title"><strong>A Struggle with Stigma</strong></p>

The Merriam-Webster dictionary defines stigma as “a set of negative and often unfair beliefs that a society or group of people have about something” (Stigma). There are many groups American society stigmatizes: men, women, Syrian refugees, LGBTQ community, and people with mental illnesses. It is important for people to realize how huge of an impact stigmatization really causes. The web log (blog) “Life Without Hurt” is written by a woman who suffers from Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) and Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) caused by her childhood experience with mother-daughter sexual abuse (MDSA). The woman has finally escaped her life of abuse at the age of twenty-nine and has started a life of her own. It is admirable to see such a woman have the strength to leave the abuser’s manipulative hands and move on; there are downsides, however. Through the blog, it is evident that this woman suffers the stigma brought on to her from the outside world. Her struggle with stigma is caused by her mother, but also enhanced by society being both under-informed about MDSA and lacking concrete knowledge about DID.

The woman in the blog first struggles with the stigma from her mother, who is also the cause of her PTSD due to MDSA. Her mother is described in the blog as “a pathetical liar” who manipulates those around her without their knowledge (Anonymous, 2015). While the writer has come to this realization, she still does not catch some of the internal ideas that have been reinforced in her by her family. While talking to her therapist, the writer realized that she should probably not use the word crazy to describe herself because her therapist would not like it. Then she thought about why she used the word crazy and realized her “mother liked to use that word to describe [her] to everybody” (Anonymous, 2015). She was stigmatized by her mother to being crazy, so she assumed she was. Her mother would also tell people she “was Bipolar and had a lying disorder” (Anonymous, 2015). The mother’s lie made people around the blogger treat her differently. She describes that at her old job, people were told by the writer’s mother about her being mentally ill, so then people who “knew [her] situation” then was able to make up for work that may not have been finished when she was struggling with her disorders (Anonymous, 2015).

The blogger then starts to worry about her new job in her new life by thinking that “no one is going to understand what is happening” if she were to “have a flashback while at work” (Anonymous, 2015). She believes that she is “going to end up getting fired” (Anonymous, 2015). The only real reason she believes these ideas is because her mother has reinforced the idea that she is crazy and manipulated people in her life into believing that the writer is someone she is not. This manipulation created a specific world for the blogger. This world was full of people treating the writer differently because of her mother’s created stigma. Not only has the manipulation changed those around her, she also now lives in constant fear that her mother will find her and start abusing her again.

The writer also struggles with society’s stigma due to their lack of knowledge about mother-daughter sexual abuse (MDSA). In the blog, the writer discusses how important to her it is that people understand MDSA. While she finds it easier to tell people that she suffers from PTSD because it is more well-known, she does not want to tell people it originated from MDSA. She devotes an entire blog post explaining what MDSA; she hesitatingly says “mother-daughter sexual abuse… by this point, I’d be surprised to have any readers left…You have made it farther than most in our society have” (Anonymous, 2015). She had to come to her own realization that she was affected by MDSA because many people in society write it off as “*Oh, but she’s your mother. She didn’t mean it like that”* (Anonymous, 2015). An example of society not taking her abuse seriously is found through her experience getting hospitalized. She had “revealed that [her] mother was [her] abuser, the nurse made a face and asked [her] ‘Are you a lesbian? Is your mother a lesbian?’” (Anonymous, 2015). While in the blog she expresses that she is “fully supportive of the LGBT community,” she is tired of people misunderstanding her traumatic experience as having to do with her sexuality (Anonymous, 2015). These experiences are the reason why it is difficult for her to tell people that she suffers from PTSD; some people do not believe that her traumatic experience could actually be seen as traumatic. The writer’s experience with people not fully understanding her MDSA has caused her to have to live a life scared that people will judge her for her experiences and write them off as non-existent.

Finally, the writer discusses her difficulty accepting her diagnosis with Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) because of the stigma that has been created by society. She describes receiving her diagnosis in this way: “Then she said the words I’ve dreaded: Dissociative Identity Disorder” (Anonymous, 2015). Her reaction to his is: “I’ve done a pretty good job at trying to hide it from people because I didn’t want the diagnosis… I’m disappointed in myself. I lost control of myself” (Anonymous, 2015). She is so dissatisfied with having DID that she blames herself for not being able to control it, but the disorder is something that one cannot simply control. She does not want DID because “there is no cure. DID won’t go away with a pill” (Anonymous, 2015). There is even a stigma on DID from people in the psychology profession: “A lot of therapists won’t even acknowledge its existence and therefore won’t treat it” (Anonymous, 2015). The negative stigma caused by the lack of knowledge about DID makes it very difficult to accept the diagnosis.

The writer constantly has to struggle to not dissociate in public, but she has an alternate personality inside of her, a seven year old girl [name withheld] that “likes Sesame Street and doggies” (Anonymous, 2015). This personality is self-destructive in the way of scratching her skin until it bleeds because she believes she needs to punish herself for all the wrong things she has done. The writer has to constantly think about how to please [the alter] and sets time aside during the week to do things that [the alter] would like so that [the alter] is happy. This time and attention can add a stigma from other people because if they found out about the alternate personality, people may steer away from having to deal with this concept. There is always a stigma towards something that people do not fully understand. If someone does not understand why a person has a different personality or how to, as some people would say, “fix” the problem, then people do not want to deal with it.

Overall, this blogger’s live is difficult because of the stigma she has received from society and also the abuse from her mother. She constantly has to live in fear of her mother possibly returning because she suffers from flashbacks due to her PTSD. She also has to deal with the negative stigma people have towards the existence of MDSA. The blogger also has to deal with making her alternate personality happy, but has to try and hide it from society as well because people stigmatize what they do not understand. It is very important for people to realize how strong this woman is for leaving the situation she was in and starting a new life of independence. Not only is she new to living on her own, but she is also has to deal with two difficult disorders caused by her MDSA. It is surely admirable to hear such a story and people need to educate themselves on disorders because people such as this should not be disregarded or ignored, they should be honored and admired.
