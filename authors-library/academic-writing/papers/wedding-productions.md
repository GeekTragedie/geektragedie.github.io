<p class="paper-title">Wedding Productions</p>

It is a western societal expectation that girls are supposed to start envisioning their weddings at a young age. Media, costumes, and toys influence the ideas that these young girls create for their future selves. With the impact from society, girls are raised to believe that weddings are to be elaborate, elegant events that are supposed to be the highlight of their life. Whatever women have planned in their minds is expected to happen, and all of those idea have to be executed perfectly, because it only happens once in a girl’s life. Throughout the years, the expectations for weddings have increased and become more extravagant. With these expectations attempting to be achieved, weddings have become more equivalent to theatrical productions because of their planning, setup, and execution. The process of wedding preparation can be related to that of a theatre production due to the roles that are expected to be played and the audience coming to attend the ritual. Theatre productions, like weddings, have also seen an increase in their expectations as audiences expect to see more spectacle and elaboration. Using the roles needed to create a theatre production as a comparison to the roles required to effectively produce a wedding, one can see that western society has unrealistic expectations for the bride to meet, causing women to be stressed and possibly over controlling, which is viewed as the bridezilla effect.

Before relating the roles a bride needs to possess and how these relate to the bridezilla effect, it is important to understand how the term “bridezilla” is defined. In *The New York Times,* Kelsey McKinney writes that weddings must be “stunning, awe-inspiring, love-filled, unique and fun all at once, and… appear to have occurred miraculously, with zero effort or emotional output on the part of the bride” (10). McKinney claims that when brides fail “to live up to the second requirement… A woman will be labeled a ‘bridezilla’” (10). This term seems to only have a negative connotation on brides attempting to plan their wedding to live up to the expectations that the media has set forth. McKinney continues in her article to state that the emotions a woman goes through are normal, and that women only get called a bridezilla when they are being particular with what they want. A bride must be particular in the case of her wedding because if the wedding is not seen as perfect, there can be social consequences that the bride must endure. Weddings are believed to have the “happily ever after” myth attached to them where “the more glorious the wedding, the more perfect and glorious the marriage” (Chesser 204). There is a lot of stress that comes from the bride having to portray the couple’s relationship to others. With the stress, a woman may show emotions that people believe are equivalent to her being over controlling, thus deeming her a bridezilla. In order to rise up to this level of emotion, there are many roles that a bride must take on to ensure that her wedding lives up to the expectations society believes it needs to.

Related to a theatre production, brides are like stage and technical directors in the way that they have to do elaborate research and take on the role of planning for the vision they want to execute. The first task that must be taken on is research. Some of the research is taken into account before a script is selected, such as becoming knowledgeable about the audience’s expectations. It is important for a stage director to understand the community of which the play will take place. It is significant to understand the world in which this production is being inserted into. The bride must also understand the audience’s expectations at a wedding. She must do research as to what needs to be planned for a wedding so that the wedding can live up to the expectations of the audience, those who attend. Barbara Chesser states that “weddings always involve pageantry and ritual” (204) and, knowing that weddings are ritualistic, the bride should know the steps to performing this ritual. Figuring out these rituals takes research. Chesser argues that knowing the meaning behind different rituals helps create a longer, more meaningful marriage. The research done by the bride, however, is influenced by the media and previous experience and so is the expectations of the audience. S. Ambirajan, in their article *Politics of Wedding and Wedding as Politics,* states that “Everything has a political dimension and use. It would be too much to expect our political operators not to use the occasion. Indeed, the socio-political utilization of marriage platforms is nothing new” (3053). A person’s wedding is not only a day for them to remember, it also influences the view of those attending the event. Society’s view of weddings has been constructed through multiple media platforms, such as movies, newspapers, and books. These media outlets help a bride create her overall vision for her wedding but, to create this vision, a bride must plan the aspects that align with her research on this ritual.

Directors and brides can incorporate their research into their productions through planning but, most importantly, planning ensures that the production is performed on time. Both directors and brides have to include research into their planning period first. Then, technical directors must plan when designers need to have their presentation and projects completed while play directors must plan their rehearsal period. After choosing a script, a stage director needs to study the script and become familiar with what is happening in the play along with the references being made. Brides also need to create a plan of action for their wedding, and while the planning and rehearsal period varies for theatre productions, brides create their estimated period for planning. Engagement periods, in the United States, average to be sixteen months, which gives the bride that amount of time to plan the wedding (“Ultimate Wedding”). Wedding plans that can be found online or in phone applications use this average to help a couple vary the duties they need to fulfill before their wedding day. These duties are created based on the ritual research done. Thierry Kochuyt lists some of the wedding preparations: “one has to rent a banquet hall and food has to be served by a catering company or restaurant… There has to be music, photographer -- possibly a limousine -- and the bride needs to visit a beautician; her hair has to be done” (1627). All of these aspects have to be planned ahead of time, because they are expectations of the audience. The plans made by the directors and brides have to also follow their overall vision of their wedding.

When plans are not turning out the way the bride intends them to, the bride can be viewed as a bridezilla as she attempts to solve the issue. A bride has invested a lot of her time and money for her wedding to turn out the way that she has planned. When people do not execute their duties or items do not turn out the way they are supposed to, it ruins the bride’s vision. These items not being executed correctly falls on the bride and her social standing. The stress caused from the bride wanting her plan executed correctly can cause her to be viewed as “cruel or selfish” (McKinney 10) when she is really only trying to make sure others’ expectations are met. When aspects of a play fall apart, a lot of the criticism call on the director as well. If the director is not mentioned, then the director is believed to have done a nice job. On the positive side, when a wedding is done well and is beautiful, the bride is normally congratulated; however, if there are negative criticisms, it is normally kept internal. All of the tasks that need to be accomplished, besides the directing aspects, are normally taken care of by designers.

In a play production, the designers’ roles (costume, scene, prop, sound, and light designers) are split between multiple people. In a small venue or a grade school setting, these roles can sometimes be left to a smaller number of people, but normally the workload is split up. In the case of preparing for a wedding, a bride is in charge of selecting, sometimes designing and / or building, a lot of these elements. When it comes to costumes, a bride sometimes finds or designs the ring, depending on the abilities of their significant other. The bride also has to help find outfits and jewelry for their significant other, bridesmaids, and groomsmen. Normally these items are created or selected by the costume designer for a play production. These costumes have to fit into the world of the play or the world of the wedding.

A bride also has to take on the role of the scene designer for her wedding. The scene designer, in a play production, creates what the setting of the play looks like. A bride does this designing through picking out the venue for the church and the reception. Choosing the correct venue and time is important. If a designer is working in an outside space, there are different aspects that need to be considered when planning to create the set. When planning an outdoor wedding, a bride must plan for any type of weather that can happen, rain or shine, the show must go on. After choosing what the architecture will look like, the bride has to choose the colors that will be used to decorate these spaces. After the colors, the bride plans what decorations she wants and where they should be located, also deeming her the prop designer. Some other scenic props that are chosen by the bride include the flowers and table settings. To cut down on costs due to money constraints, brides have been moving more towards creating bouquets and decorations themselves (McKinney 10). Doing this aligns even more with the duty of a prop designer or a prop master because they have to find what they want the items to look like, figure out a way to create them, and then take the time to put together the creations. There is a lot of thought and time that goes into taking on this task oneself. Normally in a theatre, this task is done by a group of people, led by the prop designer. While this process can be copied by a bride, this is only one of multiple designer jobs that must be taken on.

During a play production, there is a sound designer that chooses what music and sounds fit into the vision the director would like to portray through this particular show. The bride also has to choose these aspects in her wedding. While she can choose songs that sound good to her, the sounds also must match the rest of the design elements and match the expectation of the audience. These sounds also have to match the atmosphere evoked by the lights chosen. There are multiple aspects that have to go into lighting a wedding, such as time of day and angles of light, especially if the wedding is going to take place outdoors. The bride must do research on the time of year, where the sun is located, and the effects of different angles of light when choosing the time and location of her wedding. Lighting elements effect the photos that will be taken of the event and the shadows that may be seen on the faces of the couple. Lighting designers in the theatre discipline understand this concept, because it is their job and they have been professionally trained in the field. Brides, however, have not had this training and may not be aware of all of the lighting aspects.

Anyone has the ability to be a bride and plan a wedding. When it comes to the wedding, women are expected to perform the duties of these designers, but they are not professionally trained. The bride is expected to do these jobs perfectly without training or hire professionals to contribute, but these professionals cost more money to the couple who already has the other expenses to worry about. Therefore, a lot of the design aspects, such as directing, costuming, scenic, props, sound, and lighting design, end up being taken care of by the bride. The bride taking on these roles is expected to execute the job of a professional perfectly, even though they are not specially trained in these fields. These aspects take a lot of time to perform and the bride must accomplish these responsibilities before the wedding while she also must continue her daily routine and work her own job. While keeping up with everyday life as well as attempting to learn how to perform these roles, there seems to be a lack of ability to fully accomplish these responsibilities. It is an unrealistic standard for a bride to fully understand these design aspects and execute them to the expectations an audience has.

Due to the lack of experience in the different design aspects, brides use their research and media influence in order to plan their wedding. By doing this, the power in the wedding field is not in the hands of the bride, but the media and companies that influence brides’ research. Through many brides being influenced by the same aspects of society, brides are only recreating weddings that have been shown to them. Their weddings then influence others that attend the wedding and their expectations of what a wedding is supposed to be. This trend continues, so that every wedding that is performed only reinforces the ritual and eventually nothing becomes original, it is only brought back from a previous performance of the ritual. An example of these traditions that has continued throughout the years is the trend that all couples getting married need to exchange wedding rings. This tradition can be found in “history going back at least two millennia” (Ogden 38). Couples have exchanged rings to symbolize their relationship for so long that it has become a ritual. It has now become expected of a couple to perform this tradition if they plan to spend their lives together. Every wedding tradition started somewhere and the more that it is performed, in real life or in media form, the more ritualistic it becomes. While it may seem as though these rituals are being depicted negatively, this is not what is intended. Chesser recognizes that “most of us have a need for some ritual, because it provides us with a point of stability and consistency” (204). Many people find comfort through rituals because they are familiar, and they are able to comprehend the experiences easier than attempting to comprehend new material. Therefore, brides need to take their ideas from media influences and previous experiences because they are not equipped to design like theatrical professionals. When brides take these ideas, they continue to reinforce the normal wedding traditions, continuing these rituals for years to come.

Another key role that the bride takes on is that of the marketing manager. A marketing manager, in a theatre, oversees advertising the play to the community and inviting members to attend the performances. While the manager wants as many attendants as possible, there is usually a select audience that the manager advertises to because the show they selected is more directed to that audience. The bride takes on this role through invitations and creating a guest list. Brides must create save the date cards and invitations to send out to people, informing them and inviting them to the wedding. Save the date cards are given to the audience members to advertise the date that the production will take place. Invitations are sent to people who are invited to attend the event. These invitations, like a marketing manager, directly invites a select audience; however, it is advertised to more people than those that will attend. The invitations have to match the atmosphere that the wedding will have in order to prepare the audience for the feeling of the wedding, making them want to attend the event. The amount of advertisement effects the size of the audience that attends. Those who have a marketing degree understand that there are certain actions one must do in order to get the attention of those that are invited. While there is always an audience that will come to an event, there are certain audience members that must be won over by the advertisement. Many people are invited to a wedding and, like a theatre production, there is a select population that will automatically attend. There are other people, however, that must be won over through the advertising. Brides that want a certain number of attendees must make sure that the cards are perfect for the selected audience. If these aspects are not done correctly and it seems as though less people are going to attend the event than planned, this can stress out a bride, causing her to possibly become more controlling over other aspects of the wedding, making her viewed as a bridezilla.

There are three possible solutions proposed to help fix the problem of brides being viewed as bridezillas due to their stress level: one, people can either hire more people to perform these jobs; two, couples can split the day between the couple; or three, society can lower their expectations. Women can decrease their stress level if they hire other people to do work for them. To help with the directing aspects, a bride can hire a wedding planner to help do research and create a vision and plan for execution. The wedding planner then reaches out to different designers and companies to contribute to the wedding, but the bride has the final say on any decisions that are made. In this case, the bride becomes more of a producer, paying for everything and being the final say regarding any decisions made for the event. Hiring a wedding planner can help decrease the bride’s stress level because there is less responsibilities the bride takes on. A wedding planner does this as their career and already knows what needs to go into a wedding.

There are downsides to this solution, being that it costs more money on top of the cost of the wedding itself and the wedding planner will do the same process for planning weddings, making multiple weddings appear the same. According to an article published in *Money*, 34% of weddings cost between $5,000 and $9,999 and, coming in second, 29% spend between $10,000 and $25,000 (“The Wedding”). The average cost of a wedding planner is $3,500 (“The Wedding”). If a couple does not have the money to pay for a wedding planner, they tend to take on the roles themselves so they do not have to spend money on a professional to take care of planning. Due to this cost issue, many brides are unable to afford to only be a producer of their wedding.

Another solution to dwindle the concept of bridezilla is to split the responsibility of the wedding day so that it is not only considered the bride’s day. By splitting the responsibility, the groom also takes authority of some of the planning. The roles, while there still will be a lot for only two people, can then be split so that not all of the stress falls onto one person. In order to do this solution, however, the bride must trust that the groom can fulfill all of the duties as well. This process can start through deciding which rituals the couple wants to keep in the wedding. It is believed that couples should choose together which wedding rituals they would like to partake in because it “may \[have\] an important and lasting effect on the marriage” (Chesser 204). Planning a wedding together can help enhance the couple’s relationship and possibly help the two learn more about each other, especially during a more stressful time. Splitting up the responsibility also can help diminish the idea that a wedding day is the bride’s day, even though the day represents the unity of two people. The bride would not be seen as a bridezilla if the responsibility was shared between the groom and the bride because the woman would have less to do, and the groom also can be seen as the person vetoing ideas.

A possible downfall to this solution, however, is that most grooms are not told since they were a child to prepare for their wedding day. Most men do not fantasize about how their wedding day will look, or at least that is the societal belief. A groom must be as invested in planning the wedding as the bride is or else this solution is not possible. The groom has to not be worried about others possibly judging him from the high amount of participation in planning the wedding. The more men participate in planning a wedding, the more this solution will spread, and this solution can become a social norm.

One final solution, that may not be able to be implemented, is that society should lower their standards for weddings to better suit what can be achieved by a couple or the bride alone. Not all couples can afford to pay a lot of money for a large, elaborate wedding. If the wedding did not have the expectation to be perfect, then the bride would not become as stressed for the event. There does not have to be so much time spent planning the event and more time can be spent spending time with the person the bride loves. This solution is the most difficult to implement and there can be a lot of research done on this particular topic. It is not understood, even, where to start implementing this plan. Though, this is not the topic at hand. If society lowers their expectation for weddings, then a bride would have less stress on her wedding day and can stop being considered a bridezilla, because there could be less matters she would have to worry about.

Overall, by comparing the roles that theatre practitioners take to put on a theatre production and the multiple roles brides in western culture are expected to take on, there are unrealistic expectations for the bride to meet and attempting to achieve these expectations can be stressful, causing a woman to seem over controlling, which is viewed as the bridezilla effect. Through the exploration of the different roles the bride must take on, it is evident that the bride is expected to do too many tasks to reach perfection. The stress of having to perform these roles can cause a bride to want every aspect to be perfect for those who attend the wedding. When aspects are not perfect, the bride can be viewed as a bridezilla micromanaging. Three solutions to this problem can be to hire people to perform roles, divide the responsibilities between the couple, or lower society’s expectations for a perfect wedding. Many people go through the process of getting married and having a wedding, so the fact that a majority of people experience these stresses, it is important to evaluate the way these events are planned and executed.
