## 26 August 2015

Today in class we discussed age groups that we would enjoy working with. It really amazed me how different people are from each other, all the way down to the people they prefer to work with. I have always known working with teenagers is rare and that many people don’t really care for it, but I was surprised by how many different populations of people there really are. I had never thought of specific groups of elderly people or working with infants. Those ideas are exciting!

Talking about populations we want to work with and having experiences with other populations got me thinking of all the times I have worked with the population I want to work with and other times I have had other experiences to compare it to. I have worked with middle school aged kids on multiple occasions. I ran my drama club with sixth graders; I helped out with a science camp with middle school aged kids; I was in theatre productions at the Washington Pavilion with late elementary, middle school aged kids; in Ukraine I mainly worked with middle school aged kids. In all these occasions I had an amazing time and really enjoyed the work I was doing. A lot of the time it also involved theatrical aspects, which only enhanced my wanting to do drama therapy.

To compare this age group to another one, I mentioned that I have done some volunteer in a nursing home and that I didn’t really connect to the people there. [A family member of mine teaches young children], so I have been around elementary aged kids and it usually frustrates me if they don’t quite understand what I am saying. With middle school aged kids, they are just old enough to understand what I explain, but still have that child-ish aspect to them that makes them fun to be around.

## 4 September 2015

[An art therapist] came to speak to us today about art therapy and her journey to becoming an art therapist. Hearing her speak was truly inspiring, just like [the first speaker]! Hearing the two speakers and seeing how passionate they are about their jobs makes me excited for my education to come. I could see and hear their happiness as they talked about their jobs and I want to experience that.

I was happy that [the art therapist] talked about her step by step education process to becoming an art therapist. I know [the first speaker] did this as well, but [the art therapist] went into her internships and certification process. I was very interested in all of the different populations she had visited / worked at. I was surprised by her reaction to working with substance abusers: one, because I hadn’t considered substance abusers taking to art therapy; two, I didn’t quite understand how hard the job could be.  I never would have thought of the population of substance abusers, but since she brought it up, I can easily see how art therapy can help these people. I can also sympathize to her experience of relapse. Having to start over with someone that was doing well cannot be easy and it must take some real control to hide your frustration with the client.

While studying drama therapy, I had always wondered what would happen if a therapist interpreted something wrong. I’m really glad [the art therapist] brought up the point to always ask and not make judgement. If you ask a client what they did, then there is not too much room for misinterpretation and in this way it can be a learning experience for both parties. While working at the writing center, we are told as consultants to make the appointments collaborative. If they work together, that means there are two people looking at the same problem and this makes it easier to draw a conclusion. Even though one member knows has more knowledge than the other, the writer knows more about the subject. This is true to a client and therapist. The client knows more about themselves than the therapist, even if it doesn’t quite seem like it at the time.

## 11 September 2015

Well, Freud using cocaine was new information to learn today! I thought it was also interesting to learn that Carl Jung was the first person to bring art into psychoanalysis. It makes sense that all of this happened when realism came around. It was not surprising to have [the art therapist] say that artists were isolated and not really appreciated because this idea has been around since the medieval era, well, with theatre anyways. Theatre has always been the blame for anything that can go wrong in society. Especially in the Renaissance when Kings and Queens banned theatre from being performed inside city boundaries. The arts speak to people and its power is uncontrollable, which is why it scares authority figures.

We learned about Post Traumatic Stress Disorder as well. This idea is not particularly new to me because I am in the military. They inform us about PTSD and teach us about suicide awareness a lot. I was very happy to see a marine doing art, particularly a mask. Masks are used a lot in drama therapy because of the power it has. Masks help people create new characters and it is something that can be taken off, so the character goes away. It makes it easier for someone to not get too close to a situation. It is easier to talk about an object than it is to talk about yourself. What worries me about arts in healthcare is that if someone creates something and has a therapeutic discovery, the closure in a session is the more important thing. In arts in healthcare, it is possible for someone to leave without closure, but in a therapy setting, they get the help they need and are able to talk through the experience with a professional therapist. They are able to understand the feelings inside of them and discuss them. If a person doing arts in healthcare do not see the person’s problem, the person can leave confused and this possibly could cause harm if not treated correctly. Sure the arts person can talk to healthcare staff if they notice a problem because they are not trained in it, it can be harmful.

## 18 September 2015

Talking about making projects and preparation was very insightful. It was also important to learn about projects that are “crap-tastic.” I have actually heard that phrase before and have used it in other contexts. In improvisation we want to keep away from crap-tastic because it usually means the scene is not really going anywhere and the scene could easily get out of hand.

I believe preparation is very important! If a person goes to do things with a group of people, but has no idea what they are going to do, the participants are not going to get nearly as much out of the project because the person leading is not really able to fully lead. It is also important to prepare so you can get your materials ready and see if there are any materials used in the project that should not be used with the population that is going to do the project. Being given a list of materials that one should be careful with is very helpful! Many of these things I would never have thought of, probably because I do not know what a lot of the things really are.

Working at [my military job], all our hazardous material items need MSDSs in order to be shipped out. Traffic Management refuses to send out anything if the MSDS is not inside. This makes sense because whoever is receiving the item needs to know how to deal with it as soon as they get it. The MSDS also helps warehouses know where to store the item. It is dangerous to store flammable items next to aerosol items because if the flammable items were to catch on fire, the aerosol items would explode and make the fire worse.

OSHA is also important to both my [military] job and working in the theatre shop. There are specific guidelines for how everything in both need to be stored in order to protect people working there. If things are stored wrong, it can be very dangerous to those working around the area. One important thing at [my military job] is the forklift. The prongs need to be all the way down and not tilted when put away to prevent tripping. Safety is important in every environment.

Today was very exciting in class! I love learning something and being able to apply it right away. Being given the art engagement forms was very helpful. Looking at one of the projects I am very interested in doing, maybe not for class, but just because, fulfills every category. If the USD Improv Club were to go to Sanford in [town] and perform for the homeschool kids and elderly folk, this would be amazing. It fits under socialization because the performance includes information given to us from the audience. This includes turn-taking because of question, answer, and also includes greetings to introduce ourselves, and the kids. It also includes giving compliments because most of the information we get from the audience, we thank them for and say, “good suggestion.” The performance also uses gross motor skills for raising hands to give suggestions and also to give movements for characters to do in the scene they are given. The performance uses communication by listening to the performance, making requests for scenes, reality orientation for watching the scene that is related to today’s society. It also includes expression because we get different emotional suggestions from the audience. The performance uses sensory integration because the audience needs to understand the emotions of the characters. They also experience different environments that the actors create on stage. Finally, it uses cognitive stimulation with following instruction and memory activities because we ask for specific things in the audience’s life that can be portrayed on stage (but nothing *too* personal).

## 28 October 2015

## 30 October 2015

[The art therapist] came in and helped us discuss the four principles of arts in healthcare. The first one is the Hippocratic Oath: First, do no harm. The quote pretty much speaks for itself. It is important to focus on the idea of “first” because it means that no other rules come before it. Making sure you know the mission and how it fits with other people’s missions is important. You always want to make sure you are putting health and healing first. Always looking ahead and making sure you are shooting for a vision for the future. There should always be a goal. Understanding the values others have is also important so you do not get in their way or harm them emotionally (because first, you must do no harm).

Making sure you understand the purpose for being where you are. It was nice to figure out the difference between patient and client. Patient meaning the specific person while the client means everyone you serve.

It is interesting to know the difference between spiritual principles and spiritual practice. The practice being what you do while the principles is what you believe. You do not have to be religious to have a spiritual principles. It is something everyone should have. It is that idea of moral compass. Everyone should also have some sort of spiritual practice, whether or not they know that it is one. It is that idea of care for caregivers.

Finally we discussed service. You need to know what you are doing and what you are providing to others. You also need to remember to service yourself. Do not do anything you feel uncomfortable doing. You also can’t do something that would make someone else feel uncomfortable. Know your boundaries and your motives.
