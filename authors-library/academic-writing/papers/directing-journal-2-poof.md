<p class="paper-title">Directing Journal #2</p>

This project was certainly full of ups and downs. I selected my play and I absolutely love it! Exploring the text with my actors was great as well. [The actor playing Loureen] and I sat down to decide what her 8-9 “Uh!” lines meant and made the choice that the beginning would be the 5 stages of grief in about two minutes. We had a lot of fun working with this and thought that it gave a lighter side to the fact that her husband is dead. Because we don’t want the audience to feel sad about the death of her husband, adding this humor to the beginning I thought would lighten the mood. The trouble with this is that we needed to play up the emotions and have them over the top in order for the audience to catch on to them. For this, we played the improv game “Emotions” where the point of the game is to change emotions at the will of the person in charge and to play those emotions to their peak. After this game, [she] was able to understand the extent of the emotions needed and applied this to the beginning of the scene.

We had a great exploration with [the actor playing Florence] and increasing her anger as well as her volume. We tried an exercise where [the actor playing Loureen] stood close to [her] and that is when [she] was quieter, then [the actor playing Loureen] slowly moved backwards and [the actor playing Florence] had to increase her volume in order for [her scene partner] to “still hear her.” [She] was able to understand what was being asked of her by this exercise.

[The actor playing Florence] was difficult to work with as well. There were two things I would ask of her and she wrote them off and did not really care by saying, “Well, other people have told me this and I never really do anything about because I do that regularly anyways.” These two things were how she stands and giving out extra breaths in the middle of sentences. I pointed these things out and asked her to work on them, but there was no difference the next two rehearsals.

Here is my text Analysis of my play:

### Given Circumstances

- Environmental Facts:
    - Kitchen
        - This is “where women belong” and Samuel enters Loureen’s “Zone” (common knowledge)
    - Late 1970s, 5:15 in the afternoon, Spring
        - Florence’s husband is getting home from work soon and Samuel was already home from work. Spring symbolizes new beginnings. (text, research)
    - Economic environment: Relatively well off. Money does not seem to be an issue. Loureen can get a job.
        - Ages of 25 and 44 in the workforce jumped from 26 percent in 1950 to 67 percent by the mid-1980s. (research)
    - Social environment: Loureen was abused by her husband on multiple occasions and the violence was severe. (text)
    - Political environment: police officers do not like to involve themselves with family issues and do not believe women when they report domestic violence. (text)
    - Religious environment: The women believe in God and go to church. (text)
- Previous actions
    - Samuel and Edgar physically abuse their wives. (527)
    - Loureen has messed around with witchcraft. (528)
    - Florence has children. (533)
    - Samuel would abuse Loureen for multiple reasons: 1. Wrinkling his coat (531), 2. Forgetting to pick up his clothes (523), 3. Changing the TV channel (533).
    - Loureen and Florence had planned on running away together (531)
    - Loureen hates Samuel’s glasses (526)
    - Loureen and Florence have been friends since Loureen was younger (529)
    - Samuel hated when Loureen would raise her voice (527)
    - Samuel drives a motorcycle (532)
- Polar Attitudes
    - Loureen: Shock -> Freedom
    - Florence: Confusion -> Isolation

### Dialogue

- Pattern word use:
    - Uh! – Loureen uses this quite often to show her lack of words towards the beginning of the play, however she does not use this later-on and this shows her gaining her composure.
    - Violence – this idea is brought up multiple times. The women talk about the abuse they receive and Florence talks about her husband hitting someone with his car.
    - Leaving – the idea of leaving is brought up multiple times. (Samuel talking about leaving. Loureen and Florence leaving. People traveling far to see Loureen. Edgar driving. Samuel going to Reno. Samuel has a motorcycle.)
- Dialect
    - There is a southern dialect for Florence.
- Structure
    - If there are short lines, they are usually followed by short lines until a longer line.

### Dramatic Structure

- Opening Stasis: Anger
- Inciting Action: Poofing Samuel
- Crisis: Wrinkling of the Jacket
- Climax: Goodbye Samuel
- Closing: Lightening

### Characters

- Loureen
    - Desire – overcome fear of husband
    - Will – As far as running away
    - Moral Stance – Christian, do what you have to do to survive
    - Decorum – Easily frantic, questions self-behavior
- Florence
    - Desire – understand why Loureen has done what she did
    - Will – as far as yelling at her friend
    - Moral Stance – Christian, do what you can to survive and make people happy
    - Decorum – Southern, motherly

### Idea

- Title Relevance:
    - The title is the inciting action. Loureen’s life-long problem is solved by a “Poof!”
- Pivotal Statements
    - Loureen: It would’ve been me eventually. – Loureen finally understands that what she did is good for her because if he hadn’t died, he probably would have killed her at some point.
    - Florence: Well I guess my mornings are not going to be any different. – This shows that Florence does not have the strength to stand up for herself like Loureen did. Florence’s life is going to be the same unless she decides to stand up for herself and say, “Damn you to hell.”
- Major Dramatic Question:
    - Why put up with actions against you when you don’t have to?

### Style

- This play is representational because the actors do not acknowledge the audience.
- This play is a dark comedy because the subject matter is one that is heavy; however, there are certainly comedic parts to the play.
- The sensory responses in the play:
    - Buzzing silence, temperature = hot, un-patterned, limited open space
