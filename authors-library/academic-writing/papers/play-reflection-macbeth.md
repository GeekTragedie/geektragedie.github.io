### Major characters

The major characters in the play are the three witches, Duncan, Malcolm, Macbeth, Banquo, Lady Macbeth, Fleance, Macduff, Three Murderers, Lady Macduff, and the Son of Macduff.

### Plot summary

King Duncan finds out that Macdonald is a traitor in their battle and has him killed. He also decides to let Macbeth, the hero of the battle, take Macdonald’s place as Thane of Cawdor once Macbeth has returned from battle. Macbeth and Banquo, returning from battle, run into three witches that prophesize Macbeth will be made Thane of Cawdor, then King of Scotland. The witches also prophesize that Banquo’s offspring will one day be king. The men return to Scotland when King Duncan makes Macbeth Thane of Cawdor; Macbeth invites the king to dinner. Macbeth tells Lady Macbeth about the witch’s prophesy and Lady Macbeth convinces Macbeth to kill Duncan while he stays over at their house after dinner. Macbeth does not feel certain about doing this until his wife creates a plan. They plan to kill Duncan and blame it on his guards. The two guards will be drunk and passed out so they will not be able to defend themselves before they are hanged. This plan is thrown into action and the kingdom is thrown off by the murder. Duncan’s sons, Malcolm and Donaldbain, run away to escape being murdered themselves; however, this act makes them look guilty and makes Macbeth and his wife get off without being suspected at all.

Macbeth then invites Banquo and his son for dinner and plans to have them killed before their arrival. He does this because he remembers his prophesy the witches told him about Banquo’s kin taking the throne. Macbeth hires three murderers to kill Banquo and his son, Fleance. However, Fleance is able to escape while Banquo dies. This murder happens while Macbeth and his wife are holding a dinner party at their house. During the party, Banquo’s ghost visits Macbeth and Macbeth starts to act funny and scare his guests. Lady Macbeth plays it off as normal and asks everyone to leave, then scolds her husband.

Because of the ghost’s visit, Macbeth travels to see the witches again and receives another prophesy. The new prophesy tells Macbeth that he will not be able to be killed by anyone born to a woman and that he will remain king and alive until the Birnam Wood moves closer to the castle. By this news, Macbeth believes he will be safe forever because all people have to be born to a woman and there is no possibility that woods are able to move.

Due to the fact that Macbeth hears of his enemy, Macduff, joining forces with Duncan’s son, Malcolm, Macbeth sends murderers to take over Macduff’s castle. This task is done by the murderers killing Lady Macduff and all of Macduff’s family.

Macduff is trying to talk Malcolm into believing he would make a great king. Malcolm doubts it and says he is a terrible person who always lies, he then says that that is the only lie he has ever told and that he would make a great king and agrees to attack Macbeth. Macduff is then told of the murder of his family and declares it is time to kill Macbeth.

Macbeth is told his wife committed suicide and Malcolm’s soldiers had hidden themselves with Birnam Wood trees and that the woods were moving closer to the castle; this fulfilling part of the witches’ prophesy. Macduff then finds Macbeth and threatens him. Macbeth, believing he cannot be killed by Macduff because he is born from a woman, tells Macduff this information. Macduff then tells Macbeth that he was not born, but was cut out and removed by hand. Macduff then slaughters Macbeth and brings Macbeth’s head back to Malcolm and his men, and Malcolm is crowned King of Scotland. Fleance becoming king later is left for another day.

### Plot structure

The structure of the plot is like a quilt because of the way each scene is separated from the one previous. Each scene is set up as though it is a new episode of a television series. This idea is best demonstrated during the final act of the play, Act V. The act starts off inside the castle with Macbeth finding out about his wife’s suicide; the next scene is Macbeth’s soldiers deciding to fight against him. The following scene is Macbeth bragging that he cannot die. Then switches to Malcolm talking about his disgust towards Macbeth. Then jumps back inside the castle to Macbeth again, then back to Malcolm, then again to Macbeth, then to Malcolm and Macduff and finally, in scene nine, the battle begins. Scene ten is when Macbeth is killed by Macduff and scene eleven is when Macduff emerges with Macbeth’s head. By the idea of jumping around continually, each scene is given the idea that it is its own episode.

### Language

The language used is poetic language that uses rhythm and rhyme. The language is also older and more difficult to understand which gives the actors certain advantages and disadvantages regarding the audience. The entire play is written in one big poem and uses iambic pentameter and closed, rhymed couplets. The iambic pentameter is found in the conversations between characters such as: “Point against point, rebellious arm ‘gainst arm” (1.2.56). Here the beginning of a word is cut off to fit the iambic pentameter. The use of iambic pentameter throughout the play gives the audience a sense of something staying the same as everything else in the play changes. It makes them feel more comfortable and at ease while the action is happening on stage, especially since the language used is not used in today’s society anymore. The use of closed, rhythm couplets is seen in the scenes in which the witches are present. The witches begin the play with the couplet example, “When shall we three meet again? /In thunder, lighting, or in rain? /When the hurly-burly’s done, /When the battle’s lost, and won” (1.1.1-4). This use of couplets starting off the play appeals to the audience and draws them in to the play. By the use of a rhyme scheme, the audience sees that there is something special about the play. It makes them pay attention to what is on the stage and adds an immediate intrigue. This language use gives advantages to the actor regarding the audience because the actors need to be more physical in order to show the audience what the line is saying. Actors also need to make sure they say the lines in a way that the audiences knows what they are trying to portray. The disadvantage for the actor is that it is more difficult to memorize, and if they do not portray their words correctly, the audience cannot rely on the language to understand the play which could ruin the play for the audience altogether. Overall, the poetic and old-fashioned language have specific effects on the audience and how the actors need to perform.

### Central conflict

The central conflict of this play is person vs. self; Macbeth vs. his greed. The main reason for this conflict is seen through his obsession with his prophesy. After Macbeth is given his prophesy and is given the title of Thane of Cawdor, giving evidence that his prophesy could be true, Macbeth fights with his prophesy to benefit himself. Macbeth fights his greedy urge to kill King Duncan, but after his wife pushes him over the edge, he kills the king and takes the throne, as his prophesy says he would. Then fights with himself on whether to kill Banquo or not, then has him killed. After having Banquo killed, Macbeth sees Banquo’s ghost and people believe he is going insane. This causes Macbeth to get angry at himself for possibly costing himself the throne and it also causes his wife to get mad as well. Macbeth visits the witches again to gain more information about his future. From this trip, the witches lead Macbeth to believe that he is undefeatable. Macbeth is so full of himself that when he finds out about his wife’s suicide, he does not care and only comments, “She should have died hereafter; /There would have been time for such a word” (5.5.17-19). He cares so much about himself that he does not bother to care about his wife’s death. He only says that she would have died anyways. Macbeth becomes greedy with his power and full of himself, leading his enemies to attack and overthrow him. Macbeth, blinded by his believed power and greed that the greed won and ended up causing him his life. Through Macbeth’s greed for power, you can see the central conflict of the play: Macbeth vs. himself.

### Presentational or representational?

This play is representational because it does not acknowledge the audience. It may give the impression that it acknowledges the audience when Lady Macbeth talks to herself in Act I Scene 5 and when talks about his victory at the end of the play, Act V Scene 9, but the stage directions indicate that there is no interaction with the audience. Although it may seem as though the play could be presentational, the play is not.

### Theme

The theme of the play is greed and can be shown, not only through Macbeth’s actions alone, but also through his wife’s actions, and through Malcolm talking about why he would not make a great king. Greed is shown through Macbeth’s actions by his need to make sure his prophesy is correct and that he is the king of Scotland. This greed is shown through him murdering Duncan, Banquo, Lady Macduff, and her children. He also starts a war to try and take Macduff’s land both because the first apparition tells him to “beware Macduff” (4.1.69). To eliminate Macduff, Macbeth takes Macduff’s land and have more power. Macbeth’s greed is driven by the greed of his wife, Lady Macbeth. Macbeth struggles with whether or not he wants to murder Duncan and tells his wife, “We will proceed no further in this business” (1.7.31). Lady Macbeth is furious with this command and says, “When you durst do it, then you were a man. /And to be more than what you were, you would /Be so much more the man” (1.7.48-50). Lady Macbeth says that he is not a man for wanting to back down from the throne. She is greedy and wants to be queen with riches and jewels and power. The greed shown through Malcolm is by the scene when he is saying he is not fit to be king because of his yearn for power. The functionality of this scene is to emphasize greed as a theme for the play because Malcolm takes back everything he says and claims he would be a great king. Malcolm claims, “I should cut off the nobles for their lands, /Desire his jewels, and this other’s house, /And my more-having would be as a sauce /To make me hunger more, that I should forge /Quarrels unjust against the good and loyal, /Destroy them for wealth” (4.3.79-83). This shows Malcolm’s thought of greed and highlights the greed of Macbeth and his wife. However, Malcolm takes back this idea of greed and says that he is lying about himself. Although he takes this back, the greed mentioned in his lie still has a function for the play. Through Malcolm, Macbeth, and Lady Macbeth’s actions, the theme of the play can be seen as greed.
