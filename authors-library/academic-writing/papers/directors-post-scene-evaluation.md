# Director’s Post-Scene Evaluation

**Degree / Program:** BFA in Theatre  
**Course / Context:** Directing: Period Styles (Chaya Gordon-Bland), University of South Dakota  
**Date:** 2 April 2018  

---

<p class="piece-note">A look back on directing a masked scene from the end of Sophocles’ <em>Antigone</em>. The actor’s name is replaced in brackets.</p>

<div class="paper mla">

<div class="mla-heading"><p>GeekTragedie</p><p>Chaya Gordon-Bland</p><p>Directing: Period Styles</p><p>2 April 2018</p></div>

<p class="paper-title">Director’s Post-Scene Evaluation</p>

The rehearsal process for this project was rather different than how I approach other plays. We spent more time on the language than I believe I have ever done before. We started discussing given circumstances and I wanted to do more language work before getting the scene on its feet. This step was difficult because I was completely wrong in this decision. I should have had the actors on their feet earlier and it probably should have saved time. I also should have made the decision to wear masks earlier because I also think that would have saved time. I certainly think I that is one lesson I have learned from this process. I will not hesitate to use masks again, and I will not stay at the table too long. I normally do not stay at the table long at all and like getting people on their feet early. This process solidified that stage for me.

The masks challenged me. I tried to make transitions easiest for the actors, but with having little mask experience, I was not fully confident in the ways that I used them. I did not want to waste the actors’ time because I know they were stressed and busy, but I wanted to give them enough time to explore the masks. Using one full rehearsal to explore sound and half of a rehearsal to explore movement seemed like a lot to me and I hope the actors found it beneficial. I asked them if they appreciated the time and they said yes, but I guess I second guess myself when it comes to topics I have not been trained in. The actors had more experience in masks than I did, so, while I gave them time to be in the masks, I was still the one talking them through the exercises. I did not want to walk them through the exercises wrong because they were the ones that knew more than I did.

One other aspect that challenged me was directing in a classroom environment. After having directed the one acts for two years, I forgot what it was like to work with deadlines and to space out my time appropriately. I am accustomed to creating my own goals for where my scene should be and when it needs to reach those goals. It was also interesting receiving feedback throughout the process from peers. Having multiple showings was helpful to incorporate the feedback we were give, but it was also challenging because there was the pressure to include it in the scene, even though we were told we did not need to because I felt the feedback was very valuable. The classroom work was also rather difficult because it made it very important that each actor was in each rehearsal because we could fall behind if someone was missing, which happened twice. One rehearsal that was missed was one of two before our final showing for the class. Having the absence during that rehearsal was bothersome because the information discovered during the rehearsal played a crucial role for the blocking discovered during the final rehearsal. The actors did not really want two rehearsals before the final showing, but I talked them into it because I knew that we would need one more than they believed we did because of my overall plan. Good thing I ensured we had another rehearsal.

While there were many challenges, I also found many aspects rewarding. I was very proud of the work the actors put in and got a giddy feeling every time they finished their work. That feeling is always rewarding to me. [One actor] finding her Kreon body and play with the emotions, seeing them blossom, was amazing! Viewing the difference between working the blocking with and without the masks was a great experience too. The fact that the actors told me that I was asking the right questions and had an equal amount of discussion and blocking was rewarding as well.

The biggest idea that I have taken from this project is my confidence in possibly working with Greek plays in the future. I will not shy away from them or think they are too far away from my grasp. I will not think they are not too difficult to conquer and I will no longer believe that no one can relate to their stories, as I sadly did before. I will continue to challenge myself to step out of my comfort zone to try new theatrical experiences, even if I feel afraid to do so, especially in an academic setting.
