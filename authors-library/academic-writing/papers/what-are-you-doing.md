# What are you Doing?

**Degree / Program:** Drama Therapy Specialization  
**Course / Context:** Spring 2020 coursework, Antioch University Seattle  
**Date:** Spring 2020  

---

*Think spontaneously as the group navigates how to enter conversations.*

**Target Population:**

- Any group aiming to increase their social skill abilities

**Therapeutic Goals:**

- Engage in conversation
- Observe and differentiate between verbal and non-verbal communication
- Model and witness various introductory techniques to increase personal repertoire
- Practice non-verbal communication and direct verbal interaction

**Time Frame:**

- 5-30 minutes
- Time amount can depend on group size and how much you want to develop scenes as they go around the circle

**Directions:**

1. Gather the group in a circle.
1. Explain the game to the group.
1. Choose one player to start miming an activity.
1. The next player in the circle asks the miming player, “What are you doing?”
1. The miming player responds, “I am ___.” filling in the blank with an activity that they are not currently miming.
1. The player who asked, “What are you doing?” then mimes the action that the previous player stated they were doing.
1. Continue around the circle.

**Example:**

- First performer mimes that they are brushing their teeth.
- Second performer asks the first performer, “What are you doing?”
- First performer says, “I am fishing.”
- Second performer mimes that they are fishing.

**Helpful Hints:**

- This activity works best when the group is shown an example.
- This game can develop further, depending on developmental level and comfort, by inviting players join the miming scene before asking “what are you doing?”
    - However, it is recommended that the line not change from “what are you doing” to help with line cues for the whole group.

**Materials List:**

- None.
