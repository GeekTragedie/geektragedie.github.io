Homosexuality use to be considered a mental illness by the American Psychiatric Association (APA) in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual (DSM) until 1974 (Hickey, 2011). In the first DSM in 1952, homosexuality was considered a sociopathic personality disturbance due to sexual deviation (APA, 1952). DSM-II in 1968 considered homosexuality as a sexual deviation (TreeHugger, 2011). Some people today still believe that homosexuality should be considered a mental illness. The removal of homosexuality as a mental illness “was not triggered by some scientific breakthrough… Rather, it was the simple reality that gay people… gained a voice and began to make themselves heard” (Hickey, 2011). The idea of homosexuality being a mental illness has been revisited in the second season of “American Horror Story: Asylum” by one of their characters being in the asylum due to her homosexuality.

In this season, actress Sarah Paulson plays the role of Lana Winters, a woman who is institutionalized for her sexuality. This season’s settings took place in 1964, so homosexuality was still a diagnosable mental illness and followed the lines of DSM-I. The exact definition of this disturbance is as follows: “reserved for deviant sexuality… The term includes most of the cases formerly classed as ‘psychopathic personality with pathologic sexuality’” (APA). In order for Lana to be released from the asylum, she had to be considered cured by a psychologist in the facility. The nuns in the facility try Electroconvulsive Therapy to rid her of her feelings. They also try different medication not specified in the show. Nothing seems to be working because the woman is too strong-willed.

Eventually, Dr. Oliver Thredson, played by actor Zachary Quinto, decides to take her case and attempt to cure her mental illness. This season of “American Horror Story” takes place in 1964, so this is when the DSM considered homosexuality as a sociopathic personality disturbance. Dr. Oliver Thredson uses “aversion therapy” to try and cure her personality disturbance (Falchuk & Murphy, 2012). To do this he showed Lana a sexual picture of a woman, then gave her a drug to make her throw up. This process was an attempt to associate the puking feeling with the image of a woman. This is a version of the behavioral model of psychology because it is an attempt at classical conditioning. The unconditioned stimuli are the sexual images of women; the unconditioned response is the puking. What the therapist is trying to create is a conditioned response of puking when Lana were to see sexual images of women.

The show “American Horror Story” is popular in the United States. A popular show depicting homosexuality as a mental illness could possibly have negative effects on society. Viewers of the show could have watched it and believed that homosexuality was still an illness. There are still many people in the world that do not accept homosexuality and this television show could have helped enforce this idea in their mind. While it can be assumed this idea is not what the show was attempting, it is certainly an idea that viewers could have received, especially if they did not watch the entire season of the show. It could be easily missed by viewers that the show is set in 1964. This show can negatively affect the stigma society has towards homosexual people, and the LGBT community in general.

It is important to understand the time period of which a show takes place. One can easily construe their reality if they believe that a modern show is actually set in present-day times. The show “American Horror Story” depicts exactly what the title says, the horrors of America. One of the horrors of American’s past is their treatment of homosexual people. Viewers today should be educated in their culture to know that homosexuality should not be stigmatized in this way.
