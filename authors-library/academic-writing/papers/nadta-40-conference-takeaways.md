## NADTA 40 Conference Takeaways

The NADTA’s 40th conference, held in Philadelphia, PA, was my first NADTA conference experience and will certainly not be my last! I had an amazing time on this trip and the conference was certainly the highlight. I learned many drama therapy (DT) techniques and skills and was moved by many performances, but my largest takeaway from the conference was motivation, inspiration, and passion to become a drama therapist.

I attended this conference after finishing my first of three years of school and had been largely lacking motivation and felt academic burnout. Antioch University Seattle offers an amazing education, but there are not many DT students to connect with and the non-creative arts students tend to oppress DT students out of their own performance anxiety. The conference boosted my spirits and helped me find my passion for the beauty that is DT. I met other drama therapists that are passionate about the work they are doing. Their passion outweighed the negativity at my school as I now know there is a community of people all over practicing DT. I am motivated to finish up my studies and learn the most I can to best serve my future clients as a drama therapist, not just an LMHC. I am now proud to call myself a future drama therapist!

Another takeaway I had from the NADTA conference experience was to become more of a leader. I was inspired by the presenters and organizers of the conference to become more involved in the community and with leadership. While I am already a NADTA Student Committee member, I now have more respect and want to take this position seriously because we are the leaders of the future. I want to learn the most I can from this role and provide support to other DT students. I am more motivated to create a closer community at Antioch University Seattle to aid other students who may feel the way I did before going to the conference. The first step I plan to take is to continue the DT student group at my school and aid in its growth.

The day after the conference, two fellow DT students and I went sightseeing around Philadelphia. We took a short tour of Independence Hall and heard a beautiful story about how the United States has been a role model for other countries to promote freedom and justice for all. I was moved by the story I heard and then got excited because that is how I feel about the NADTA. I hope the organization can be a role model for other creative arts therapies to grow and promote freedom, justice, and equality. I know the organization can be more than that. It can use its power for good to aid with social justice and equality for all people that are oppressed in our society. In Independence Hall, I took a picture with my colleagues because the three of us are the future of the NADTA. We want to help the organization grow and help foster an environment that can aid other organizations. Most of all, we want to seek social justice for all people, which we hope to prosper in our future as drama therapists. Our picture is simply a symbol of the dedication we have to foster growth in the DT community.

I would not have had as amazing a time at the conference as I did without the DT Fund. Receiving the funds to the conference was immensely helpful as I was able to fully take the week off of work to simply enjoy the academia that was the conference. I had the privilege of not feeling a financial burden at the conference because I was able to stay in the present with people and not be in my anxious, worrying mind. Thank you so much for the opportunity to apply and receive this scholarship for the NADTA 40th conference; it really means a lot to me. I already have the NADTA 41st conference on my calendar! See you in San Diego!
