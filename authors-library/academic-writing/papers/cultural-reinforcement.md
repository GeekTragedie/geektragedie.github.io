<p class="paper-title">Cultural Reinforcement</p>

Theatre can be viewed as a reflection of society. The actions taking place on stage are influenced by the culture surrounding the action; however, theatre also impacts the society it is rooted in in two ways: formation and reification. Theatre helps form communal identities or laws of morality for a society to live by. Reification of these identities also comes out of theatre. Plays from different time periods and in different countries are vastly different because the cultures those plays originated in are different. They are forming and reinforcing the culture identities created around them. Therefore, plays help form and reinforce communal identities. By looking at the cultures surrounding the plays *Snow in Midsummer*, a Chinese Zaju drama, and *The Martyrdom of the Holy Virgins Agape, Chionia, and Hirena*, a play from the European Medieval period, one can understand how play help with the construction and fortification of their cultures’ respective communal identities.

The Yuan Dynasty helped influence the play *Snow in Midsummer* based on the subject matter of the play. Many plays during this dynasty surrounded the idea of justice. One of the theories behind why justice was a main theme for these playwrights leads back to when the “the Mongol khans overthrew” the Song dynasty, leading to the beginning of the Yuan Dynasty (Winet 501). The Chinese government oppressed their people, which opened up creativity and political theatre. Politics can be seen as an influence in *Snow in Midsummer* through the idea of justice. Dou E accused by Donkey of murdering Zhang. Dou E is innocent and Donkey really framed Dou E for the murder. To protect Mrs. Cai from being whipped, Dou E claims to have done the murder and is killed. This lack of justice connects with the lack of justice Chinese people viewed in their own community. The play reflects the Chinese political culture and its injustice under the Mongol’s rule. The play does not end with the death of Dou E, however. Dou E’s ghost appears to her father, Dou, and tells him of the injustice. Dou tells the truth and creates justice by stating to officials, “Let the Donkey be killed in public… So that my daughter’s spirit may go to heaven” (Hanqing 522). By having the play end with justice, the playwright is wishing for the community to reflect this. The play is reflecting times that they hope will come to be their communal identity. The audience of the play is the oppressive Mongol government that will hopefully listen to their citizens and change their judicial ways. The play sets out a construction for the government to follow to create justice, if the politicians would listen to the people.

While the play *Snow in Midsummer* lays out ideals for their government, they also lay out ideals for their own society to follow as well. Many Yuan Dynasty writers also convey the message of “the principles of Confucius,” “The Three Duties of and Four Virtues for women” and men (Winet 504, Genna). Women are to have “obedience” to their father, then to their husband, and finally to their son (Hanqing 520). Dou E follows these rules by listening to her father giving her to Mrs. Cai (508). Dou E remained loyal to her husband until he died and then mourned his death (508). Dou E never had a son to be obedient towards. Dou E fulfilled her three duties, but she also fulfilled the four virtues for women: “serve your parents-in-law, to show respect to your husband, to remain on good terms with your sisters-in-law, and to live in peace with your neighbors” (520). Dou E remained loyal to her mother-in-law by lying to the court to protect her Mrs. Cai (515). She did mourned the loss of her husband for three years (508). She did not have sister-in-laws to remain loyal to and the play does not talk about quarrels between neighbors. Dou E followed her four virtues as a woman. With Dou E following these social rules, her poetic justice at the end of the play helps reinforce these communal ideals. Dou E’s death resulted as honorable because she remained true to her three duties and four virtues. This play shows society the importance of following the rules outlined by society, therefore reinforcing the rules onto society.

The second play that reifies a communal identity is *The Martyrdom of the Holy Virgins Agape, Chionia, and Hirena* (*The Martyrdom…*). This play reinforces the importance of the Christian church and worshiping God. As Dr. Raimondo Genna stated in his lecture on medieval theatre, the church believed the “classical world” (Romans and Ancient Greeks) were “more concerned with… pleasures in this world” (Genna). In the play *The Martyrdom…,* Diocletian does not believe in God and refuses to worship God and is a Roman emperor (Hrotsvit of Gandersheim 494). Diocletian wants to have sex with the three virgins in the play and believes that he is walking into a room to do so. He ends up embarrassing himself by kissing pots and pans (495). By these actions, an audience is influenced into believing that those who do not believe in God, like the Romans, shall be fooled. This also shows the blindness of a person who is tempted by their sexual desires, as Diocletian was blind to the fact that he was kissing pots and pans. The church during the medieval period was more revolved around life after living on earth. The church believed that sexuality, an earthly temptation, was a sin. Through the character Diocletian being punished, *The Martyrdom…* reinforces the church population’s identity to worship God before anyone else, unlike the Romans.

Another ideal of the Medieval period is the Great Chain of Being. The Great Chain of Being is a spiritual organization of importance of beings. God is the first being on this list, followed by angels, saints, and then finally people (Genna). Diocletian refuses to worship God and demands the sisters “deny Christ and comply by bringing offerings to \[his\] gods” (Hrotsvit of Gandersheim 495). Diocletian was not putting God first and respecting the Great Chain of Being, therefore he is punished. Hirena, the youngest sister, never denies her faith and stands strong through her sisters’ deaths. Through her triumph, it is assumed that God saves Hirena from a painful death (499-500). By Hirena being saved, the playwright is reinforcing the communal identity of putting God first, the Great Chain of Being. The Medieval government has a strict feudal system that should not be messed with. This play helps tell people that those who mess with the system shall be punished and those who comply with the system shall be rewarded.

Overall, the plays *Snow in Midsummer* and *The Martyrdom of the Holy Virgins Agape, Chionia, and Hirena* help construct and fortify the imagined communal identities of their respective cultures. The plays reached specific audiences to help the audience either understand a certain perspective or understand certain constructions of society. *Snow in Midsummer* speaks to both a political population to seek a change in justice and to the society population to reinforce moral ideals. *The Martyrdom…* speaks to audiences to keep society the way it is formed and not return to how the Roman empire functioned. It shows people why the system works is constructed in the specific way. It is important to know how theatre influences the culture of which it comes from, because theatre can be seen as a reflection of society. Theatre helps people know what is important, or is perceived as important, to society.
