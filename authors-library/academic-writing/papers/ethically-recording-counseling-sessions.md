## Ethically Recording Counseling Sessions

All counselors need to follow the ethical guidelines pertaining to their licensure to continue practicing and protect all parties involved in the process. On top of following these guidelines, drama therapists also need to follow the North American Drama Therapy Association (NADTA) ethical principles. This paper takes one ethical issue, recording a counseling session, and applies NADTA Code of Ethical Principles specifically.

## Issue Summary

There are a few reasons as to why a therapist may want to record a client session. The therapist may want to view the session later to consider therapeutic goals or track client progress. A supervisor may want to view a session to help the therapist best serve the client. A session with clients may be recorded for educational purposes to help educate future therapists. With multiple benefits to recording sessions, it is important to obtain the material in an ethical way.

## DT Ethical Standards

Drama therapists have two steps to take before recording a session with a client: considering client fit and obtaining informed consent. According to NADTA (2017), “drama therapists are responsible to practice drama therapy techniques in a manner that is professional, maintains professional boundaries, and is always based on the individual’s therapeutic goals, safety, and best interest” (p. 2). Clients’ needs and goals should always come first in counseling. If recording a session is not in the best interest of the client, therapists should revisit their reasoning behind recording sessions. In training facilities, recording sessions may be necessary for therapists’ coursework or training. However, when recording a session for educational purposes, it is crucial to consider client fit.

Drama therapists must also receive informed consent from the clients before recording. NADTA (2017) states that drama therapists must gain “permission from the individual(s), or their legal representatives, to whom he/she provides services before recording voices or images” (p. 1). To gain permission, the therapist must create an informed consent form for the client to sign. The consent form must be written using language that client can understand to create a safe common ground for the use of the recording (NADTA, 2017, p. 1). In the consent form, the therapist should tell the client what the recording will be used for, who will have access to view the recording, how the recording will be stored and for how long because drama therapists have the “responsibility to keep clients… informed at all times during therapy” (NADTA, 2017, p. 1). To best serve the client, a drama therapist should discuss the consent form to ensure mutual understanding and have the client sign the form before recording a session.

After obtaining informed consent and recording a session, a therapist should be mindful of the client’s recordings. Drama therapists must “ensure clients’ records are stored in a secure location and ensure records and client information are available to authorized personnel only” (NADTA, 2017, p. 1). With a client’s recordings on file or in a database, it is the therapist’s job to ensure the recordings maintain the standard in the consent form, which includes: erasing the recordings when necessary, ensuring only those known to the client view the recording, and securing the storage of the recording. A therapist needs to uphold the contract created for the therapy to best serve the client’s needs and maintain confidentiality.

## Personal Challenges

While I understand the benefits, I face personal challenges when I consider recording sessions with future clients. I am concerned that recording sessions may decrease my ability to be authentic and present with clients. I recorded sessions with fellow students in other classes and felt self-conscious about being on camera. I also am concerned about relying too much on recording sessions early on in my career. If I overuse recordings in my training, I may struggle in the future when I no longer have access to the sessions afterwards. I know my upcoming curriculum includes recording sessions. With more practice during my training, I hope to become more tactful with my use of recording sessions with clients.

## Conclusion

Drama therapists must consider the NADTA Code of Ethical Principles over the course of their careers to ensure licensure and best serve clients. In terms of recording client sessions, there are stages and instances where recording may be appropriate. It is important for the therapist to understand the NADTA ethical codes regarding recording sessions to ensure the recordings are best serving clients’ health needs. I also need to approach my personal challenges regarding recording future sessions and personal authenticity. Once I have become a registered drama therapist, I want to maintain ethical standards to ensure I offer the best care possible to others.
