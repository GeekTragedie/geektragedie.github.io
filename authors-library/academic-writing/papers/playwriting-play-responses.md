## Play Response 2: How I Learned to Drive

*7 September 2017*

I think that the play does a nice job of introducing the different aspects of the relationship between Lil’ Bit and Uncle Peck so that the audience can follow their relationship comfortably, or at least as comfortably as one can take this subject. I personally enjoyed the jumping around in life. That is normally how a mind thinks. You think of one idea and then it reminds you of another idea. I actually found it pretty easy to follow. Through this structure, I found it fun to piece together the relationship and follow it. I liked learning the different things that influenced the relationship that society deems wrong.

The structure of the play impacted the character by showing the Lil’ Bit throughout her life. Lil’ Bit has the most story arch, while I do not see as much change in Uncle Peck. It also alludes to Uncle Peck doing this in the past, showing that he does not seem to learn. I think that the structure really enhanced the audience learning about the characters and their personalities. I appreciated the chorus the entire play and I liked that they were all the same throughout, but just changed roles. I feel it gives more insight to how all people in the situation feel the same towards the situation. All of the people are just grouped together into one character (one male and one female). I think that the thought was good because, as I said above, it jumps from one idea to the next. While the ideas are connected, the timeline is not, necessarily, related. I think this structure helps makes the play a little more relatable because that is how most people’s minds work. It also adds to the character building of Lil’ Bit because we see how her mind also works. The language of the play really enhances the characters by adding pauses and changing the language of Lil’ Bit throughout the age changes. I think that the first scene, the spectacle is unique because Lil’ Bit and Uncle Peck do not actually touch. I feel as though that really helps the audience from cringing at the relationship and feeling bad throughout the entire show. I also feel as though the titles of the sections are included in the spectacle of the title of the play, but also enhances the theme.

## Play Response: *Three Sisters*

*7 November 2017*

While there can be an argument regarding whose story this is, I believe that the story really belongs to the character that an audience member can most identify with. Those who can understand the situations of Olga, Masha, Irina, or Andrey can more see them as the protagonist. They can understand the story as being the story of those characters. Discussing this play with other people, I notice that others see the play through different eyes. While I understand that’s how all plays are, they seem to follow the storyline of different characters. It is easy to make a case for any of these characters to be seen as the protagonist.

While I was reading through the play, I seemed to follow the storyline of Andrey. I know the play is called *Three Sisters,* which makes you believe that the story is about these three women, I enjoyed following the storyline of Andrey. He seems to be in this relationship with Natasha that no one in his family likes. He decides to marry her and they have a baby (which the timeline in this play seems to be ridiculous). He has this idea of the ideal life that he seems to attempt to fit into. He seems to attempt to defy what his sisters believe he should do. Through this he does not particularly live a great life. Andrey should have followed what he wanted to do, but he seems to be most attempting to fit into what society wants him to fit into. Andrey has a large struggle in this play with what he should and has the ability to do. This struggle can be seen from the beginning to the end of the play.

One aspect that I believe is important in seeing Andrey as a protagonist is because I believe audiences may see him in this way as well. Audiences are accustomed to seeing males as the lead roles, so they may want to follow this character throughout the play. Many audience members may have a connection to this character, so seeing the play through his eyes, I feel, is a good way of analyzing what may be viewed.

## Play Response: Anton in Show Business

*14 November 2017*

I really enjoyed this play! I like how the play seems to tell a story about people, as *Three Sisters* does. I personally cannot say who the story is about, because I feel it is about Holly, Casey, and Lisabette. It is a story about how the three grow through this one experience in their life. It can also be seen as a play where there is a lot of talk about something that is going to happen, but that event does not happen. The three main characters, Holly, Casey, and Lisabette, at the end of the play are still in the same position they were at the beginning of the play. However, many of the side characters, Ben and Kate, have changed from the beginning to the end. While this stance may be true, it can be assumed that the characters have learned something from the experience, but the audience is not able to see this growth because, really, and audience does not see beyond the play. The audience, as the play states, can only interpret the director’s interpretation of the script. So, to truly know the answer to many of the questions posed, it is crucial for one to watch the play (at least that’s what the script is saying). However, that is only the play within the play.

This play is very meta. There really are many plays within this play. There is the play of *Three Sisters,* the play where the actors are rehearsing *Three Sisters,* and then there is the play where the characters are playing actors and talking to the audience member, Joby. The play being performed is more than just that story. There are parts of the play that break and the actors play characters that are actors and are talking to Joby, a reviewer. I also believe that at the end of the play within the play, the actors are playing characters that are actors leaving their performance. It is at this point that Lisabette is talking to the audience. Lisabette could either be the character or the actor in the play, talking to the audience. I believe that this can be a story about the actor of Lisabette learning about the theatre from her acting experience with Joby as an audience member. While these interactions are very short, I believe these scenes are crucial to understanding who the play is about.
