There are few times in my life where I have questioned my life choices and wondered why I have become who I am. I have one of these moments after watching the film *Creating Freedom: The Lottery of Birth.* After this film, I started to question what I want to do with my life and, more importantly, why I am pursuing the goals I have created for myself. While I believe this film would be beneficial for everyone to watch, there are specific ideas that arise having watched this film while being enrolled in an evolutionary psychology course. The film *Creating Freedom: The Lottery of Birth* can make viewers consider nature and nurture as it effects evolutionary psychology as well as human’s behavior and cognitive processes, making them question the idea of freedom or free will.

Viewers can view the role of nature in society’s evolution through the idea of human survival. One of the human instincts is survival and in order to survive, there are certain behaviors one must do to accomplish this. Today, one must work to make money and one must learn obedience. To survive in society, one must have a job to make money. Money provides food and shelter, two items mandatory to survival. To get a job, there must be jobs available for someone to choose from. Society controls people’s behavioral and cognitive abilities by the jobs they provide. Students’ education revolves around them graduating with the skills to perform the job they want. The students also learn the behaviors and thought processes of those working in the field they wish to excel. To keep their jobs, people must maintain ideological discipline and carryout the beliefs of their company. Failure to comply may cause the person to lose their job, therefore, their money. For people to obtain and keep a job in the career field of their choice, they must learn obedience. People need to learn the rules, both written, spoken, and unspoken. People need to know what is expected of them in order to keep the career they need to survive. If someone does not obey laws, they are put in jail, where they also lose more of their freedom. This idea is shown through Yale’s experiment in the film. So, the film discusses human’s natural instinct to survive is controlled by society through employment and obedience.

The film also discusses the idea of how nurture also influences human’s behavior and cognitive processes through education. Students are taught obedience, as discussed in the previous paragraph. Students’ education is also very limited by those who create the curriculum taught. Societies control the education provided to their students. Through this control, they are trying to increase pride for their particular heritage. The education is controlling the thoughts of their students by limiting their knowledge and framing it in a way that makes their history look appealing. I could not believe that, before watching this film, I had never heard of the trilateral commission or the US government trying to overthrow 50 other governments before watching this film. That information not being taught in my education makes me wonder how many other ideas I have not learned due to society’s system. The education system is also controlling students’ behavior by teaching the them the ways in which society works and is structured. By teaching the students the structures of society, they students are not taught ways in which the structure can differ. Therefore, the education system is controlling the cognitive abilities of students to continue the society in the way that best fits their interests. Everything is political; there is no neutral. So, with nurture through society’s education system controlling the way students behave and think, there is yet another question of how free one may really be when they grow up a specific way in a specific society.

By the series title, *Creating Freedom,* the film questions whether or not humans have freedom. Through nature and nurture, it is rather clear that free will and freedom is questionable at this point in our society. People have grown really to benefit themselves, which is understandable considering survival is key to human existence. However, these acts of human survival has created a society that mandates others to fit into the outline previously created in order for them to survive as well. People are told they can be whoever they want and do whatever they want, but in reality, their decisions have to fall into the guidelines of society or else they are not going to survive.

Overall, the film *Creating Freedom: The Lottery of Birth* positions viewers to question nature and nurture as pertaining to evolutionary psychology as well as human behavior and cognitive processes, making them question the idea of freedom. Nature shows that survival is crucial in human society and in today’s society one needs to be employed and obedient. Humans are nurtured through education to join a particular society with a particular structure. The biggest take home for me in this video is that people should be aware of their surroundings. They should know where they come from and how they have been taught. They should be taught to question society and its ways, though they may not be able to change anything about it alone. However, there is a feeling of more freedom in their decisions when one is able to understand why society works the way it does.
