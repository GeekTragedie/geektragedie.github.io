## 10 Minutes Play Reflection

<p class="piece-note">Advanced Directing: One Acts · 29 August 2016</p>

For this first journal I am going to quickly recap what I have previously done, say where my theatre focus is in, and then say what I like to direct/what plays draw me in. I have directed a one act play with fifth graders and a number of shorter plays for fortnight. I have also taken a directing class where we finished the semester with a 10 minute scene. I have been an assistant stage manager for the show *Standoff at Highway #37* (the number may be incorrect). I have also been a stage manager for the show *Venus in Fur*. I also work in the box office and have been in two USD theatre productions. My focus in theatre is really improvisation and the amazing work it can do for people. While do care about the audience for improvisational performances, I really have a focus on the work the individual performers do. I believe improvisation really has a healing point to it and anyone can grow any aspect of their life with improvisation. As for plays that I am drawn to, I like plays regarding family hardships or personal, mental hardships. This may explain why I liked and disliked some of the 10 minute plays I read.

### *Sure Thing* by David Ives

This play was about two people meeting each other for the first time and how their encounter *should* go. If one person ever said something that makes the interaction between the two not go well, a bell rings and the person changes what they said so the conversation succeeds.  I liked the fact that the play shows the dos and don’ts of conversations and how someone has to say the right thing in order for interactions to go well. There are so many things that can go wrong and this play shows them. I think it is sad how judgmental people are to write off a person for one thing they say and this play shows how often these things can happen. I really enjoyed the constant change in political alignment and how it showed to never talk about politics with someone you just met. What I do not care for in this play is the constant change in conversation and repeating. I feel as though it did get a little boring to read as I went on because I did not know where the play was going, so I had no reason to continue reading to get to the end.

### *Poof!* by Lynn Nottage

This 10 minute scene was about a woman saying something that caused her husband to turn into ashes, leaving his glasses. She calls her friend and they discuss what to do with what happened. It is revealed to the audience that the two women live in the same building and are both victims of domestic violence. The friend says the woman should be lucky, but the friend has to think about her children before getting out of her relationship. The woman sweeps her husband under the rug, literally, and goes on with her life. I like this play because it brings to mind how women think about domestic violence and how trapped they feel. It also has a bittersweet ending where one woman is free, but the friend is not. It goes to show that while some women in life can get away, there will always be some that cannot.

### *Bedtime* by Mary Gallagher

This scene was about two sisters trying to go to sleep, but one girl cannot sleep because she is thinking about the end of her life and what happens after you are gone. The other sister is trying to talk her out of her crazy ideas about the end of the world and what happens after you die. What I liked about this piece is that it was not afraid to talk about the subject of what happens after you die. This subject seems to not be discussed in society now-a-days because it is religious and people try to not talk about religion in public. It also shows how kids think, which is always fun.

### *Lynette at 3 am* by Jane Anderson

This play is about a woman trying to fall asleep, but cannot and believes she heard a gunshot in the building, so she wakes up her husband. He refuses to do anything because she thinks she was making it up. She wakes up her husband one more time to talk about life, but he again tells her to go to bed and falls asleep. She is then visited by a ghost of a man who was just killed a floor below by his brother because he was sleeping with his brother’s wife. The woman and the man talk about love and what it should consist of and then fall asleep together. This play was funny towards the beginning because of the things the wife tries to discuss with her husband, but then takes a turn when the ghost and her talk about love. The woman does not believe she has a real love because of the way the ghost describes the love he had in life. I like the funny start off to a chilly end. The audience starts off feeling sorry for the husband, but then is sorry for the wife in the end. There were some parts of the play that felt as though they dragged, however.

## How is it Going?

<p class="piece-note">One Act Journal · 4 October 2016</p>

So far I believe things are going very well! We have the whole show blocked and I feel as though, through our collaboration, the actors are understanding the main ideas that should be portrayed in the show. I am very happy about the homework I have been giving the actors! I can tell they all do the homework because they connect the homework to their character and blocking throughout rehearsals. I am really happy that the actors are getting a full understanding of the mental disorder so they can portray it.

I feel as though my show has accomplished a lot in one-ish weeks. Together we figured out where the characters originated and their role in Amanda’s life. Working with the text for character work has been a struggle for everyone. Due to the fact that they are very long monologues, the actors need to make more of a background for themselves. The given circumstances for each character is not easy to find in the text, which has made the character development difficult. However, after we figured out which characters were what part in the traumatic event, the actors found it easier to tie this to their earlier monologues and figure out their own given circumstances (which is what happens with DID). Working with the text has been a blast. The more times we (I) read the script, the more, little things we find to tie in to the show either through character analysis or blocking or foreshadowing.

Our biggest success in rehearsals is a tough one to answer. I guess the biggest success was everyone figuring out how they fit into Amanda’s life. Once we accomplished this, it made blocking and character work easier. Another big success, that was fun for me, was figuring out the blocking at the end of the show. It was fun explaining to the actors how the alternate personalities have finally figured out what has happened at the end and the symbolism of the ending picture. The alternates have realized that they need to work together to help Amanda and this is created by the blocking and gestures at the end of the show. Having my cast fully comprehend where their character was at the beginning and at the end was definitely the biggest success of rehearsals so far.

What we have left to do is show the two new-ish people their blocking and help them get into their new characters. We also need to work on the timing and transitions between characters. I think the timing and transitions are going to be the biggest challenge. While we have caught some of the timing, I think it will be easiest to catch timing when people are more fluent with their lines and are off-book. I am ready for this challenge and my cast will be too (next week)!

## Journal 3

<p class="piece-note">One Act Journal · 31 October 2016</p>

I really enjoyed the process overall. While there were certainly curveballs thrown my way, I am very happy with my performance. The new performers were enjoyable to work with and I feel as though I really learned a lot by working with the actors I ended up having. Throughout the process I really learned how to adapt my way of directing to different actors. Most of my actors like different ways of direction, which I realized as we were working on monologues individually. Though I was happy with the way things turned out, there were some things that I would change after having the audience in the house and after seeing my show multiple times.

One thing I would like to go back and change is where [one actor] is positioned during the gun shot. I would have liked to have her further in the corner so that people behind her could still see her and leave the diagonal open for everyone. I feel as though some of it was blocked. I also looked at some of the movement and realized some of it is too repetitive and I would like to change some of the blocking, especially for [two of the actors]. As for tech, there was a song I would have added to the preshow music that I think also would have went well with my show that I would have liked to include.

I have realized that through my collaborative process, I was able to accomplish a lot more with the actors in a shorter amount of time. The actors were able to help with blocking and this also helped with everyone learning the blocking. I do wish our cast had a little more time to get to know each other. Not only did I figure out that collaboration helps with actors, I also learned how to collaborate with designers. While I do wish I had more collaboration with my costume designer and had been more firm about what I wanted, I did realize we were on a budget and that there were very limited costumes to choose from. After [a fellow director] pointed out Amanda’s costume, I didn’t quite like it in the show the more I thought about it.  I wish it had been what [a designer] had envisioned for me towards the beginning of the process. I guess there was one other thing that I did not care for. It was much too far into the process before I got an office chair and then by the time I got a chair it was yellow… I really wish I didn’t have a yellow chair. I also wish the divan could have been covered more efficiently because the down stage side still had red showing and that bothered me.

I got a lot out of this experience. I learned how to get what I wanted out of casting. I also appreciated callbacks and being able to control those, while they helped relatively little in this process… I was able to realize which actors I wanted to each role. I also learned how to work with designers, which I had never done before besides stage managing. It was a lot of fun to see all the lighting work that [the lighting designer] did, which enhanced the show immensely.

Through watching other directors work, I realized that I seem less specific in what I want. I feel as though the other directors were able to voice concerns regarding things that I don’t think I would have even paid attention to. Whether or not I would have changed anything, I do wish I could have looked at technical things like they did. I really did appreciate [that fellow director] working so hard to make her show fit into a thrust stage! It was fun to watch the things she did to portray different locations in her show.

One of the things I am most proud of are my actors. All of the hard work they put into the show really stood out to me and it was greatly appreciated. I really enjoyed watching them grow throughout the process and create the show they did. [One actor] put ***so*** much outside-of-rehearsal time into her research and it was very evident. All of the insight she put forward not only helped evolve her own character, but the other characters as well, given the circumstance of the play. One moment I was surprised by, maybe just happy by, was after our first show, I came back to help do scene changes and all the actors looked at me and asked how the show was. I said it was so amazing and I was very proud. I said they all do such a great job! I apparently get very giddy when talking about my shows and [that actor] said, “I don’t know what makes me strive to do better more, having an audience, or having [our director] be so proud of us afterwards.” [Another actor] then agreed with her. That made me feel happy about the relationships I built in my cast; however, they then are more wrapped up in me being happy and proud than telling the story to the audience… I still don’t quite know how I feel about that.

If the world were my oyster, I would do another show discussing mental illness. It was very difficult to find a good script that discussed mental health concerns… maybe I would create one with the help of people who can write well! Maybe I’ll create one with the help of devised theater! I don’t really know, but I really did enjoy educating people about mental illness through theatre.

## Next Project

<p class="piece-note">One Act Journal · 31 October 2016</p>

While this may take some digging, because it was hard enough to find a good tragedy written about the subject, I would like to try and find a short 10-20 minute comedy that deals with mental illness. I would then like to try to do a ground plan and blocking the scene. The actors would not, by any means, need to memorize lines. I would just like to block the scene and dive into the text with them. There should not be any outside-of-rehearsal time for the actors, unless I give them quick research homework.

The reason I would like to do blocking and work with actors is so I can get the feeling of timing in a comedy, because timing is one of the most important aspects in a comedy.
