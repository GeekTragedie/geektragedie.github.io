<p class="paper-title">Political Theatre – Theatrical Politics</p>

Ideas in society have control over what media is released. Government has huge control over what content is produced in the society. When the government takes a stance, different sides of the issue are expressed using theatre. This connection between the government and theatre used to be stronger, however. During the French Neoclassic era and the English Restoration the government had total control over the content and form of plays that were popular and on many occasions what plays were permitted. Two plays that were written using styles that were influenced by political popularity are *Tartuffe*, written in France during the neoclassic period, and *The Way of the World*, written in England during the Restoration. Using both content and form, Moliere’s *Tartuffe* and William Congreve’s *The Way of the World* promote their state’s power over theatre respectively by following the expected rules for playwrights during the given time period.

When King Henri IV died, the crown was then handed to Louis XIII, who was nine years old. Louis XIII turned to Cardinal Richelieu for guidance during his reign. Richelieu developed literature and art, creating the first Italian style theater in France. He then helped create the French Academy in 1637. The French Academy was in charge of renewing neoclassical principals and applying them to theatre and plays. It was the French Academy’s duty to make sure playwrights followed the ideas established. The French Academy was directly linked to the monarchy during the French Neoclassic period.

In the play *Tartuffe*, Moliere strategically follows the characteristics of French Neoclassicism established by the French Academy by his use of reality, morality, and universality in the content of his play. During the French Neoclassic period, the French believed that people should follow reality by encouraging the discussion of events that can actually happen in real life; there should not be talk about ghosts or fantasies. *Tartuffe* followed this rule by having a plot that could possibly happen to someone in the middle class. A man can be blinded by his own thoughts leading him to believe his friend is good while his own family knows the truth. Another French Neoclassic idea that a play should have is a good moral standing. In *Tartuffe,* the title character has a plan to scam Orgon’s family out of their house and money but, this is discovered by the king (5.4). Tartuffe is then arrested for his scheme and hypocrisy (5.4). The play uses morality to better help society by showing that God is good because Tartuffe, using a false appreciation for God, is caught by the king and then punished for his evil deeds (3.3, 5.4). *Tartuffe* also uses the idea of universality, a final French Neoclassic idea. The play is easy for all audiences of the time to understand and connect to. People are able to connect with the characters because Moliere uses the common *commedia dell’ arte* stock characters. Orgon can be related to the character Pantalone because he is blinded by his own beliefs. Tartuffe has attributes of Dottore because one of his functions in the play is to be an obstacle for the young lovers, Mariane and Valere. By using stock characters that audiences of the time are aware of, the audience is able to follow who the different characters are and their mindset easier. Overall, *Tartuffe* follows the political ideology that society should focus on reality, morality, and universality.

Not only does *Tartuffe* reinforce these French Neoclassic ideas through content, it also follows the form of a comedy laid out by the French Academy. The play, as all comedies should, has a happy ending because Tartuffe’s scheme is discovered and Orgon’s family is able to live in their house peacefully and Mariane and Valere are able to marry (5.6). Comedies are also supposed to use private affairs from characters in the middle class. This play does so by using characters from the middle class that are dealing with a father’s difficulty of seeing the error of his ways. There is also a very private scene where Orgon’s wife, Elmire, tries to lead on Tartuffe to prove to her husband, who is hiding under the table, that Tartuffe really is an impure man (4.5). This idea of including private affairs into a comedy is a common practice in France during this period along with following the three unities: time, place, and action. These ideas also can apply to *Tartuffe* because the play takes place in the same day. It also takes place in the same location, a drawing room, which is where Moliere sets most of his plays. It also has to do with the same action, the fact that Tartuffe is an evil man and people are trying to convince Orgon of this fact. Through the use of content and form, Moliere follows the French Neoclassic ideas perfectly and *Tartuffe* is an excellent example of the how comedies were written during this time.

While France had their own idea of how theatre should be portrayed, England had their own rules. When Charles II came into power, he was in charge of issuing patents and licenses to acting companies. Patents were given to companies that maintained royal patronage while licenses were issued to those who wished to perform plays. In order to perform plays and be a well-known playwright during the English Restoration, it was imperative to receive a license or patent from the king to do theatre. One man who was given a by Charles II was William Davenant. Davenant then created the theatre Lincoln’s Inn Fields. In order to perform plays and continue to succeed in theatre, playwrights needed to follow certain rules regarding what audiences would like. Whatever was popular to audiences and the monarchy would succeed. In a way, this gave kings the power to control what plays contained. Due to the civil wars that were happening during this time, playwrights were not able to write about religious aspects. This is how the idea of the Comedy of Manners and Comedy of Intrigue were created. The ideas behind them were liked by audiences and the king, so plays needed to contain these ideas in order to do well in the eyes of not only audiences, but also other playwrights.

A play that is known as being the finest example of Restoration comedy is William Congreve’s *The Way of the World.* William Congreve performed his plays in the Lincoln’s Inn Fields, established by the licensed William Davenant. He primarily wrote plays that fell in the category Comedy of Manners. The basic elements of Comedy of Manners are characters from the upper class, contained seduction and arranged marriages. It has the latest fashions and uses stock characters with names that match.

The Comedy of Manners ideas can all be found in Congreve’s *The Way of the World*. The characters are from the upper class, shown by the fact that many of them have money to spend. There is seduction between characters which can be seen though all of the different affairs and love interest throughout the play. Sir Wilfull Witwoud is meant to marry Millamant when she loves Mirabell. Waitwell, disguised as Sir Rowland, attempts to woo over Lady Wishfort. There is an affair between Fainall and Mrs. Marwood. Overall, the play would not happen if there were no love interests. The play included the latest fashions because Mirabell tells Millamant that he does not want her to be a slave of the fashion of the time, giving the impression that other in the play women are (4). The play also has stock characters which names match their characterization. Lady Wishfort wishes for her niece to marry her nephew Sir Wilfull Witwoud and not Mirabell. Sir Wilfull Witwoud is willful that he has wit, while in fact he is actually drunk in the third act and is not clever throughout the play, but he tries. Waitwell waits to woo over Lady Wishfort so he can be with his wife Foible. These are all examples of how the character’s names fit with their personality during the play. By following the ideas set by Comedy of Manners, Congreve’s play was popular to audiences because these things are what audiences wanted at the time. It had become the social norm because that is what was popular with King Charles II.

Overall, *Tartuffe* and *The Way of the World* follow the guidelines that were created by their respective governments for playwrights during the time they were written. While France had more direct control while using the French Academy, England had less direct control because the contents of the plays were determined by what was popular to the king. Moliere’s *Tartuffe* follows the direct rules given by the French Academy through its use of the French Neoclassical ideas and the use of the three unities. Congreve’s *The Way of the World* follows the popularity of the time by following the rules of the Comedy of Manners because it was popular with King Charles II. Whether it is obvious or not, government has an influence over what art is produced in society.
