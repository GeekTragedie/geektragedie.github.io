## Final Reflection Paper

While I have taken an ethics course in my undergraduate, this course helped me understand the ethical requirements surrounding my future work as a counselor licensed by the American Counseling Association (ACA) and the North American Drama Therapy Association (NADTA).  It is important to know what information is available from your licensing association, especially their codes of ethics. At the beginning of my summer quarter, I viewed ethics as scary rules that needed to be memorized and followed or I may lose my license.  After taking this course, I perceive ethics as protectors that help me be mindful of my world view, comfort me, and provide me a playbook for taking action in difficult situations.

My shift in the way I view ethics originated with Handelsman’s (2013) idea about the architecture of positive ethics.  He makes a distinction that following the rules and not getting into licensure trouble is the ethical floor, but his research team looked at the ethical ceiling.  This idea emphasizes the morals we have as individuals and what we want to do.  He shifts the focus from the “don’ts” to the “dos;” it focuses on acculturating to an ethical way of being (Handelsman, 2013).   I appreciated Handelsman’s approach because it creates a positive atmosphere around ethics instead of the fearful way I viewed ethics at the beginning of the quarter.  More people could be interested in ethics if this approach is normalized.

I now find comfort through ethical guidelines because the codes are there to protect counselors and clients, not to punish them.  Many ethical experts can talk about the same ethical situation and have different opinions of a course of action (Reamer, 2013).  Many times, there is not a correct choice to make, so a counselor needs to think of a choice that fits themselves and their client(s).  It is comforting having spoken to counselors doing the work who also believe there is no correct way to approach ethics.  Practicing counselors have consult teams they can work with so ethical decisions are not made alone.  It is comforting to know that no one is alone when making their decision-making process.  I will be able to turn to colleagues and supervisors in my future practice to gain guidance.  It is also comforting to know I will need to continue my education after school and keep up to date with ethical changes.

With a new-found view of ethics, I also view the written codes as playbooks for taking action when faced with an ethical dilemma.  The guidelines were created by practitioners that have faced dilemmas and have researched best practices.  There is comfort in being able to turn to a written code to provide guidance to future dilemmas I will face.  There is plenty of research regarding ethics, and while I am not in that realm of discourse, those who write ethical codes are.  I appreciate the advice of experts in the field.  For example, if I face organizational conflict in my future work at an agency, I know I can turn to the *ACA Code of Ethics* section I.2.d.  to give me guidance of what to do in that situation.  This guidance has been reviewed by multiple counselors who have probably encountered the same issue I would be facing.  To provide the best form of care to my future clients, I want to embrace specialists’ views by following their playbook.

Overall, there are many ethical guidelines counselors should be familiar with, but that does not mean they need to be memorized.  Making a good ethical decision takes time, research, and consultation.  It is comforting to know I will not be alone in making my decisions in the future.  I have become more familiar with my codes of ethics as well as some state laws that need to be followed regarding ethical decision making.  I look forward to continuing my ethical education after finishing school, but for now I will remember to not have sex with my clients.
