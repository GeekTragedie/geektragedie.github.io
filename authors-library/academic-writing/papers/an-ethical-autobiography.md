## An Ethical Autobiography

There are specific values I believe are important in relationships I have with other people: relationship understanding and open-mindedness. In this paper, I describe how I view understanding and open-mindedness in relationships, how these values originated in my life, based on my cultural background, and how I can apply them to professional behavior.

## Understanding

For a healthy relationship, both parties in a relationship should understand and agree to the terms of a relationship. Mutuality is important to knowing how to interact with someone else. No relationship is the same, so no relationship rules are going to be the same. There are cultural norms that take place when you meet a person, and these rules change over the course of the relationship as two people learn more about each other. For example, I may ask a friend from school a concert with me if they have the same taste in music, but I would not ask my boss to go with me. My boss and I do not have a relationship outside of the workplace; I have not seen a sign from her indicating she may want one, and I do not want to initiate that type of relationship. My boss and I have an unspoken mutual understanding that we will not have a relationship outside of work. I want mutual understanding in a relationship to feel comfortable sharing stories or not or asking for help with something or not depending on the relationship.

Mutual understanding cannot be had without trust in a relationship. When mutual understanding is created or is being developed, there starts to be different levels of trust. I need to trust the other person understands me and will follow our spoken or unspoken rules. When a person agrees to do something in the relationship, I need to trust that they will follow through. That also means that I need to only agree to do things that I can follow through on as well. I need to hold myself to the standard that I expect from the other person, showing they can trust me.

### Origin

My belief that understanding is important in a relationship evolved from cultural background and other relationships being modeled for me. I was told and modeled for by my parents how to interact with different people: strangers, teachers, co-workers, family, and friends. I started school after watching my parent’s interactions and followed what they did. In school, we were taught “stranger danger;” do not trust people you do not know. So, I followed this course of action, assuming other people did as well. I did not interact with people I did not know because I assumed they also understood to not interact with me. If someone approached me outside of school/daycare or without my parent’s consent, I thought it was abnormal. The small amount that I was approached by others, I grew to believe this was a cultural norm.

Understanding in relationships became more important to me after having negative relationships due to lack of understanding. Most of my negative interactions happened when I performed a behavior resulting in negative consequences because of the “that’s not how we do things around here” rule. At daycare, I took a toy from someone else, was spanked, and put into time out. I learned that the other child still wanted the toy, so there was no understanding between me and the other child. I also learned that my daycare provider managed the punishments, so I should not argue with the authority figure because they could harm me. I eventually decreased my behaviors that resulted in negative consequences and increased the behaviors that were rewarded or granted me no punishment. Mainly, I learned the normal cultural behaviors for someone with my background living in South Dakota.

### Cultural Influence

My background plays an intricate role influencing my belief in understanding. I grew up in a place of privilege that taught me these lessons to try and succeed in life and continue the legacy of hard-working, respectful family. I am a white heterosexual female that grew up with no disabilities. I was raised to see abnormalities, such as mental illness and developmental disabilities, as things that happen to other people and not me. This belief also included assault of any type due to gender. So, if someone interacted with me in a way that was not normal, then I was automatically suspicious of their mental and physical abilities or intentions with me, which is not a healthy way to live. My view on target populations has changed, having become a target myself. I have realized that what I once labeled abnormal can happen to anyone. My label of abnormal has changed, so I now approach interactions differently as well. I hope to understand what it is the other person is looking for from the interaction. I keep an open mind about the other person’s intentions.

### Professional Behavior

Creating a mutual understanding in a professional environment is important to me. I feel protected and confident in my work when I have a job description, employee handbook, and codes of ethics to support me. These items help create a mutual understanding between employer, employee, and clients, especially in the helping field. I have been employed in jobs where bosses have exploited my work, which made me feel used. I have been an intern where I was exploited for my free labor. I have been an employee who has done someone else’s job because they did not fulfill their part of their job description. None of these professional experiences are or should be acceptable, and it is in these moments that I wish I had stood up for myself to prevent this from happening because there was little mutual understanding. Of course, it is difficult to stand up for yourself against those who are in a position of power over your professional development. In terms of professional behavior, it is important to have understanding of roles to prevent exploitation and harm and protect people with holding less privilege or agency.

## Open-Mindedness

It is important to keep an open mind about other people in a relationship. Open-mindedness in a relationship includes having curiosity and positive regard, not drawing conclusions or judging someone else without having communication first. I appreciate having space to explain a situation or an automatic reaction because I truly believe people behave with good intentions. Keeping an open mind helps people learn to understand others’ points of view and in turn learn to understand their own. I find the best way to keep an open mind is to use the Socratic method of always asking someone why they feel a certain way, behave a certain way, or say a certain thing. Asking a person why can help you understand their point of view and give you empathy or understanding.

### Origin & Cultural Influence

I have formed my perspective on open-mindedness through the target identities of my cultural background, specifically my gender and my mental health. Most of these encounters were negative or hurtful, teaching me to not do those actions to others. I witnessed my mother take on roles in the family due to her gender. After watching my mother struggle with many household duties that were assigned to her for being female, I decided to not take these roles on alone; however, other people, including my mother, do not have the same perspective and have judged me for not taking on the same role.

I have been stigmatized by my family due to a lack of open-mindedness regarding mental health. After my brother passed away, I made a decision about my own care that I knew my family would not understand. When this subject comes up, the reaction I received the first time still matches the reaction I receive today. No one has asked me about my struggle. No one asked why I made the choice I did. No one has asked how it has helped me. I like to include the Socratic method, keep an open mind, and be curious about others’ decisions to prevent feelings of judgement in relationships. I understand how it feels to be judged and not understood, so I want to minimize the possibility in other relationships in my life as well.

### Professional Behavior

Keeping an open mind at a professional level is very important to prevent harm from being done to others, which I have learned through observation and personal experience. I have witnessed others being harmed from lack of professional open-mindedness, and I have also been harmed from a professional not being open minded. I have seen co-workers and classmates discriminated against because of their race, gender, and sexual orientation. The main reason for this discrimination was the other party not understanding or keeping an open mind about others’ lives and ways of living. Others’ discrimination costs people their jobs, self-esteem, self-identity, and livelyhood. Having witnessed the devastation in the lives of people I care about made me realize how important keeping an open mind is.

Living in a rural community and growing up with a low socioeconomic status has also reinforced my view on open-mindedness. When moving to Seattle, I was assigned a new doctor who asked me many questions about my background, which I was unable to answer. Where I grew up, families often relied on low-cost clinics, and it is common practice in South Dakota to not have a primary care physician. The Seattle doctor became irritable with me for not being able to answer her questions, and I felt insulted for the way I was raised. If my doctor had used the Socratic method and asked me why I did not have the information she was looking for, I would have been able to explain my background more and there could have been more mutual understanding from being open minded. From this interaction, I hope to not cause this type of harm to others in my future career.

## Reflection

After reflecting on what I feel is important in relationships, I realize how much I have learned from negative interactions I have had. Noting this, I remember being taught “The Golden Rule” in elementary school: treat others the way you want to be treated. This rule helped me learn from others’ behaviors and feelings and try to incorporate these lessons into my own actions.

It is also interesting to see what I hold as important in words. Many of the values I perceive as important in relationships are unspoken rules. If I did not take time to think about these relationship aspects, I may not notice when my boundaries are being crossed or I am being exploited, which is important in every relationship, but I have a history of in professional relationships.
