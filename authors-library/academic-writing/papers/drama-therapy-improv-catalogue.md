## Warmups

### Sound Round

Game Type: Warmup

Number of Participants: Group

- Everyone forms a circle
- Leader makes any noise and any motion
- The participant to the left of the person who made the sound and motion then copies them and the sound goes around the circle to the left (like the wave at a sports event)
- Once the sound and motion get back to the starting point, the person repeats their original sound and motion
- The person to the left of the start then creates their own sound and motion and steps three through 5 are repeated
- Once the sound gets back to the leader, the game is over

Reference: USD Improv Club

### Wah! / Nuts!

Game Type: Warmup

Number of Participants: Group

*For the purpose of instructions, the word, “Nuts” is used; however, if you are familiar with using “Wah!” feel free to interchange them.*

- Everyone forms a circle
- Leader starts as the person with the nuts
- To receive the “nuts,” you put your palms together and thrust them behind your head and say, “Nuts”
- The people next to the person with the “nuts” then put their palms together and make a swiping motion at the person with the “nuts”
  - Do not hit the person with the “nuts”
  - Person with the nuts keeps their hands behind their head during this time
- The person with the “nuts” then throw the “nuts” to any other player in the circle by making a swiping motion and making eye contact with the other person and saying “nuts”
- Participants then repeat steps 3-5
- Participants must try to keep a steady rhythm
- Participants who mess up are out
- The final two participants are the winners

Reference: C. G.-B., personal communication, Fall 2013

### Don’t Laugh

Game Type: Warmup

Number of Participants: Group

- Everyone forms a circle
- Leader starts in the middle of the circle
- Leader tries whatever they want to make anyone in the circle laugh
  - They cannot touch anyone
- Anyone who smiles or laughs in the circle then switches spots with the leader
- The person in the middle then tries to make anyone in the circle laugh
- Anyone who smiles or laughs in the circle then switches with the person in the middle and this pattern repeats

Reference: Washington Pavilion, personal communication, Fall 2012

### Conference

Game Type: Warmup

Number of Participants: Group / 6 – 9 people

- The participants line up, usually sitting.
- The MC asks questions of the group as if they were all part of a panel at a conference or convention.
- The participants are not allowed to contradict each other, disagree, or bicker.
  - Participants are also encouraged to actively agree and repeat what someone says.
  - The point is to teach agreement and discourage negativity in a scene.

Reference: Upright Citizens Brigade, personal communication, Spring 2016

### I am a Tree

Game Type: Warmup

Number of Participants: Group

- Everyone forms a circle
- One person steps to the middle and says, “I am a tree” and then acts like a tree
- Someone else from the circle claims to be something else that relates to a tree and then acts like said object
  - Example: “I am a leaf.” Then the person hangs on the tree’s branch
- A third person from the circle claims to be something else that relates to the tree and second object and then acts like said object
  - Example: “I am a caterpillar.” Then the person acts like a caterpillar on the leaf
- The original person and the second person leave the middle of the circle, leaving the third person
- The third person reclaims what they are
  - Example: “I am a caterpillar.”
- Repeat from step three

Reference: USD Improv Club

### Emotional Fruit Salad

Game Type: Warmup

Number of Participants: Group

Reference: expertvillage, 2008

- Explain the game
  - I’m going to call out an emotion. When I call that emotion out, find a partner to emote that emotion with. I will then call out another emotion; find a new partner to emote that emotion with. If I go back to an emotion already stated, find the same partner you shared that emotion with.
  - Give an example
- Facilitator asks everyone to walk around the space
- Find someone to be silly with
- Find someone to be angry with
- Find someone to be sad with
- Find someone to be suspicious of

## Focus Warmups

### Name Game

Game Type: Focus Warmup

Number of Participants: Group

- Everyone forms a circle
- Leader says the name of another person and points at them.
- That person points and says the name of another person and this continues
  - No repeating names
  - Leader is last
- Repeat the same pattern of names a few times before moving to the next step.
- Pick a new category (remembering the pattern of the names)
- Using a different pattern, the leader points at another person and says an object from the selected category
- That person points and says a different object from the selected category
  - No repeating the same person
  - Leader is the last
- After practicing this pattern a few times, layer the names on and do both the selected category and names at the same time
- More categories may be added on if desired
- Leader ends the game whenever desired

Reference: C. G.-B., personal communication, Fall 2013

### Ball Bag

Game Type: Focus Warmup

Number of Participants: Group

- Everyone forms a circle
- Leader has a fake ball bag
- Leader takes out a fake ball and tells the group what kind of ball it is
  - Balls can be different colors or patterns
  - It is easier to start with colors and add patterns later
- Leader tosses the ball to any other participant
  - Use eye contact
  - Physically act as though you are throwing a real ball
- When tossing the ball, the person throwing says the type of ball
  - Example: “blue ball” “polka dot ball”
- When catching the ball, participants thank their thrower
  - Example: “Thank you blue ball.” “Thank you polka dot ball.”
- The leader can add as many different balls to the group as desired
- Try to keep all balls alive
- Leader may collect the balls to end the warmup

Reference: USD Improv Club

### Yes, Because…

Game Type: Focus Warmup

Number of Participants: Group

- Start in a circle
- Explain the game
- One person makes a statement regarding how you are feeling so far this quarter
  - The next person says “yes [repeats the person’s statement] because” then states why the person may have said that statement
  - The “because” can either relate to the other person personally or can be completely fantasy
  - Example:
    - A: I am drowning this quarter!
    - B1: Yes because your classes are demanding more from you this quarter.
    - B2: Yes because you were so tired of treading water in the middle of the ocean that you started to sink!
- The next person in the circle creates a new statement. Once everyone has gone, start with someone else so everyone has a chance to do a statement and a because

Reference: Carrane & Allen, 2006, p. 22

## Scene Building Games

### Rotating Scenes

Game Type: Scene building

Number of Participants: 6 people

- Begin by pairing the six participants off. They stand in a triangle, with one group standing towards the front.
- The MC gathers suggestions from the audience, usually one word, for each pair. The pairs repeat their suggestions back to the audience. The pair in front begin a scene.
  - The pairs in back may discuss or may remain silent. Chatter is acceptable, but it should be a goal to remain silent and enjoy their fellow performer’s scenes.
- At the MC’s behest, they call out “rotate” or “reverse.” Upon this call, the three pairs must switch places, either “rotating” clockwise or “reversing” counterclockwise, thus bringing another pair to the front. The scene must stop when this is announced, and the next scene begins upon the next pair’s taking up the center stage.
  - The point is to show clear progression each time a scene is returned to. Time should pass ideally, or a character should evolve. This gives the performers an artificial intermission, smoothing the transition and making this a very good practice game.
  - A skilled MC is the key to allowing what begins as a scene-building exercise flourish into a hilarious performance. Identifying the natural flow of a scene, even a conversation, is a matter that takes practice and a sense of comedic timing.
- The game continues in this matter until the MC ends it.
  - The MC should do their best to allow each scene to progress to a natural stopping point. Often rotating through each scene once at first helps the audience to learn the rules of the game and gives each pair a chance to begin. Of course, it is improv, so feel free to improvise any order you choose.

Reference: USD Improv Club

### 4 Square

Game Type: Scene building

Number of Participants: 4 people

- As the name suggest, this game begins with acquiring four participants and arranging them as the four corners of a square.
- The MC gathers suggestions, usually one word, from the audience. Each participant is granted a word which they repeat back for clarity. The two participants in the front of the square begin a scene based on the word of the person stage-right. This person, traditionally, begins the scene.
- At the MC’s behest, they may call out “rotate.” When this is announced, the performers must rotate counterclockwise, with the beginner of the previous scene moving to the left and allowing someone into the scene in their place. A new scene begins, going off the word of the person stage-right, who begins the scene.
  - The idea is that with each rotation, the scene should progress forward in some way. Either moving forward in time, or to another location.
  - After the first rotation, it is not mandatory that the same person must speak first each time, but it helps in games with beginners.
- This continues for two rotations, or as long as the MC continues it for.
  - A skilled MC is the key to allowing what begins as a scene-building exercise flourish into a hilarious performance. Identifying the natural flow of a scene, even a conversation, is a matter that takes practice and a sense of comedic timing.

Reference: Jumping Jacks Improv, personal communication, Spring 2016

### Pyramid of Scenes

Game Type: Scene building

Number of Participants: 5-6 people

- Two participants begin in the front. The rest stand either off to the side or behind them. The MC gives them a suggestion, and they begin a scene.
- At any point, another of the performers may yell “Freeze”, “Hold,” or “Stop” and the performers must freeze where they are. The performer then injects themselves into the scene and begins a completely new scene based on how the first pair froze. This scene continues until a new performer repeats the process.
- This continues until the final person enters the scene. At such time, the performers must exit the scene in reverse order that they arrived.
  - When there are large amounts of people in the scene, it is important that they work in a coherent fashion, avoiding talking over each other.
  - This must be done in a natural manner that flows with the scene.
- This progresses until the number of performers has been reduced to the original.
  - The MC will count down each time, and will usually announce the final slide.

Reference: USD Improv Club

### Survivor

Game Type: Scene Building

Number of Participants: 5

- The performers begin in a line. They are given a suggestion for a scene.
- They then perform a scene involving all five performers, coming in whenever the scene requires.
  - The object is to heavily involve each performer in the scene, making sure they all have a vital part.
- The MC stops the scene (keeping it short if they want it to be easy, keeping it longer for more difficulty). They then allow the audience to vote by applause on which performer will be removed from the scene.
- The remaining performers must then repeat the scene. The part of the missing performer must be filled by the remaining people.
  - For practices, this teaches attentiveness and encourages performers to share the spotlight. In performances, it's just plain hilarious.
- This continues, repeating the scene after eliminating one performer until only one person is forced to do an entire five person scene by themselves, playing each part and hitting each main point.
  - This is a fantastic game to finish a show with, as the climactic final scene is exhausting to perform and to watch.

Reference: USD Improv Club

### Getting Connected

Game Type: Scene Building

Number of Participants: 4 – 5 people

Reference: Carrane & Allen, 2006, pp. 42-43

- Walk around the room
- Make eye contact with someone and they are your new partner
- Study your partner, look at their whole body
  - How do they feel?
  - How are they expressing that feeling?
- Get closer to your partner
- Tell your partner what emotion you saw in them
- Have one partner decide the relationship
- Have the other partner decide what is happening in the scene
- Ask the clients to have high stakes for what is going on in the scene
- Have clients do their scenes, all at once
- Ask the scenes to come to a close

## Think on your Feet

### Questions Only / Answers Only

Game Type: Think on Your Feet

Number of Participants: Group or 4 people or 2 people

*This game can be played with any number of people. The important thing is that there is a number of people that are ready to step in*.

- Two people begin a scene
  - During this scene, only questions may be asked.
- If someone makes a statement, they are eliminated and rotated out of the scene, with another performer stepping into their place and continuing in the same location.
  - Additionally, a person may be eliminated if they give too many one-word questions, use too many “didn’t you know _____” questions, or otherwise makes no attempt to progress or develop the scene. For example: continuous repetitions of...
    - “What?” “Who?” “How?” “Which?” and on and on
    - “Don’t you know it’s over there?” “Didn’t you know I’m too tall for that?” “Can’t you see that I have a moustache?” and on and on
    - Falling into “the existential hole” (“Why are we here?”, “What’s the meaning of life?”)
    - And anything else that, in general, does not advance the scene
- Continue the scene until each performer has been in the game once or twice

Reference: Atkins, 1994, p. 93

### New Choice

Game Type: Think on your Feet

Number of Participants: 3 – 5 people

- Designate 1-3 people to act as moderators for this game. For performances, one is recommended.
- The participants receive a suggestion and begin the scene.
- Whenever the moderators so choose, they may interrupt the scene with the phrase “change it.” This is a cue for the performers to change what they just did/said and come up with a new option off the top of their head.
  - It is encouraged that the moderator is merciful in their forced rewrites, but by no means is it necessary.
  - Saying “change it” in times when there are many things happening at once, saying “change ____” helps. However, the ambiguity of the simple phrase “change it” lends more power to the performer, who can interpret it as they will. However, it is done is entirely up to the moderator.

Reference: USD Improv Club

### Slide Show

Game Type: Think on your Feet

Number of Participants:  4 – 6 people

- The performers are divided into two types.
  - The person who goes on a trip. This person leads the game. They are responsible for narrating the events that happen, and developing an original character.
- The performers are given a suggestion for a place for the vacationer to go on a trip.
- The MC counts down. While the vacationer looks away, the other performers must get into a position and freeze when the countdown is over.
  - The intention is to create a scene that looks like a picture.
  - Differing levels, interaction with other performers, and dynamic scenes are highly encouraged and nearly crucial to the success of the game.
- The vacationer then describes the “picture” and what is happening in it as if it was a slideshow depicting their trip.
- This repeats for 3-4 slides.

Reference: Atkins, 1994, p. 93

## Character Games

### Party Game

Game Type: Character Game

Number of Participants: 4 – 6 people

- Select one person to be the Host of the party and have them leave the room
  - Host should come up with a party theme and how the room would be set up for the party
  - Host should come up with a general personality for their character
    - Example: Nerd, Cat Lady, Child
- Remaining participants are party guests and the MC and audience should pick out a character for them to portray at the party
  - Real characters from movies, books, TV shows etc.
    - Example: Dory, Harry Potter, Michael Scott
  - Stereotypical characters
    - Gym Teacher, Crazy Cat Lady
- Party guests decide the order of which they will arrive to the party
- Host can reenter the room and start setting up for their party
  - Host should create a clear setting and party type quickly
- Once the setting is clear, party guests may knock to arrive to the party
- The host greets the guests as they come in and may ask them any questions a host asks his guests at a party
- The object is for the host to correctly guess all the guests at their party
  - Guests should answer the questions as their characters
  - Guests should not be too obvious in their responses, but not too vague
  - Host cannot ask guests direct questions regarding their character
    - Example:  What movie are you from?
- When the hosts correctly guesses the character, the character must find a logical reason to exit the party
  - Audiences may applaud when the host correctly guesses the party guest’s character
- The game ends when the host has correctly guessed all guests
  - If the host is struggling, a fellow member may help with questions

Reference: USD Improv Club

### Superheroes

Game Type: Character Game

Number of Participants: 4 – 5 people

- One player is given a superhero name and some problem to solve.
  - The name should not be a normal superhero name. Classics include “Cockblock Boy,” “Flatulence Dude,” or more fantastical names like “Loves to Shove People Down the Stairs Lass.”
- The player begins the scene, establishing it until another performer enters, either prompted or unprompted by the first performer. The first performer then names the entering person with a superhero name, and that person must instantly assume that persona.
- This continues until all four performers are in the scene.
- When all are in the scene, they must work together to solve the problem as a group.

Reference: USD Improv Club

### Park Bench

Game Type: Character Game

Number of Participants: Group

- Set up 3 chairs right next to each other in a line.
- Start with 1 person on the bench in the setting of a park
  - This person then comes up with a persona or character for themselves
    - Ex: Policeman, college student, escaped prisoner, etc.
- People then come up, one at a time, and try to get that person off the bench in a logical way
  - Ex: if the person on the bench is a policeman the person who comes up could be a donut salesman
  - If they cannot get them to leave the bench, another person can come up and try with a different character
- Once they get the person off the bench, whoever removed them gets to stay. They keep the same persona and then you repeat the process of trying to get them off.
- The object of this game is to cycle through as many characters as possible in a short period of time. **It is not a competition to see who can stay on the bench longest.**

Reference: USD Improv Club

### Marriage Counselor

Game Type: Character Game

Number of Participants: 3

- Set up 3 chairs, two in a pair and another adjacent.
- Designate one of the participants as the “Counselor.” The Counselor leaves the room.
- The other two participants assume the role of a married couple that is having issues.
- The married couple is given a problem they are having with their marriage.
  - This problem can range from more mundane, (Disagree on having kids, one hates dogs the other loves them) to the outlandish (hates the way their spouse is always murdering people)
- The Counselor reenters the room and starts the scene.
- Over the course of the scene, the married couple and the Counselor discuss the couple’s problem, all the while helping the Counselor to guess what their problem is.
- Once the Counselor guesses the problem correctly, they must give the couple a suggestion to solve the problem.

Reference: USD Improv Club

## Being Controlled Games

### Props

Game Type: Being Controlled

Number of Participants: 4 people

- The performers are divided into two groups.
  - Group 1, the Prop Masters, are allowed to move and speak freely.
  - Group 2, the Props, function as any object that the Prop Masters refer to in the scene. If one of the Prop Masters refers to a fork, then one of the Props may offer their three-fingered hand as a fork.
- The performers are given a suggestion and begin the scene.
  - The Prop Master function to give the Props opportunities to shine. Bad Prop Masters make bad Props.

**Side Note:** This game can also be played with audience members functioning as Props. This makes for very unpredictable yet often hilarious results. This should only be done with experienced performers who are very comfortable working with strangers.

Reference: USD Improv Club

### Catchphrase

Game Type: Being Controlled

Number of Participants: 3 people

- The MC begins by granting the participants specific roles.
  - One of the improv-ers may speak freely throughout the game.
  - The other two are only allowed to speak with two phrases. The phrases traditionally consist of one statement and one question, but can be any combination of sentence types. The point is to make them applicable to different situations and scenarios, especially for beginners.
    - “That’s easy for you to say” and “How’s that work?” are good, easily utilized sentences.
    - “Someone stole my diary” and “What’s your favorite kind of pie?” are much harder, and would take a lot more skill and work to utilize effectively
- The three receive a suggestion from the audience and begin the scene.
  - The freely speaking performer has the most work to do, they must establish a task or scene that can progress and set up opportunities for the other two to chime in. For this reason, a person with a lot of skill in scene-building should be used for performances in front of audiences.

Reference: Jumping Jacks Improv, personal communication, Spring 2016
