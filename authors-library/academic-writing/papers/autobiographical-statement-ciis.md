<p class="paper-title">Autobiographical Statement</p>

My junior year of high school I went on a mission trip to Ukraine where I organized camp activities and taught Ukrainian students English. I enjoyed working with the students to break down our language barrier as we took on a variety of tasks, such as Camp Olympics and fixing up the school yard. I thoroughly enjoyed discussing how the Ukraine and United States had cultural differences. On my flight home, I reflected on the trip and realized how much I enjoyed helping other people, and from that moment, helping others became my main goal in life. When I introspectively look at myself, I do not see individual characteristics, I see the stories, like my Ukraine experience, that have helped shape who I am. I believe that my personality is made up of my past experiences which have led to my desire of becoming a drama therapist. While theatre has been a crucial part in my life since middle school when I started oral interpretation, my desire to be a drama therapist started when I discovered my ambition to help other people. The groundwork laid by my time in the Ukraine and theatre was built upon through various experiences that enhanced my self-motivation, leadership, and a willingness to help others. These are factors I feel help identify who I am and my future outlook.

After my travel in the Ukraine, I created a drama program for the sixth graders in my school district as my high school project because there was no program similar for this age group. The year-long program started with practicing theatre games, performing readers’ theatres, and recreating nursery rhymes. The program then moved into oral interpretation and finished with the students picking out a one act play for me to direct. The production was open to the public and attendance filled the auditorium. The students enjoyed creating theatre and were proud of the work they did on the production. After watching the students grow throughout the semester, I found I was glad to have started this program for them, and I wanted to continue to offer theatre to those who do not have the opportunity to create theatre.

I followed my theatre ambition into college and became a theatre major. In my first theatre course, I watched an NBC news broadcast highlighting the work of Tim Robbins and The Actors’ Gang in the California prison system. The program led by Robbins taught prisoners to express emotions they have been taught to suppress. The broadcast stated that those who had taken the class and left the prison were never reconvicted. Having enjoyed bringing theatre to other students in my district, The Actors’ Gang intrigued me by bringing theatre to other populations who do not have easy access to drama programs. I have always found the progress of this group inspiring. This news program showed me that theatre can be used to improve the lives of others, which motivated me to combine my aspiration of helping others with my love for theatre by becoming a drama therapist.

After deciding I wanted to become a drama therapist my freshman year of college, I declared a theatre and psychology double major. To specifically learn more about drama therapy, my mentor and I created an independent study where he let me guide the course throughout the semester. I read books and essays regarding drama therapy, and, in the end, I chose to write a research paper about drama therapy with juvenile adolescents. I learned a lot of information regarding drama therapy through this course while also discovering I am a self-motivated individual. I used this trait later to be accepted into multiple conferences and earn a research grant.

My self-motivation sparked when I heard about the DePauw Undergraduate Honors Conference. I researched the conference and decided to submit the paper I wrote for my independent study. I was accepted into the nationally competitive conference and had scholarly conversation with people from across the United States, and it created more interest in research and literature. I was awarded the opportunity to attend this conference two more times, and later, I used one of the papers from DePauw to get into another conference at the University of South Dakota (USD), IdeaFest. While I like presenting my own research to others, I enjoyed learning from other scholars regarding their research and expanding my own knowledge. My self-motivation also pushed me to present at the Kansas Art Therapy Association Conference using my own research study.

My self-motivation allowed me to get into a competitive research grant program and do research on theatre’s effects on incarcerated adolescents, I applied for a research grant to create a theatre program at a local juvenile detention center (JDC). After being awarded this grant, I created an eight-week, summer theatre program which consisted of improvisation, mask creation, ambiguous scene work, playwriting, and performance. I then designed a questionnaire that measured self-esteem, self-consciousness, social anxiety, and self-concept. After finishing the program’s analysis, I had gained statistically significant information showing that participants’ social anxiety decreased by the end of the summer program. While I was pleased to have gained quantitative information through this program, I learned more about myself and the JDC population.  I was moved by one of the adolescents telling me that, with theatre, she learned she could have fun without using drugs and another voiced she learned theatre was a positive outlet for emotions. I am overjoyed in having had the opportunity to start this project because I was able to inspire the youth in this facility to enjoy theatre while giving them a creative outlet to express themselves even though society seemed to have tossed them aside. Through this experience, I realized that I had valuable leadership abilities, which I saw in other aspects of my life.

One instance I applied my leadership skills was when I co-founded the USD Improv Club my sophomore year of college. I researched how to start a student organization and, when creating our constitution, I set goals I wanted the club to achieve: bring comedy to campus, improve improvisation skills, and accept everyone. Having goals ensured that everyone was on the same page and worked towards the same ideas. With my creative abilities, I was able to help others advance their own improvisational skill sets. I also fundraised and organized the contract to bring Upright Citizens Brigade (UCB) from New York to our campus to offer workshops and perform. Our club learned a lot from UCB’s attendance and each of our members grew in their improvisation abilities. The club has massively grown to forty-three members, and performances became a place where other college students could come and relax. I am proud to have used my leadership abilities to create this club for people who have utilized its activities, meetings, and performances.

My leadership skills were also shaped through my experiences as a director. I have learned that a successful director needs to have a strong sense of what they want their production goals to be. They then need to lead the artistic team, designers and performers, down this path. When directing my production of *… and others* at USD, I acknowledged the fact that I knew little regarding lighting and sound, so I fully trusted my designers to convey the effects I wanted in the production, and I was thoroughly impressed with their abilities. This experience taught me that full collaboration and trust when working in a team makes for the best outcome when striving towards any goal. I was blessed with the ability to direct again for the department when a professor approached me and asked me to direct another one act play. Directing *‘dentity Crisis* helped me explore my directing abilities in a comedy show. Having learned new leadership tactics from my previous production experience, I was able to hone my skills through this production and learn how to also make actors a larger part of the collaborative team. This production showed me that working collaboratively with other people towards the same goal is my leadership style.

Being a self-motivated leader has allowed me to work with different populations throughout my college career, especially once I joined the Arts in Health certificate program. Through these classes, I learned the difference between artists in residence and creative arts therapists. I was given the ability to learn about the healing power of the arts with different populations and experience it firsthand with college students, dementia patients, Native American students, and elders. I also learned ethics in healthcare settings and safety concerns when aiding different populations. My experience working with diverse populations reinforced my willingness to help others.

One of my Arts in Health professors forwarded an opportunity for me to work with a residential and rehabilitation organization creating theatre at their facility. The organization assists people with developmental disabilities, and they teamed up with a summer stock theatre to do a performance of *The Wizard of Oz*. I was hired to help actors at the facility learn their parts and assist the directors in teaching choreography. I also organized the actors off stage and helped move them to their entrance positions. I learned a lot from this experience regarding individuals with developmental disabilities. The actors were proud of their roles and tried their hardest to meet expectations. Just the fact that they were given the opportunity to perform made them feel special and put a smile on their faces. Their families were so proud of their performances, which made the actors proud of themselves. I was glad to have been given an opportunity to take part in this production and work with this population.

Another experience which reaffirmed my willingness to help others was when I worked as a consultant at the USD Writing Center and assisted multiple students with their writing abilities. I enjoyed working with people from diverse backgrounds and reading their essays. I was able to work with people from all degree fields on campus which widened my horizons and world views. Due to my experience teaching English in Ukraine, I was asked by my supervisor if I would help English as a second language (ESL) students with their English-speaking skills. I spent four years working with ESL students from Brazil, Japan, Germany, and India, among others. I have learned a lot about various cultures and their views on American society. A graduate student in the English department at the USD noticed my enthusiasm for working with these students and asked if I would like to teach an English and South Dakota culture summer class to Japanese students at a nearby university. This experience helped me to directly acquire knowledge about these students’ culture. I found that I liked to work with people who come from different walks of life than myself, showing me that helping others can also enhance my global awareness.

Another experience that broadened my understanding of those with different backgrounds than my own and allowed me to help others was when I worked at the Family Visitation Center (FVC). My work at FVC has shown me what foster children go through, especially those with mental health problems or behavioral issues. These youths were in a state of flux and attempting to adapt to multiple changes. I realized I had a special place in my heart for children in need, especially those coming from broken homes. I enjoyed transporting the children from their placement homes to spend time with their family members. I also am happy to have given children a safe environment to transfer between parental guardians. It is important to have someone stable for those children, and I liked to be the stability for them, at least once during their week. I felt as though I learned a lot in this experience about family dynamics and am proud to have assisted families in their times of need.

After having worked with various populations, I applied for an internship at Sanford Arts to finish the Arts in Health certificate program. I was accepted and look forward to working in their children’s wing and cancer and rehabilitation centers. I am excited to shadow expressive art therapists and artists in residence at the facility. I also am enthused by the opportunity to use my abilities and create diverse arts projects for the different populations. I believe this internship will be a great experience to help prepare me for graduate school and look forward to seeing how I inspire other people and how they influence me.

While I see myself as a self-motivated leader who has a willingness to help others, each aspect has been created through multiple life experiences. My passion for working with others comes not only from myself, but from the numerous incredible people I have had the honor of working with through the JDC, writing center, and the rehabilitation organization, to name a few. I would like to continue creating experiences as a drama therapist that help shape who I am and how I view the world.
