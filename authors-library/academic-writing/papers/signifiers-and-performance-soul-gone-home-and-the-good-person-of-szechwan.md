<p class="paper-title">Exam 2 Essay Question</p>

Imagine opening up a magazine and seeing a beautiful woman in the papers. The image of the woman evokes the idea of lustfulness. The image of the woman is a sign or signifier while the feeling evoked, lust, is the signification. While the woman herself, were you to find her in real life, is known as the referent. Catherine Belsey discusses the poststructuralism idea behind referent, signs, and signification. She defines that referents are “things in the world,” (51) signifiers are “the spoken, written, drawn or otherwise material indicator that does the signifying \[observers\] learn to interpret” (52) and the signification “is a matter of social convention” (53) or how an observer interprets the signifier. These poststructuralist ideas can be applied to plays to help further analyze subjects given in the text. When applying signifiers to plays, it brings out different aspects of performances in the plays that can contribute, overall, to the audience’s signification of these signifiers. Langston Hughes’ *Soul Gone Home* and Bertolt Brecht’s *The Good Person of Szechwan* are examples of plays that both use signifiers in their plays and these signifiers give messages regarding the idea of performance.

In *Soul Gone Home,* there are many ideas that can be considered as signifiers, two of which being the idea of pennies on the eyes of a person that has passed away and the other idea is having black bodies on stage and the language they use. By the mother using pennies on the eyes of her son, the pennies are the signifiers, but their signification is the idea of a traditional burial. The signification of traditional burial combines with the signifier of using black bodies on stage and the language that they use. While the original writing of this play was initially for black audiences, it is important to view how other audiences would respond to the play as well. By using black bodies on stage as a sign, its signification to the audience is the essence of how the individual audience member views African Americans, traditionally. When the mother speaks, the language she uses is that of which is signified as African American, which further enhances the signification that the audience member is feeling towards the characters on stage. This correlation is a reification of what the audience believes to be true; however, the language and essence is only a social construct, as Belsey states. These signifiers in language and the mothers actions influence how the audience views the mother’s performance.

In *Soul Gone Home,* there are times of which can be considered performances and times that are non-performances. One of the times of performance in the play are the mother performing how she is sad about her son’s death; however, as soon as her son comes back to life, she is upset at his existence (535). This change in action gives the impression that the mother was only performing her grief and it was not real. The mother’s performance enhances the idea of African Americans’ signifier because audiences connect the grieving to how an African American grieves. When the son comes back to life and the mother no longer seems sad, this gives the impression that she is no longer performing, giving the essence of non-performance. Having the two behaviors foils the mother’s ideas for the audience.

Another performance that can be seen in *Soul Gone Home* through the signifier of African Americans on stage is the performance of being black. The mother’s performance of grief is really a performance for the white medical staff that are coming to pick up her dead son (537). The son also performs the role of a dead black son by ensuring that his hair is combed perfectly under his hat and that the pennies are on his eyes. The role the mother takes on as the white medical staff arrive is that of a black, grieving mother. As soon as the medical staff exits, the mother calms down and prepares herself to leave the house. To prepare herself, she lightens her skin so that she could possibly be viewed as more appealing before she exits the house to perform what others view as African American, reifying the idea of the signifier and signification. On a larger scale, looking at signifiers and how they influence performance in the play *Soul Gone Home* show how African Americas are viewed in this play and overall contributes to the audience’s signification of black people.

There is a main signifier in *The Good Person of Szechwan,* which are the gods. When having gods on stage, they are the idea of the gods, which brings out the essence or signification of gods to the audience. Many people associate gods with the idea of good and just, which is the gods’ signification to the audience. When the audience sees the gods, they believe that the gods will make the right decisions. This idea is reinforced by the characters in the play that believe that the gods are just and turn to the gods to solve problems in their society, like what to do with Shui Ta. However, this signification is broken at the end of the play, for the audience, when the gods leave without fixing the problem that Shen Teh experiences.

The aspect of performance is brought out through this play when Shen Teh creates the persona of Shui Ta to help herself cope with her own environment (II). The performance of Shui Ta helps Shen Teh, but Shui Ta is not seen as a good or just person because he is living in society like everyone else. Shen Teh needed to perform as Shui Ta to move forward in life and make a living; however, some of the actions of Shui Ta were not well perceived by people in the play, so Shui Ta is put on trial for not performing as a good person. When the gods find out the Shen Teh is still there, they believe that she will perform the role of a good person, so the gods believe they do not need to step in. Shen Teh attempts to explain that she needs the performance of Shui Ta in order to live in society. The gods leave believing that, because Shen Teh is a good person, she will remain that way (X). So, the gods do not help Shen Teh when she asks for help. Through the non-performance, breaking of the fourth wall, of a player at the end presenting the epilogue, the audience is challenged to question who is right and what the definition of the word ‘good’ really means. The audience can think about the gods failing to do what may be perceived as just. This challenge construes the audience’s signification with the signifier of god. By challenging the audience’s signification with the idea of god in the play, the audience may challenge the idea of God’s just actions in their own life, outside of the theatre.

Overall, *Soul Gone Home* and *The Good Person of Szechwan* both use signifiers to bring out different aspects of the text while also bringing out different ideas of performance and non-performance. *Soul Gone Home* does this through traditional burial techniques and the use of black bodies on stage. The performance of the African American mother and son bring out the mass idea of how to perform black and how that influences audiences’ signification of the African American signifier. *The Good Person of Szechwan* uses the gods as signifiers. By Shen-Teh having to perform as Shui-Ta to get by in life, the audience questions whether or not the gods’ definition of ‘good’ is really just and right. This idea is enhanced by the non-performance of the player at the end challenging the audience’s thinking about the play. Through the art of theatre, performances have the power to look at social constructions and defamiliarize them in order for an audience to see ideas in a new light or challenge the way they are viewing the constructions.
