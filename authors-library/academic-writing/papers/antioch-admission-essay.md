1\.  I went on a mission trip to Ukraine my junior year of high school where I organized camp activities and taught Ukrainian students English. I enjoyed working with the students to break down our language barrier as we took on a variety of tasks, such as Camp Olympics and fixing up the school yard. On my flight home, I reflected on the trip and realized how much I enjoyed helping other people, and from that moment, helping others became my main goal in life. When I introspectively look at myself, I do not see individual characteristics, I see the stories, like my Ukraine experience, that have helped shape who I am. I believe that my personality is made up of my past experiences which have led to my desire of becoming a drama therapist.

Another experience that influenced my need to help others was when I worked at the Family Visitation Center (FVC). This organization transports children from foster placements to visit with their biological family members to ensure family relationships, even if the children are taken away. They also provide a safe environment in which children can transfer from one parent to the next.  My work at FVC started my interest in family dynamics and what can happen to a family if parents do not cooperate. This experience made me interested in Antioch University Seattle because the drama therapy program emphasizes Couple and Family Therapy.

My self-motivated nature moved me to apply to a competitive research grant program and create a theatre program at a local juvenile detention center to research theatre’s effects on incarcerated adolescents. With this grant, I created an eight-week summer theatre program which consisted of improvisation, mask creation, ambiguous scene work, playwriting, and performance. I then designed a questionnaire that measured self-esteem, self-consciousness, social anxiety, and self-concept. At the conclusion of the program and after finishing the program’s analysis, I had gained statistically significant information showing that participants’ social anxiety decreased by the end of the summer program. I am drawn to a master’s degree in Drama Therapy because I would like to increase my credibility to do research, which Antioch’s program requires. I am interested in research and want to see how my theatre background can combine with this interest.

Another passion of mine Antioch can help me implement into my Drama Therapy practices is improvisation. I co-founded the USD Improv Club my sophomore year of college because I firmly believed in improvisation’s abilities to help others. I created the club’s constitution ensuring to set goals I wanted the club to achieve: bring comedy to campus, improve improvisation skills, and accept everyone. Having goals ensured that everyone was on the same page and worked towards the same ideas. With my creative abilities, I was able to help others advance their own improvisational skill sets. I also fundraised and organized a contract to bring Upright Citizens Brigade (UCB) from New York to our campus to offer workshops and perform. Our club learned a lot from UCB’s attendance, growing in their improvisation abilities. The club has grown to forty-three members, and performances became a place where other college students could come and relax. I am proud to have used my leadership abilities to create this club for people who have utilized its activities, meetings, and performances. I am also drawn to Antioch’s drama therapy program because it teaches improvisation and how it can be applied to drama therapy. I am very interested in learning how to take my previous experience and apply it to my future.

2\. There are some areas in which I am concerned with in regard to attending Antioch University Seattle. One area that I feel will challenge me is the new environment. I am from a small town in South Dakota with a population of 5,000 people. Seattle is a large city with about 700,000 people. This change in size is rather daunting; however, I feel as though I have a plan to support myself. Before moving to this large city, I plan to visit the location with family and have those around me to become accustomed to the new surroundings. I will also move to the area with my husband and close friend who will support me. I plan to live there for at least a month before starting school and believe this will allow me to settle into my surroundings and prepare me to start classes.

One problem that I have been told to consider is my ability to pay for my graduate education. I have a plan in place to pay for my education, and I believe that through the support of my family and my work ethic, I will be able to successfully finish my graduate program without becoming distracted by financial stress.

I also foresee graduate level classes as a possible challenge for me. While I am accustomed to my university’s style of teaching undergraduate courses, I have been told by many professors that graduate level courses are more difficult and the programs are more rigorous. To approach this challenge, I plan to use my time management abilities to set aside enough time for classwork and reflection, ensuring I gain as much knowledge as I can in my courses. I also am self-motivated, so I firmly believe I will put forth the effort to learn all that will be expected of me, especially given my interest in the subject matter at hand.

The final challenge I will face in this program is working with people in a clinical setting. I know these situations can be emotionally draining, especially if I work with those who have had similar life experiences as myself. I plan on approaching this challenge though personal counseling and self-care. I have found success in personal counseling when dealing with family problems, which also drives me to believe in therapy. I plan to continue counseling in graduate school to help me cope with the change in location and increase in stress and workload. I also believe that having counseling will help me learn how to cope with my own emotions while I do therapy with others. Through my Arts in Health certificate program, I have learned individual self-care skills that help me remain healthy, mentally, and emotionally. I plan to implement these self-care techniques while attending my graduate program. Through my previous experience, self-knowledge, and support system, I firmly believe that I can overcome any challenges I may face at the graduate school level.

3\. The first time I heard about theatre actively helping others was when I was enrolling in my first theatre course in college. I watched an NBC news broadcast highlighting the work of Tim Robbins and The Actors’ Gang in the California prison system. The program led by Robbins taught prisoners to express emotions they have been taught to suppress. The Actors’ Gang intrigued me by bringing theatre to other populations who do not have easy access to drama programs. I have always found the progress of this group inspiring. I firmly believe that incarcerated individuals should have more rehabilitation services available to them so they can better adapt to society once they are released. The Actors’ Gang started my passion for drama therapy with incarcerated populations, so I continued my interest with this population by starting a theatre program for juvenile adolescents as part of a competitive grant research program.

My self-motivated passion for working with adolescents in juvenile detention centers (JDC) lead me to pursue more work with this population. I believe the voices of this population should be heard and that these youths need more attention and chance for rehabilitation. I learned a lot about myself and the JDC population. I was moved by one of the adolescents who told me that, with theatre, she learned she could have fun without using drugs. Another voiced she learned theatre was a positive outlet for emotions. I am overjoyed to have had the opportunity to start this project because I was able to inspire the youth in this facility to enjoy theatre while giving them a creative outlet to express themselves even though society seemed to have tossed them aside.

When considering diversity and cultural competence, I have taken three courses directly learning about minority populations: Social Psychology of Prejudice, Psychology of Sexual Orientation and Gender Diversity, and Arts in Health: Ethics and Multiculturalism. In these classes, I have studied diverse minority populations and how they are treated by society. I have gained firsthand experience with racial minorities when I worked in the USD Writing Center where I had the privilege to work with English as a Second Language students from multiple countries. I thoroughly enjoyed discussing the differences between cultures and learning about various experiences and celebrations. In my multiculturalism class, we read the book *Understanding White Privilege* by Frances Kendall. I believe this book challenged my views of myself and others. I am passionate about equality and justice for those who have not had the same opportunities those with higher privilege. I am excited by the fact that Antioch University prepares their students to become culturally competent and would appreciate the opportunity to learn more regarding social justice, especially when working with diverse or marginalized populations.

I have had the opportunity to work with families that have experienced being separated by the child services at the Family Visitation Center. My work at FVC has shown me what foster children go through, especially those with mental health problems or behavioral issues. These youths were in a state of flux and attempting to adapt to multiple changes. I realized I had a special place in my heart for children in need, especially those coming from broken homes. I enjoyed transporting the children from their placement homes to spend time with their family members. I also am happy to have given children a safe environment to transfer between parental guardians. It is important to have someone stable for those children, and I liked to be the stability for them, at least once during their week. I have had multiple encounters with various populations and always welcome the experience to work with and learn from others.

I would like to use drama therapy techniques to help adolescents learn about themselves and give them tools to help them in the future, particularly those in foster care, incarcerated, or at-risk. I realized my passion to provide theatre opportunities for adolescents through creating a drama program for the sixth graders in my school district and while on a mission trip to Ukraine. I then pursued more opportunities to work with young adult populations at the Family Visitation Center, local juvenile detention center, and Sanford Arts. Antioch’s program involves Developmental Transformations, and I feel as though this program will help me learn how to help struggling families and adolescents. I am very interested in Antioch’s work with marginalized populations and with the placement options offered at Antioch, I ultimately believe that this school will be a great stepping stone to my future career goals in drama therapy.

I believe the only way to ensure that drama therapy will continue to be used as a therapeutic practice is through research. To show that drama therapy would be a useful therapy method with adolescents, on-going research needs to be conducted to prove its benefits. When attempting to find research in this area, I have noticed that there is a limited amount of information regarding drama therapy with the populations I lean towards. Accumulating quantitative and qualitative data has helped me learn the most beneficial way to help others through theatre. I have enjoyed conducting research to see how theatre can affect others, and I am interested in learning new techniques to add to my repertoire of helping people with different experiences.  With a degree in drama therapy, I would also like to be a researcher to show others the benefits of drama therapy. Antioch offers multiple research and theory classes with drama therapy and I would like to use the information I learn in these courses to further the research base of the drama therapy field.
