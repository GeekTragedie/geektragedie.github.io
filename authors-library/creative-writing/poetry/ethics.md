# Ethics

**Course / Context:** Ethics in Creative Arts Therapy (Pella Schafer Weisman, MA, LMFT, RDT), Antioch University Seattle  
**Date:** July 17, 2019  

---

<p class="content-note"><strong>Content note:</strong> a brief mention of a client's suicidality.</p>

<pre class="poem">
<strong>Self-Care</strong>

Find a way to decrease your stress.
Find a way to leave it behind.
Find a way to enjoy your time.

      Do yoga. Do meditation. Do a diet.
      Stay healthy. Stay positive. Stay smart.
      Don’t worry. Don’t complain. Don’t falter.

      Leave the work behind.
      Focus on the present.
      Always check-in with yourself.

I found my own way.
I create art, diet, treat myself, do yoga, check-in, play games, enjoy life, and focus on the present.
These things work for me.
What works for you?

<strong>My Values</strong>

My values come from my roots.
My roots originate in a small town.

Springs talking on the dock.
Summers swimming in the lake.
Falls raking and jumping in leaves.
Winters sledding down the backyard hill.

Learning the rules of the family given through the Christian lens.
Learning the difference between right and wrong.
Learning the ways to better myself in the world.

I need to be mindful of these lessons that have provided me a particular view of the world.
Growing up in South Dakota only gives a small view of the world.
So, I need to be mindful of others’ views and accept them without judgement.


<strong>Informed Consent</strong>

While growing up, you are influenced immediately by your family.

      You do not know what beliefs are instilled during your growth.
      You do not know what information has been left out of your education.
      You do not sign an informed consent with your family of origin.

Enough elements in life are outside of our control.
It is important to know what is allowed in a relationship.
Written boundaries and an ability to protect yourself is important.

Counseling relationships are different than other relationships.
The counselor can be like family members
      holding power that influences without knowledge.
Clients need to know what their rights are
      to create a healthy relationship with a therapist.

Don’t hoard your power. Empower.


<strong>Assessment &amp; Diagnosis</strong>

You don’t need to know everything.
Just know where to find the information
      – like a hoarded garage of goodies.

That being said…

      Know the theory that fits your core.
      Know how you and your theory view diagnoses.

      Know how you want to assess.
      Know what you want and need to assess for.

            Should I diagnose?
            The label sticks forever…

            Do I need to take action?
            My client is suicidal.

Relax. Lean on your supervision. You’re doing great.


<strong>Advocacy</strong>

While a client can be empowered inside the counseling room,
      The outside world causes oppression.

Watch out for others,
      It’s important to let everyone enjoy what life has to offer.

Therapists have a duty outside of the office to say something.
      Voting. Credentials. Referrals.
      To name a few.

Be mindful of your agency.
Use it to empower others.


<strong>Multiculturalism</strong>

I’m a

      24 year old,                                                woman.

            able-bodied,                                    U.S. born

                  white,                              middle-class,

                        Midwestern,             heterosexual,

             There are so many items that influence my therapeutic relationships!
                    Each of my identities have multiple roots in my childhood backyard.

Many of my tales come from a backyard of privilege…
      Privilege that my clients may not have had.
      A backyard that my clients did not come from.

My tales and my clients’ tales will be different –
      and will always need to be considered.

      And vice versa.


<strong>Code of Ethics</strong>

Rules at the lake are all about keeping everyone safe.
      Don’t run on the dock.        Wear your life jacket.  Eat your vegetables.

The rules at the lake house surpassed my youth and evolved into my future.
      The rules found two categories reflecting the grass and sky.

The ground contains the rules of gravity
      – rules that everyone knows and follows
      – rules that have been written down and placed on the fridge

The sky contains the rules of heaven
      – rules that feel right in your heart
      – rules that you obey even when no one is watching

These rules reflect into my codes of ethics.
      I need to integrate the ACA and NADTA
            To protect myself and my clients

Therefore, I remain conscious of my roots in that backyard
Upholding my ability to remain flexible over time
      I’ll follow my Ethics
