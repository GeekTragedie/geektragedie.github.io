# I Am a Perfectionist

**Course / Context:** Spring 2020 coursework (Drama Therapy Specialization), Antioch University Seattle  
**Date:** Spring 2020  

---

<p class="piece-note">Untitled monologue; the title is taken from its opening line.</p>

My name is GeekTragedie, and I am a perfectionist.

Now… what does perfectionism look like in my life?

- For me, it causes projects to be both the first thing I think of when I wake up and the last thing I think of when I fall asleep
- It turns 1 hour projects into 5 hours or more
- It interferes with my collaboration skills
- It causes trust issues when attempting to delegate tasks as I can’t trust others to do a task “correctly”
- It causes constant worry that I have missed something
- It prevents me from feeling joy during events I’ve planned because I’m worried about the event being “perfect”
- It blinds me from seeing and acknowledging my accomplishments
- It limits my self-compassion
- It depletes my mental capacity to live in the present – instead I live in the future
- It prevents me from seizing the day

So, what did the world tell me that made me believe I needed to be a perfectionist?

- You must work hard to get what you deserve
- Thus, if something bad happens, you probably did something wrong
- In order to receive praise, you have to accomplish something big
    - So even though you got good grades, you still didn’t work hard enough to be granted praise.
- To avoid being yelled at, just do a good job.
- If you aren’t being productive to better your future, your efforts are pointless
- You must have physical proof of your accomplishments
- Non-academic or career accomplishments don’t count for anything.
- If you want something done right, do it yourself.
- You must be *always* working towards something.
- Taking a break is a waste of time.
- Asking for help is a sign of weakness

Based on the messages I heard and believed, what did I actually tell myself?

- I don’t deserve what I have because I haven’t worked hard enough for it.
- Bad things have happened to me because *I have* done something wrong.
- I didn’t receive praise because I haven’t accomplished anything yet.
- My projects were never done well enough to receive praise.
- I got yelled at because my work wasn’t good enough.
- I’m not productive when I do things for fun, so I need to make my hobbies into my career so that my hobbies can matter.
- I don’t have physical proof of how much I have changed or grown, so it hasn’t happened.
- I can’t let my emotions or mental state get in the way of anything because they aren’t physical or visible problems.
- I can’t ask for help because it would look like I don’t know what I’m doing and I must always know what I’m doing.
- I can’t pass on this task that is too daunting because I can’t trust someone else to do it correctly, so I just need to take care of it myself.
- I don’t really know what to do for fun.
- I must be moving forward… great. I finished that list, what’s next?

What do I still tell myself?

- Now it is time to let go of who I’m supposed to be and embrace who I am because I am enough.
