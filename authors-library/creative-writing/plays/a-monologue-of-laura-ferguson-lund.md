# A Monologue of Laura Ferguson Lund

**Course / Context:** Introduction to Drama Therapy (Fred Landers, Ph.D. & Pella Weisman), Antioch University Seattle  
**Date:** November 19, 2018  

---

<p class="content-note"><strong>Content note:</strong> the deaths of children, war, and loss.</p>

<p class="piece-note">A monologue in the voice of my great-great-grandmother. Family names, the business name, and some details have been changed.</p>

**LAURA**
Hello, my name is Laura Lund, formally known as Laura Ferguson and I am GeekTragedie’s great-great-grandmother. GeekTragedie’s mother was named after me. I heard GeekTragedie’s grandma, my granddaughter called me “quite a lady!” I am the first of three daughters of Agnes and John Ferguson who immigrated to the United States from Scotland. I am a very hardworking woman that has lived through a lot of hardship. No matter how hard my life was, I always lived through it, which I have passed down through many generations in my family. Overcoming trauma and remaining strong for others has become quite a trend.

While many women during my time did not work, I was at work constantly. I was an adult during the great depression in the Midwest where I made jam and bread for everyone around the neighborhood and did their ironing for them. I started and ran a greenhouse called Ferguson Greenhouse where I worked every day of the week. I taught GeekTragedie’s grandmother how to do floral arrangements. *(I’m the reason her bouquet during her wedding was so beautiful.)* I was a very patriotic woman! I put signs in my bouquets during World War II that read “Buy War Bonds!”

I guess it’s time to talk about the hardships I went through. My husband died at an early age due to a stroke and I continued on working long after he passed away. I also lost four of my five children in various ways. Edward died of a fever when he was 6 months old. I lost two sons in World War II. Harold was downed in a plane and Frank… Well he was never the same. He came back with wounds no one could see, and in the end the war took him too. The worst of my losses was Dorothy. She left me by choice when she married a Frenchman and moved to France! George always stayed around though, which I am thankful because I was able to have an amazing relationship with my granddaughter and share my Scottish heritage with her by taking her to see Scottish Dancers. While I have lived through many years of hardship, I’m glad I passed down the trait of resilience through my generations; however, it may have caused future generations to bundle up their feelings and not quite express them openly. For that, I’m sorry.
