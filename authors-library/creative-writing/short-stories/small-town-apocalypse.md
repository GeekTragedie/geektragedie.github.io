# Small Town Apocalypse

**Course / Context:** Creative Writing, English 283 (Holly Baker), University of South Dakota  
**Date:** 7 August 2015  

---

<p class="content-note"><strong>Content note:</strong> horror violence, gun use, and deaths.</p>

There is a small town in the Midwest called Centerville. Like almost every small town in the Midwest, it was in the middle of nowhere. To help define the size, the graduating class of 2011 had sixteen people; it would have been seventeen if they hadn’t had one drop out. Centerville consists of one main road running north to south. On the main road you can find the police station, general store, bank, and gas stop; the town is too small for a clinic to stay in business. Branched off of the main road are small neighborhoods and churches. The school is on a road at the far north of town. On the south side of the road, is the one thing that makes the little town unique. This is where you can find the town graveyard surrounding a tall hill and on top of that hill is where the mental hospital is located.

Both the hospital and the town have been around since 1890. The hospital is the reason the town exists to begin with. The state decided that it didn’t want to store their crazy people near their sane people, so they decided to exile them far away into the middle of nowhere. The workers needed a place to live, but didn’t want to travel far to and from work, so they established the small town of Centerville: the center of all care. The family members of the care takers started farming and eventually the town practically became self-sufficient. The only people coming and going were patients or trucks with medical supplies and hygiene products. As time grew on, many of the original founders died and a new wave of staff came in. This happened every thirty years or so, but as vehicles became a modern necessity, the doctors and nurses decided they didn’t want to live so close to their patients. This is understandable given the nature of their work. All of the doctors working at the asylum now live anywhere from twenty to fifty miles away. They drive to work in the mornings, then go straight home after. The town still exists, being populated by farmer families or people who commute their own twenty to fifty miles to work, but enjoy the close small town community.

The mental hospital isn’t the reason the town is well-known across the United States. Actually, Centerville made FOX News and The View back in late spring 2012. Why? Well, it all started when one of the patients decided he was too good for the hospital. The guard and nurse on evening duty had decided that sex in the electrotherapy room was more important than making sure all the doors were securely locked for the evening. The man realized this and opened the door. He walked right out, grabbing garden shears from the janitor’s closet on his way. After cutting through the fence surrounding the facility, he rolled down the hill towards the graveyard.

The manager of the store in town had just closed up shop and had ridden his bike to the town graveyard. He was putting daisies on his grandmother’s grave for her birthday when a loud thud nearly made him scream. He whipped around quickly to see nothing but the darkness of the night. He then heard a low moan slowly grow louder.

“H… Hello?” the manager whispered, hoping for no response. He saw something shift to his right, followed by another moan. He spun to look, jumping back as his heart leaped into his throat. There was man-like figure slumping towards him, one foot dragging and head tilted to the right and slightly forwards. The manager turned to run, but tripped over his grandmother’s gravestone. The figure wrapped its cold, wrinkled fingers around the man’s calf and dug his teeth into his flesh. The manager let out a painful howl, but there was no one to hear his cries in the graveyard in the middle of nowhere.

The manager woke up a few hours later to his phone ringing. He slightly remembered having a weird dream of a zombie attacking him. As he opened his eyes, he found himself still sitting in the graveyard. He couldn’t recall why he was there. He checked his phone seeing that he had five missed calls from his wife and knowing that she would be waiting up to yell at him when he finally arrived home. Next he felt a burning sensation coming from his leg; when he looked down, he realized what he thought had been a crazy zombie dream, had actually been reality.

Startled by this idea, he got up as quickly as his leg allowed and limped over to his bike. His leg prevented him from being able to ride his bike, so he used it as a crutch to make his way home.

The manager lived on the far north side of town on a side street by the school. When he got home, his wife was waiting up for him, worried. She started yelling at him the second he got through the door about being too inconsiderate to call her. Disregarding her verbal blows, he told her to quiet herself or she would wake up their daughter. He then started to pack a bag to leave. He told his wife that he had been infected by a zombie in the graveyard and didn’t want to bring harm to his family or town. His wife told him he was being ridiculous and had just watched too much of “*The Walking Dead*.” He denied it and told her he remembered seeing the creature in the graveyard. It had yellow skin, bloodshot eyes, and a dirt covered face, like it had just come out of the ground. It was moaning with its head tilted sideways, he told her. He described the events that had happened and his wife told him to shower and crawl into bed because she didn’t want to hear his tall tales anymore.

As the manager and his family slept, the zombie from the graveyard had made its way into town.

One of the high school nerd-gamers was walking home from his friend’s house when the zombie from the graveyard startled him on the side walk. After playing endless hours of “Minecraft,” the gamer knew what a zombie sounded like. He crept closer to try and get a better look, because “Minecraft” doesn’t particularly give you a great vision of zombies, but the zombie swung to grab him. Weighed down by the Xbox 360 in his arms, he couldn’t easily make any sudden movements. So, as the gamer turned to run away, the cord on console let loose and wrapped itself around the gamer’s leg. This caused him to fall face first into the cement, crushing his new Xbox on his way down. The zombie grabbed the boy’s leg and pulled him closer to its mouth. The gamer’s face dragged on the cement, but he was soon forgetting about the pain of his face once the zombie dug his teeth into his side. The zombie then left him for dead, if only death were the thing to come.

As the manager slept, he continually had nightmares of the zombie biting him. In the dream, however, his bite mark started turning the color of death and he felt himself turn into a zombie. With a thud, he woke to find himself on the floor, very confused. His body felt cold and as he shivered, he looked down at his leg, his wound had turned purple and his skin looked a shade of grey. These symptoms all paralleled what had happened to him in his dream. With this in mind, he came to the realization that he had turned into a zombie. He was then overcome with the feeling of hunger.

He rose from the ground slowly, watching as his wife switched on the light and screamed, frantically trying to free herself from the grasps of the blankets. She, too, had dreamt of her husband turning into a zombie, so waking to this image terrified her. His cravings drew him to stalk to her side of the bed, grab her arm and bite at her smooth skin. His subconscious caused him to freeze. This was still his wife in front of him, but the hunger quickly overthrew this feeling.

With her husband frozen, the wife freed herself from the blanket, snatched her arm from her zombie-husband’s grasp, and ran at the door. She could already feel her skin growing colder as she ran. Trying to open the door to her daughter’s room, she felt herself turn. Their daughter woke up by her mother opening the door. Her mother didn’t quite look herself and was making a weird moaning noise. Her head was tilted towards one side and she was dragging her foot behind her.

“Mommy, are you okay?” Her father then appeared at the door, making the same noises as her mother. The girl was startled by her mother grabbing her leg. The girl yelled and was able to rescue her small ankle out of her mother’s grasp. She ran towards the door, her small body nimble enough to dodge the hands of her father. She knew exactly what had happened to her parents. They had turned into the same things that were on the show her father liked to watch. She had sneakily watched an episode from the doorway when she was supposed to be in bed. Those things had haunted her dreams for weeks afterwards.

She made her way to the front door, threw it open and ran to the neighbor’s house. The woman who lived next door was the town’s school doctor. The sun was starting to rise at the top of their street as the daughter pounded on the door and begged for the doctor to let her in to safety. As a light turned on in the upper window, the ugly creatures that were once her parents emerged from their house; they had made it to the sidewalk by the time the doctor opened the door. The girl ran inside and cowered in the corner of the foyer.

When asked what was wrong, the girl only pointed a shaky finger at the door and whimpered, “Zombies… Mom… Dad…” She then repeated the moaning noise they had made.

Thinking the girl was being ridiculous, the doctor opened the front door only to see two figures limping their way up her front walkway. It wasn’t quite light enough for her to see what they looked like, but she could see their distorted body shape and hear their soulless moans. When the rising sun hit their eyes she saw they were bloodshot. Seeing the way their figures moved, it did not look human. This was enough, the doctor slammed the door, grabbed the girl’s arm and pulled her to the top of the stairs as the zombie’s arm shot through the glass on the door and let itself in.

The only place the doctor could think of hiding was the bathroom with the door locked. The two sat in the bathtub, the doctor holding a plunger as the girl was armed with a toilet brush. There was pounding on the door and the girl let out a shout in terror. When the door was broken down, the doctor jumped out of the tub and thrust her plunger at the female zombie’s torso. The zombie stumbled back and fell, but took the plunger down with her. The male zombie stepped over her and approached the two humans. The girl jumped up and waved her toilet brush at her previous father, but he knocked the brush away and cornered the two. By then, the female zombie had gotten up and was enraged.

The girl and doctor were dragged into the hallway and were being bit when the doctor’s boyfriend, Randy, ran through the front door. He was the only police officer that lived across the street. He had heard the doctor scream and hurried over to investigate. When Randy met his love’s eyes, she yelled for him to run away and warn the town of the zombies before it was too late.

Randy did just that. He hopped in his police cruiser and drove down all the streets telling the citizens to arm themselves to fight the zombies or evacuate. He was able to make his rounds through the most of the streets, but once he got towards the south side of town, the part of town closest to the graveyard, he ran into another group of zombies led by the high school gamer. Randy drove to his office and hid in the basement, wishing he had actually gone to college before becoming a police officer.

So, at this point there were two groups of zombies, one on the north side and the other on the south side. Since there was only one road leading out of town and there were zombies on both sides of said street, no one could evacuate, as Randy had advised them to do.

Randy’s drive by announcement was how Megan’s dad found out about the zombie attack. Megan was going to be a senior in high school and was looking as though she was going to be the valedictorian of her graduating class. Her family lived in the middle of town and the family only knew of one group of zombies. Megan’s dad came running inside reporting that Randy had warned that there was a zombie attack happening so they needed to arm themselves and hide. Her dad and her each grabbed a golf club while her mom grabbed their old t-ball bat in one hand and her three year old brother, Toby, in the other. They then adventured downstairs to hide. Megan felt her heart beating fast as they huddled into the corner of the basement with a single light hanging in the center. There wasn’t very much stuff in the basement, just a couple of boxes filled with baby clothes and some holiday decorations. Therefore, there weren’t very many good places for them to hide.

As Megan scanned the room, she saw a foot drag by the small basement window. Then she heard a crash come from the window under the staircase. The gamer emerged through the window and started crawling into the basement. He had scratches down his grey face and was covered in dirt. His eyes were yellow and bloodshot. His mouth looked crooked and he now walked with a limp as he dragged his left foot behind him. Megan could see the outline of his veins and each contour of the bones in his body. He was making a noise that could only be described as sounding like The Creeper from Scooby Doo.

“Run upstairs!” her dad ordered, “I’ll hold them off so they don’t follow you!”

When her mom and she got to the stairs, her mom gave her Toby. She told her daughter to run upstairs and escape through the front door while she stayed to fight off the gaming herd. Toby was howling at this point and he squirmed in his sister’s arms as she fumbled her way up the stairs. Reaching the top, she could see her school doctor, hobbling her way towards her. The doctor was blocking Megan’s way to the front door, so she ran the opposite direction, went upstairs into her room, and locked the door. While trying to calm down her brother, she thought of where they could hide. There was the closet, but it didn’t lock, so that didn’t work. They could go under the bed, but Toby wouldn’t be quiet enough to hide and she had no idea if the zombies had a heightened sense of smell for fresh flesh. She gazed out her window, which led to the top of the garage. She had used the window in the past to sneak over to friends’ houses when her parents had grounded her. This was the only way out, so she set Toby on the bed and threw open the window. Grabbing Toby once again, she carefully stepped over the window sill. As soon as she set her other foot safely on the roof of the garage, she heard the bedroom door slam open and saw the doctor make her way towards them. Megan thought it was weird seeing the person that usually helps you feel better trying to hurt you.

On the garage roof, Megan held her brother close and realized she couldn’t shimmy down the drain pipe as she had done in the past; not with her brother in her arms. He was too young to hold on to the pipe himself. All the time Megan spent thinking of an escape plan gave the doctor more time to reach the window and suddenly Megan realized the doctor wasn’t alone. Behind the doctor were Toby and Megan’s zombified parents.

Megan looked down at Toby as he reached out and sobbed, “Mommy! I want Mommy!” She felt her heart drop into her stomach because Toby couldn’t have Mommy; he couldn’t have Daddy. Sissy was the only person he had left to take care of him. Megan was now determined; she had to make sure he stayed safe.

The doctor had one foot out the window and Megan still needed to figure out a way to get her and her brother to safety. She glanced around and realized the tree on the side of the house was close enough to the garage that she could step onto the branches. The other zombies had given up on their house and had moved across the street, so the ground surrounding the tree was clear. She took a deep breath to help calm herself, told Toby to hold on tight as she shifted him onto her right side and stepped onto the first branch. She grabbed the tree trunk and took the branches one step at a time. Toby seemed to enjoy the tree climbing because he let out a little giggle.

Megan got to the closest branch to the ground and jumped, making sure her knees were bent enough to catch their fall. They stuck the landing and Megan took off running; she didn’t know where she was running to. She just ran.

Once she reached the corner where their street intersected the main road, she glanced back towards the street and immediately wished she hadn’t. There were about twenty-five zombies attacking her neighbors and she knew all of those who were attacking. She saw her dad at the front of the group fighting a man who held a shovel. Megan let out a small cry and covered Toby’s eyes as she saw the man swing the shovel into her father’s back. He fell to the ground, blood pooling. His eyes seemed to be back to their normal, deep blue color. The neighbor man ran towards Megan as his wife was fighting a young girl a block farther down. The woman armed with a metal lamp smashed the little girl in the head. The zombie girl stumbled backwards and hit the ground. Her skin still looked rather young and innocent. The mob was made out of all people Megan knew her principal, hair dresser, teller, cashier, her best friend, all vicious and blood thirsty. She decided she couldn’t watch the horrific scene anymore. She needed to stay strong for her brother and seeing that scene unfold would only make matters worse. So, she turned back towards the main street and tried to figure out where to go for safety.

She thought of going to the store, that way they would have food and water. Who knew how long the zombies would be around? Glancing at the store, she realized there was another group of about ten zombies attacking people who had thought of the same she had.

They could go to the school. It’s rather large and there are many lockers, closets and classrooms they could hide in. Plus, there’s a cafeteria where they probably keep an endless supply of food. Then she remembered that they lock the school when it’s not in session because of hooligans that have nothing to do with their time but vandalize school property.

The police station seemed like the safest bet because it had secure walls and maybe Randy had stayed alive and would be able to help them. Megan turned left and ran to the station, but the door was locked. She pounded on the glass, asking Randy to open the door. She saw their mom reach the intersection and glance around. Megan noticed her mother’s skin didn’t have the grey color she had seen before. It seemed normal, like the girl’s. When Megan’s mother turned to look in their direction, Megan could see her eyes were clear too. Their mom looked practically normal except for some scratches and bruises from the fights and her limp and head tilt. Before Megan could examine her any longer, Randy opened the door just quick enough for them to sneak in, then slammed it immediately.

“Are you infected? Is he infected?” Randy asked sternly. Megan told him they were fine, but had lost their parents. He led them to the basement which was smaller than their house’s and more cluttered. It had boxes stacked on top of each other, filled with old police files. Not a whole lot happens in Centerville, so practically all the important files fit into a small basement. Any unimportant paperwork was recycled.

Randy locked the door and made his way down the stairs. Toby calmed down enough in his sister’s lap to fall asleep as Randy told Megan of what he had seen and how devastated he was of witnessing his girlfriend being bit. When he was finished she shared her story and when she reached the part where they jumped from the tree, her brother stirred in her lap and woke.

“Sissy, I’m hungry.” He whined as his deep blue eyes stared into hers.

His eyes looked like their dad’s and this made Megan almost cry, but she held it together and asked Randy if he had any food. Randy said he totally forgot about food because he was in such a state of panic that he just locked himself in the basement. Like such a gentleman, he offered Megan his keys to go get food if she wanted.

“Toby, Sissy will be back in a little bit with food, okay? You are going to stay here with Randy until I get back. He will keep you safe, okay?”

Toby looked at her and nodded. She told Randy to protect Toby with his life, and he gave his word.

“Here, take my spare hand gun too. We don’t need Toby losing his sissy.” Randy gave her a half smile as she took the gun and holster from his hands and adjusted the rather large strap to fit her waist.

She turned towards the stairs, climbed them slowly, and opened the door at the top. The station seemed clear and she didn’t see or hear any zombies nearby. The place was a mess with shattered glass and broken chairs, but it seemed as though the zombies didn’t stay for too long. She closed the door quietly behind her and locked it with the keys Randy had graciously given to her. Approaching the main door, she glanced up and down the streets. They all seemed clear, which unsettled her a little, but she ventured outside anyways.

She carefully walked down the street, not wanting to run in fear thinking that it would draw attention to herself. The store was across the street from where her street intersected the main road, so across the street and three stores down. She glanced behind her every few steps to make sure there was no one there. She reached the store safely and found it was vacant.

As she quickly went down a few aisles and filled up bags with food, she wondered where all of the zombies had gone. Maybe they ventured up to the mental hospital and are parading there. Maybe the community members had won and all the zombies gone and people were at home celebrating. Maybe the zombies hadn’t made it to all the streets and were finally getting towards the end of either side of town. When she had filled two bags full with food, she made her way to the door and checked the streets again. As she peeked out the store window, she could see one lone zombie limping their way towards the police station. Having only her brother’s safety in mind, she dropped the bags of food and grabbed the gun out of the holster.

Being from a small, Midwestern town, everyone is pretty familiar with guns. Although she had never shot a hand gun before, she did go deer and pheasant hunting with her dad every year, so she knew how to shoot. Plus, she also liked crime shows and had seen the cops use hand guns all the time. It couldn’t be very difficult. She stepped out of the store and walked towards her brother’s safe haven. The zombie she saw was actually her mom. Her mom stopped in front of the police station, glanced inside and decided to enter. As she entered the station, Megan started to sprint. Upon reaching the building, she saw her mom banging on the door to the basement, so, gun drawn, she yelled at her mom to draw her attention away from the door. She turned towards Megan and stared at her. Her chocolate eyes were clear and jaw fully intact. She couldn’t see a single bone in her body and her skin looked as though if you were to grab it, it would rubber band back to its original state. She looked nothing like the zombies she had seen earlier. She looked… normal.

This made Megan think back to the gamer, the doctor, the little girl and her own father. Was what she saw really real or did she simply imagine what they looked like because that is what she was told they were supposed to look like? Maybe there weren’t actually zombies, but people only think they are because others tell them they are.

She decided to test the theory. Keeping the gun raised, just in case she really was a zombie, she said “Mom? Can you hear me? It’s me, Megan. Your daughter?”

Her response was a low moan as she rolled her head to the other side and snarled. That approach didn’t work.

“It’s Megan. You used to tuck me into bed at night and sing ‘Sweet Home Alabama?’” Her eyes only squinted that time. It seemed as though she was listening, so she tried again, “You would take me to the park and push me on the swings ‘til I touched the stars!”

Her head started to straighten a little more and she let out a grunt that time instead of a moan. The grunt seemed to be more of a knowing, “Huh.”

Megan lowered the gun and took a small step towards her so she could see her mother’s face more clearly. “I would beg you to let me stay up so we could watch ‘Friends’ on TV until late at night.”

Her eyes shifted this time as she fully straightened.

“You have a wonderful son, Toby that has deep blue eyes like his dad and curly blonde hair like you.”

Suddenly, she seemed to be back to being Mom. She looked at Megan confused, unable to figure out what had happened. She had no recollection of the incident or what she had done. All she remembered was her husband coming home late and that she was upset with him for not calling to check in.

Megan brought her confused mother down to the basement where she gave Randy back his gun and explained to her mother what had happened. Toby sat on his mother’s lap, cuddled himself into her chest and took another nap. After explaining to mom and Randy her theory, the three came up with a plan to save the town.

Toby and Randy stayed in the basement to remain safe as Megan and her mother ventured upstairs again and made a beeline straight to the school. They wanted to lure as many “zombies” to the school yard as they possibly could, so as they ran. They hooped and hollered and made as much noise as humanly possible. Once they arrived at the school, the mom unlocked the gate using Randy’s keys. The school had a fence around the entire area to help prevent vandals, but would soon be used as a corral for “zombies.” The two entered the school yard, ran to the building, and unlocked the door. Once inside they made sure to lock the door behind them, then made their way to the secretary’s office. Grabbing the microphone connected to the school speaker system, the mom pressed the button and spoke to the audience outside.

Megan went to the front door to see if the people were listening and motioned for her mom to continue talking.

“Attention, you are all citizens of Centerville. You are not zombies and you do not want to attack each other. You are regular, caring citizens that love each other. You mean harm to no one.” Megan saw her mom glance in her direction for assurance.

There were limping people still coming down the main street and pouring into the school yard. The overbearing volume of the loudspeaker seemed to be working and the people seemed to start understanding what Megan’s mom was saying. She motioned her to continue.

“Back in January we all participated in the city-wide snowman building contest and got together for a huge snow ball fight! Last month we all got together in the park and had a city-wide picnic to raise money for a new playground for the school! A few weeks ago we celebrated the founding of our city and crowned Mr. and Mrs. Care Center! We all love each other and get along, so you need to snap out of this and start caring again!”

Megan was fascinated while she watched her neighbors come to life again. Slowly, people started to straighten up, move their head around confused and listen intently. Everyone seemed to go back to the way they were before. Megan saw a caring look return into the eyes of her school doctor, Stella. The store’s manager, Mike, scratched his belly as he glanced around, confused. Sheryl, the mother of the little girl Megan had seen get hit with a lamp a few hours earlier, held her head in her hands. Megan exited the school and told everyone they could go home and clean up, but that everyone would need to clean up the streets. She glanced around for her father, but he was nowhere in sight and neither was the little girl.

Everyone ended up coming together as a town and helping people put their houses back to normal. It was a project that would originally take a month, but with hard work and dedication, they were finished in two weeks.

As for those who were killed in the horrific incident, Megan and Toby’s father being one of the victims, the town had a huge ceremonial burial for them. The funeral was one of the saddest days in the history of Centerville, but it was a beautiful ceremony. Each churches’ pastor spoke a few words, but no one got long winded and everyone was given twenty-three roses, one for every deceased individual.

No one was charged for murder; which, if they had charged people, the whole town might be in jail right now. All of the people killing were not in the right state of mind to murder anyone, so the deaths were written off as accidental.

As for the escaped patient, the nurse and security guard who let him get away were fired. The man was the only one in the school yard that didn’t turn and no one was able to identify him. Randy had locked him in the cell for safe keeping and the doctors the next day came looking for him. As it turned out, the reason he was in the mental hospital is because he thinks he’s a zombie.

The surrounding towns found out about the tragedy and that’s how news spread until it reached the Big Apple. After that, the whole world knew about the “Small Town Apocalypse.” Multiple journalists and scientists visited and studied what had happened. The scientists wrote the event off as a mass hysteria, where all the people thought they had a disease, but really their mind was fooling them. No one had ever seen a case like this, but weren’t surprised that it was possible because of society’s obsession with zombies.

Even though the town had been through a lot, most of the people stayed living there. The publicity brought in more people to the town and it has actually grown. The graduating class rose to a total of forty-two people!

Megan’s brother started school the year after she graduated. Megan decided to stay home instead of going off to college and help her mother around the house and with Toby. She joined Randy on the police force; since the town grew, it needed more people to protect the population.

No matter what happens, this town will always be someone’s home. Well, why not? Centerville is the center of care!
