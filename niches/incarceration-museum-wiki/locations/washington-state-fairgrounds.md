# Washington State Fairgrounds (Camp Harmony)

**Era / Category:** III. State Confinement & Memorialization  
**Type:** Temporary Wartime Assembly & Internment Center  
**Location:** Puyallup, WA, USA  
**Current Status:** Active Fairgrounds with Historical Memorial  

---

### Overview & Historical Background

*   **Forced Confinement of Japanese Americans (1942):** Following Executive Order 9066, the Puyallup Fairgrounds were requisitioned by the U.S. government and designated as "Camp Harmony," a temporary assembly center used for the forced confinement of over 7,000 Japanese Americans from Washington and Alaska prior to their transfer to inland incarceration camps like Minidoka.
*   **Racial Panic & Profiling:** The mass removal and detention of U.S. citizens and residents of Japanese descent was driven by broad racial profiling, wartime hysteria, and systemic racism rather than individualized evidence.
*   **Indigenous Naming & Ongoing Cultural Context:** The city of Puyallup takes its name from the Puyallup Tribe (*S'Puyalupubsh*), underscoring the layered history of Indigenous land and racialized exclusion in the region.
