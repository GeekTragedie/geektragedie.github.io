# Cárcel de la Corona

**Era / Category:** I. Pre-Modern  
**Type:** Ecclesiastical & Political Prison  
**Location:** Calle de la Cabeza, Lavapiés, Madrid, Spain  
**Current Status:** Elder Living Facility (Subterranean cells preserved)  

---

### Overview & Historical Background

*   **Historical Context:** Historically utilized for religious and political monk confinement, specifically housing clerics and monks who had broken ecclesiastical rules or their vows of silence.
*   **Current Building Function:** Above ground at the corner of Calle de la Cabeza and Calle de Lavapiés, the structure currently operates as an elder living facility. However, the historic subterranean cells remain intact beneath the building and can be accessed via an open stairwell in the hallway directly to the left of the front entrance.

### Architecture & Facility Directory

#### Subterranean Stairwell & Corridor
*   **Access & Conditions:** The descent features steep, well-worn stone steps that become slippery even when dry, accompanied by a noticeable temperature drop as the walls and air grow cooler underground.
*   **Masonry:** Constructed with vaulted brick arches and rough stone masonry. Modern lighting has been installed along the floor and walls to illuminate the underground passages.

![Underground brick arches in Cárcel de la Corona](images/carcel-de-la-corona/20230424_185922.jpg)

#### The Monks' Cells
*   **Cell Layout:** The individual cells are small and no longer have their original doors.
*   **Inter-Cell Peepholes:** Rather than exterior windows, the thick brick walls feature small arched openings between adjacent cells, allowing confined individuals to see into neighboring cells.
*   **Street-Level Water Shafts (Torture Mechanism):** High in the brickwork are small shafts leading up toward the street level. Rather than providing light or ventilation, these openings functioned as a torture mechanism, channeling water from the street down into the cells and forcing prisoners to stand, sit, and exist in cold water.
